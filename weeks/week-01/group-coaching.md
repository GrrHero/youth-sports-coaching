# Week 1 · Part 2: Volunteer Coaching at the Baseball Fields

[Week 1 overview](README.md)

Coaching a group is different from coaching your own two. Here's what works with groups of 8–15 kids ages 4–8.

## 1. Keep attention: the group control basics
- **Have a "freeze" signal and practice it first.** For example, "When I say 'Baseball,' you say 'Freeze!' and put your hands on your head." Practice it like a game in the first 2 minutes. It works far better than yelling.
- **Talk to them for 30 seconds or less.** Demo + one cue + "Go!" If you're talking longer than that, you've lost the 5-year-olds.
- **Kids face away from distractions.** When you talk, put yourself in front of the sun or the parking lot so they face away from it (and away from other teams). Have them sit down or "take a knee" so they're not wandering.
- **Get on their level.** Kneel down when you talk to them. It changes everything.
- **Names, names, names.** Learn every name on day 1 (name tags or tape on helmets help). "Nice step, Sofia!" lands much better than "Nice step, kiddo."
- **No lines longer than 3.** Long lines are where trouble happens: kids pick dandelions, wrestle and tune out. If a line is longer than 3, split it into two stations.

## 2. Stations: the secret weapon
With 2–4 helpers (other parents), break into small groups and rotate every 5–7 minutes.

**Sample 4-station rotation (30 min):**
| Station | Activity | Helper's one cue |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Throwing | Throw at a target (bucket, fence) | "Point, step, throw" |
| 2. Catching | Soft toss, "catch ladder" | "Eyes on it" |
| 3. Hitting | Tee hitting into a net/fence (*safety line!*) | "Squish the bug" |
| 4. Running | Run the bases, races, "Beat the Ball" | "Cheek to cheek" |

**Station tips:**
- **Give each helper a card** with the station's one game, one cue and one safety rule. Most parent helpers have never coached, so a card makes them confident.
- **Rotate on a whistle,** always in the same direction (clockwise).
- **Hitting station = tightest safety rules.** Only the batter inside the "bat circle" (chalk or cones), everyone else behind the line, and the helper watches the batter only.

## 3. Managing mixed skill levels
- **Same activity, different difficulty (the "choose your challenge" approach):** Set targets at 3 distances or sizes: "Easy, medium, or hard?" Kids pick their own challenge. Strong kids get pushed, and new kids aren't embarrassed.
- **Adjust the ball:** a foam ball for kids who are nervous, a real ball for kids who are ready. Nobody needs to know why.
- **Pair kids carefully:** sometimes pair similar levels (so both succeed), and sometimes have a confident kid "help" a newer one (in short doses).
- **Have a challenge for the advanced kids** so they don't get bored: "Can you catch it with your eyes closed until I say 'open'?" "Can you hit it to the cone?"
- **Move the struggling kid closer, quietly.** Say "Let's try from right here," and don't say "since you're having trouble."

## 4. Handling frustration and tears
**For the upset or crying kid:**
1. **Get low and calm.** Kneel down and use a soft voice. "Hey, that was frustrating, huh?"
2. **Name the feeling.** "You're upset because you missed it. That's okay. Big leaguers miss a lot too."
3. **Give a little space or a job.** "Want to take a water break, then come back and help me with the cones?"
4. **Make it easier for the next try.** Move them closer, use a softer ball, give them a "win" fast.
5. **Praise the comeback.** "You came back and kept trying. That's what tough players do."

**Don't** say "Stop crying," "It's no big deal," or "Be a big boy/girl." It dismisses their feelings and usually makes it last longer.

**Common triggers and prevention:**
- **Getting out or losing:** In games, avoid elimination (in tag, kids get "unfrozen," never "out").
- **Getting hit by the ball:** Use soft balls early. If a kid gets hit, stop, check, comfort, and let them choose when to go back.
- **Waiting too long:** Short lines and stations fix most of it.
- **Tired, hungry or hot:** Water breaks every 15 min, and keep practices short.
- **Parents watching:** Some kids freeze when a parent is calling out tips. A friendly pre-season note helps: "Please let the coaches coach. Cheer for effort!"

## 5. Positive reinforcement that works
- **Catch them doing something right,** loudly, and as specifically as you can: "Wow, Jayden, you watched the ball all the way into your glove!"
- **Aim for 4–5 positives for every correction.** If you're correcting a lot, the drill's too hard. Make it easier.
- **Praise effort and choices, not talent.** Say "You hustled to first base!" rather than "You're a natural."
- **Team traditions:** a team cheer at the end, a "Hustle Award" (a sticker, or carrying the team bucket) for effort each practice, rotating so every kid earns it over a few weeks.
- **End with a highlight:** in the closing huddle, name one thing *each kid* did well (or a few kids each practice, so everyone gets it every 2–3 practices).

## 6. Age-appropriate language
**Use pictures, not mechanics talk.**

| Instead of saying... | Say... |
|---|---|
| "Rotate your hips" | "Squish the bug" / "Belly button to the pitcher" |
| "Shoulder to target" | "Look over your glove shoulder" |
| "Track the ball" | "Eyes on the ball" / "What color was it?" |
| "Extension through the throw" | "Throw it like you're throwing it over a fence" |
| "Soft hands" | "Catch it like an egg" |
| "Get in an athletic position" | "Ready like a goalie" / "Hands on your knees, bounce bounce" |
| "Don't swing at that" | "Wait for your pitch" (7–8yo) |
| "Lead foot" | "Step on the sock" (put a sock there!) |
| "Stop chasing the ball" (soccer) | "Small touches, ball on a leash" |

**Language habits:**
- Say what TO do, not what NOT to do. "Eyes on the ball" works better than "Don't look away," because kids hear the last words.
- Ask questions: "What worked?" "Show me how you did that!"
- Keep instructions to one or two at a time, and very short.
- Use their names and celebrate small wins out loud.

## 7. A sample 45-min group practice (tee ball / coach pitch)
1. **Arrival game (5 min):** Tag variations or "Ball Hunt" (scatter balls, kids collect them).
2. **Huddle (1 min):** Practice the freeze signal and name today's focus: "Today we're working on our throwing step!"
3. **Warm-up (5 min):** Animal walks, follow-the-leader running.
4. **Stations (24 min):** 4 stations × 6 min.
5. **Water break (2 min).**
6. **Team game (6 min):** Simple base-running race ("everyone runs to first, then second...") or a scrimmage.
7. **Closing huddle (2 min):** Praise a few kids by name, team cheer, high fives.
