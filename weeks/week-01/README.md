## Week 1 at a Glance

**Theme: "Body control and first touches."**
This week isn't about making them great at anything. It's about three things: (1) they love coming outside with you, (2) they learn how a "Dad practice" works (game → learn one thing → game), and (3) they get a first, simple version of each key skill.

**Progression goals by the end of the week (signs it's working, not a test):**

| Skill | 7-year-old | 5-year-old |
|---|---|---|
| Balance | Stands on one foot 10 sec each side | 5 sec each side |
| Running | "Cheek to cheek" arms (hand swings from cheek of face to cheek of bum) most of the time | Arms swinging opposite to legs (no "T-rex arms") |
| Throwing | Side-on, steps toward the target, throws overhand ~30 ft | Points glove at target, steps, throws overhand ~15 ft |
| Catching | Catches soft tosses with the glove, fingers up above the waist | Catches a big/soft ball with two hands, "eyes on it" |
| Soccer | Dribbles with small touches, stops it with the sole of the foot on command | Keeps the ball close while walking/jogging, stops on "red light" |
| Flag football | Pulls a flag from a jogging target, carries the ball "high and tight" | Pulls a flag from a walking target |

**What you need:** soft balls (tennis balls, a foam ball, a beach ball), gloves, a tee + light bat, a size 3 soccer ball (fine for both ages), a small football (junior/"pee-wee" size), 2 flag belts (or socks/bandanas tucked in a waistband), 6–10 cones (shoes, water bottles and paper plates all work).

## Part 1: How to Teach (Read This First)

You already know how to play. Teaching is a different skill, and it's mostly a few habits. Here's the playbook you'll use in every session.

### The 6 rules that make young kids learn

1. **Play → Practice → Play (whole-part-whole).** Start with a game that uses the skill, pull out one small piece to work on, then go straight back to the game.
   *Why it works:* kids learn movement by doing the whole thing. Pulling out one piece briefly gives focus, and going back to the game is where it sticks. Starting with a game also earns their buy-in before you "teach."

2. **One cue at a time.** A 5-year-old can hold about one instruction at a time and a 7-year-old maybe two. Choose one cue word per activity and repeat it. Leave everything else alone, even if it's wrong.
   *Why it works:* young brains get overloaded fast. One focused fix beats five half-fixes.

3. **Show, don't tell (the silent demo).** Show it 2–3 times *without talking*, then once more with the cue word: "Watch my feet... [demo]... *step*!" Face the way they'll face (stand beside them, not across from them) so they don't have to mirror you.
   *Why it works:* kids copy what they see far better than they follow what they hear.

4. **Ask, don't always tell (guided discovery).** "What happened when you stepped toward me?" "Which way did the ball go when you used your toe?" Let them try two ways and tell you which worked better.
   *Why it works:* when they find the answer themselves, they remember it and they feel smart.

5. **Praise effort and the specific thing.** Say "I loved how you stepped toward me!" instead of "Good job." Say "You kept trying even after that drop. That's what athletes do." Aim for at least 4 positive comments for every correction.
   *Why it works:* praising effort builds kids who keep trying (a "growth mindset"). Specific praise tells them exactly what to repeat.

6. **Short and switching.** Each activity takes 4–7 minutes. Stop *while it's still fun*, before it falls apart. Attention spans run roughly 2–3 minutes per year of age, so about 10–15 min for the 5yo and 15–20 for the 7yo even when they're into it, and much less when they're tired.
   *Why it works:* ending on a high keeps them asking for "Dad practice" tomorrow.

### The Coaching Loop (use it for every skill)
**Game → Notice → Show → Cue → Try → Praise → Game**
1. Let them play the game.
2. Notice the *one* most important thing to fix.
3. Show it silently.
4. Say the cue word.
5. They try it 5–10 times.
6. Praise the specific improvement, or the effort if it's not there yet.
7. Back to the game. Say the cue during play as a reminder: "*Step!*"

### How to correct without deflating them
- **Sandwich it:** "Great hustle! → Let's try pointing your glove this time. → I knew you'd get it!"
- **Fix one thing, then let it go.** If the throw is better but the foot is still wrong, celebrate the throw.
- **Use "try" language:** "Let's try..." and "Can you show me...?" instead of "No, that's wrong."
- **Turn it into a challenge:** "Bet you can't keep it close enough to stop it on 'red light'!"

### Running two ages at once
- **Same game, different rules.** The 5yo stands closer, gets a bigger ball and gets more tries. The 7yo gets a smaller target or a time limit.
- **Compete against yourself, not your brother.** Track personal bests ("Last time you got 6 — can you get 7?") rather than head-to-head races, which the 5yo will usually lose.
- **Give the 7yo a "coach" job sometimes.** "Can you show your brother how you step?" It builds confidence and checks that he understands. Keep it short and supervised so it doesn't turn into bossing.
- **When they do go head-to-head, handicap it** (the 7yo hops, or starts farther back) so it's actually close.

## Session Template (20–30 min)

| Block | Time | What |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up game | 4–5 min | Movement game (tag, animal walks), no instruction |
| Skill 1 (Loop) | 6–8 min | Game → one cue → game |
| Water / silly break | 1–2 min | Let them talk; ask what was fun |
| Skill 2 (Loop) | 6–8 min | Game → one cue → game |
| Finale game | 4–5 min | Biggest fun of the day, uses that day's skills |
| Huddle | 1 min | "What was your favorite part? What did you get better at?" High fives. |

**Tip:** if the 5yo melts down or wanders off, skip straight to the finale game. A short session that ends happy beats a long one that ends in tears.

## Next Week Progression (Week 2 Preview)

Keep what works, add one layer per skill. **Only move a boy forward when he's doing the Week 1 cue about 70% of the time.** Otherwise, repeat with a new game (same skill, new fun).

| Skill | Week 2 add-on (7yo) | Week 2 add-on (5yo) |
|---|---|---|
| Running | Starts: "fall and go" lean start, and races with a turn | Cheek to cheek arms |
| Throwing | Hip turn ("belly button to the target" at the finish); add distance | Step + point consistently; throw to Dad (not just targets) |
| Catching | Glove side vs. backhand; moving to the ball; "alligator" two-hand cover | Switch from the big ball to a tennis ball, two hands |
| Hitting | Full swing breakdown: load, stride, squish; soft front toss | Tee contact; "squish the bug" |
| Soccer | Inside-of-foot passing ("ankle locked, hit the middle"); both feet dribbling | Dribble around cones; first kicks at a goal |
| Flag football | Simple routes (go, stop, in/out); QB grip | Catching a soft football; carrying "high and tight" |
| Athleticism | Skipping, hopping on one foot, side shuffles | Skipping, jumping and landing "quiet like a ninja" |

**Also in Week 2:** Start letting each boy choose one game per session (ownership = buy-in), and try one "teach-back" a session where the 7yo shows the skill to his brother.

## Pocket Cheat Sheet (screenshot this)

**The Loop:** Game → Notice → Show (silent) → Cue → Try → Praise → Game
**Rules:** One cue at a time · Talk 30 sec or less · 4+ positives per correction · Stop while it's fun · Praise effort and the specific thing

**Cue words this week:**
- Running: "Cheek to cheek"
- Balance: "Be a statue" / "Tummy tight"
- Throwing: "Point, step, throw"
- Catching: "Eyes on it" / "Fingers up" / "Alligator"
- Hitting: "Squish the bug"
- Dribbling: "Small touches" / "Foot on top"
- Flag pulling: "Eyes on the belt" / "Chop, chop, GRAB"
- Ball carry: "Hide it!"

**When it's going badly:** Make it easier → make it a game → or end with the finale game. A happy 15 minutes beats a frustrating 30.
