# Week 3 · Session 4: Athleticism: Jump, Land, Bound, React (25 min)

[← Session 3](session-3.md) · [Week 3 overview](README.md) · [Session 5 →](session-5.md)

**Equipment:** A jump rope or garden hose (for a line on the ground), 4 cones, 2 tennis balls, a broom and 2 chairs (for a crawl tunnel), a low step (curb or porch step, around 6 inches). Flat soft grass is a must for jumping.

## Warm-up: Puppet Skips (5 min) · Cue: **Puppet**
- **Setup:** Two cones 15 to 20 steps apart. For the 7yo, a "reach target" hanging up: a ribbon on a tree branch or your hand held up high halfway along.
- **Steps:**
  1. Skip between the cones (step–hop, step–hop).
  2. On each hop, drive the knee up and swing the **opposite arm up high**, as if a string pulls the knee and hand up together like a puppet.
  3. Go for *height*, not speed.
  4. 4 to 6 trips.
  5. *7yo:* on the middle skips, reach up to touch the ribbon or your hand.
- **Silent demo:** Do one normal low skip, then a big bouncy puppet skip: pretend to grab an invisible string above your head and "pull" your knee and opposite hand up. Exaggerate the height.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Can't skip yet and gallops instead (very normal at 5; skipping often clicks around 5 to 6). **Fix:** Let him gallop. Then say "Step–hop, step–hop" slowly while holding his hand and skipping with him. No pressure.
  - *5yo:* Same arm and same leg go up together. **Fix:** Don't correct it much yet. Focus on bouncing. The arm-leg pattern usually sorts itself out with practice.
  - *7yo:* Skips fast and low. **Fix:** "Skip so high you could see over a car." Add the ribbon to reach for. A target fixes it.
  - *7yo:* Arms flop at his sides. **Fix:** "The string pulls your hand to the sky." Make the reach target slightly higher than he can comfortably touch.
- **Make it fun:** You're the puppet master pulling invisible strings. When you "drop" the strings, they flop down like sleeping puppets, then pop back up for the next trip.
- **Why it works:** Skipping for height builds elasticity, rhythm and opposite-arm-leg coordination, which are the building blocks of sprinting and jumping. The reach target gives an **external focus**. For the 5yo, letting him gallop until skipping comes is **developmentally appropriate**. Forcing a skill before he's ready just frustrates him.

## Skill Work A: Whisper Landings (6 min) · Cue: **Whisper**
- **Setup:** The rope or hose as a line on flat grass. For the 5yo, the low step.
- **Steps:**
  1. Feet hip-width apart. Small dip, swing the arms and jump forward over the line.
  2. Land **as quietly as possible**, "a whisper, not a shout": on the balls of the feet rolling to the whole foot, knees bent and pointing over the toes, hips back, chest up, arms forward.
  3. **Freeze 3 seconds.**
  4. 2 rounds of 5 jumps with a rest between. Keep it low volume; quality over quantity.
  5. *5yo:* step or jump off the low step and land in a whisper freeze. Then jump over the line on the grass.
  6. *7yo:* forward jumps for distance with a stick, then a "quarter turn" jump (jump and turn to face the side) and stick.
- **Silent demo:** Do one loud, stiff-legged "THUD" landing and make a pained face holding your knees. Then do a silent, soft one and freeze, with a finger to your lips ("shh"). Repeat the quiet one twice.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Lands stiff-legged and bounces or tips over. **Fix:** "Land like a cat sneaking up on a mouse." Go back to stepping off the low step until his landings are quiet.
  - *5yo:* Can't hold the freeze and keeps hopping. **Fix:** Count the 3 seconds out loud together. Accept 1 or 2 seconds at first.
  - *7yo:* **Knees cave inward** on the landing (knock-kneed). This is the most important one to fix. **Fix:** "Knees point at your shoelaces," or put a small ball or stuffed toy between his knees that should *not* get squished. Shorter jumps until the knees stay out.
  - *7yo:* Lands on his heels. **Fix:** "Whisper means no heel sound." Have him jump onto a towel and try not to make it slide.
- **Make it fun:** You close your eyes and judge the loudness. Silent landing = 3 points, small sound = 1, THUD = 0. Each boy tries to "fool" your ears.
- **Why it works:** Learning to land *before* adding more jumping is good long-term athletic development and reduces knee strain later. The "quiet" target is an **external focus** that naturally creates soft, bent-knee landings without a list of joint instructions. The eyes-closed scoring makes it a game they can't fake.
- 🎥 **Video:** [Jumping and landing for kids](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=60N3vdH6NgI) · *Verified title: "Elementary physical education and assessment- jumping and landing" (Dr. Jordan Weber).* A PE-teacher view of what good jumping and landing look like at elementary age.

## Skill Work B: Skater Bounds (5 min) · Cue: **Skater**
- **Setup:** The same rope or hose line.
- **Steps:**
  1. **7yo:** Stand on one foot beside the line. Bound sideways over it and land on the *other* foot, knee bent, hips back, arms swinging across like a speed skater. Freeze 2 seconds. Bound back. 2 rounds of 6 (3 each leg).
  2. Progression for the 7yo: 3 bounds in a row with a stick on the last one.
  3. **5yo:** Two-foot side jumps over the line with a whisper freeze. If that's easy, try one foot to one foot with your hand for balance.
- **Silent demo:** Crouch slightly, swing your arms like a speed skater, then bound sideways and stick on one foot, holding still with arms out. Wobble a bit on purpose, then steady and smile.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Loses balance and puts the other foot down. **Fix:** Totally fine. He's 5. Hold his hand on the landing side, or let him do two-foot jumps.
  - *5yo:* Jumps forward instead of sideways. **Fix:** Have him face you while you stand at the end of the line. He jumps "past" you sideways and keeps looking at you.
  - *7yo:* Landing knee caves in. **Fix:** Same as Whisper: "Knee over your laces." Shorter bounds.
  - *7yo:* Lands upright and stiff. **Fix:** "Land like you're about to sit on a stool." Hold your hand out as the "stool" at hip height.
- **Make it fun:** They're Olympic speed skaters. Commentate like the Winter Olympics. Each clean stick = a "gold medal skate."
- **Why it works:** Sideways single-leg bounds build the balance and push-off that fielding, cutting and dodging all rely on. Differences between kids are expected. **Scaling the task** (two feet for the 5yo, one foot for the 7yo) keeps both of them working at their edge without failing.

## Game: Pounce (6 min) · Cue: **Pounce**
- **Setup:** You stand holding a tennis ball in each hand, arms out at shoulder height. The kid stands in ready position 3 to 4 steps away (5yo) or 5 to 6 steps away (7yo).
- **Steps:**
  1. You drop one ball (no warning).
  2. He **pounces**: reacts, runs in and catches it **after one bounce**.
  3. Caught = 1 point. Two bounces = 0, try again.
  4. 5 catches in a row = he steps back one step for the next round.
  5. *5yo:* only one ball (no choice), and you give a little nod before dropping. *7yo:* two balls, and you drop either. Later, he starts facing sideways.
- **Silent demo:** Have the 7yo hold the balls. You get in ready position, stare at his hands, then explode forward and catch one after a bounce. Freeze in a "pounce" pose like a cat.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Starts flat-footed and late. **Fix:** "Popcorn feet first." Remind him of Session 1's ready position. Move him closer so he succeeds.
  - *5yo:* Tries to catch it in the air before it bounces. **Fix:** Fine, but "Let it bounce once, then catch." Use a bouncier ball so the bounce is higher and easier.
  - *7yo:* Guesses and runs before the drop. **Fix:** That's a "false start" (no point). Hold the balls for different lengths of time so he can't guess the rhythm.
  - *7yo:* Gets there but stands up to catch, so it bounces past. **Fix:** "Stay low when you pounce, hands near the grass."
- **Make it fun:** He's a cheetah and the balls are rabbits. Keep a "cheetah record" chart on the fridge. The step-back challenge makes every round feel like leveling up.
- **Why it works:** This trains **reaction time, first-step quickness and hand-eye tracking**, the same things fielding a grounder needs. The adjustable distance keeps the success rate high but not too high (around 60 to 80% success is a good learning zone). The unpredictable drop forces real reading of the cue rather than guessing.

## Cool-down: Sloth Crawl Tunnel (3 min) · Cue: **Sloth**
- **Setup:** A broom resting across 2 chairs (about 2 feet high) as a "tunnel." A few couch cushions can extend it.
- **Steps:**
  1. Crawl through the tunnel *in slow motion* on hands and knees, opposite hand and knee moving together (right hand with left knee, left hand with right knee).
  2. 3 to 4 slow trips.
  3. Then lie on your back and hug your knees into your chest for 3 slow breaths, and lie in a long stretch for 3 more.
  4. Question: "Which jump felt the quietest today?"
- **Silent demo:** Crawl through slowly with a sleepy sloth face, deliberately moving opposite hand and knee. Point to your moving hand and opposite knee.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Crawls fast, same-side hand and knee (ok at first). **Fix:** "Sloth speed." Go slowly beside him and tap the hand and knee that should move together.
  - *7yo:* Makes it a race. **Fix:** Reverse it: "Slowest sloth wins, but you can't stop."
- **Make it fun:** They're sloths crawling along a branch, and the floor is "hot lava jungle." Make slow sloth noises.
- **Why it works:** Opposite-arm-opposite-leg crawling builds coordination between both sides of the body (the same pattern as running). Doing it slowly brings the heart rate down while still moving, which is an easy cool-down kids don't fight.
