# Week 3 · Session 5: Mixed Backyard Day: Fly Balls, Run-Throughs & the Triple Crown (28 min)

[← Session 4](session-4.md) · [Week 3 overview](README.md)

**Equipment:** A balloon, a beach ball (or large playground ball), soft tennis balls, gloves, a base (or a towel or cardboard), a streamer or toilet paper strip, a laundry basket, 4 to 6 cones.

## Warm-up: Orbit Ball Wraps (4 min) · Cue: **Orbit**
- **Setup:** Each boy holds a ball. 5yo: a big playground or soccer ball. 7yo: a tennis ball.
- **Steps:**
  1. Pass the ball around your waist hand to hand, "orbiting" like a moon, 5 times each direction.
  2. Around the knees, 5 times. Around the head, 5 times.
  3. *7yo:* figure-8 through and around the legs, then toss it up, clap once, catch. Then try two claps.
  4. *5yo:* waist and knees only, then toss to himself and catch with two hands.
- **Silent demo:** Slowly orbit the ball around your waist with your head following it like a planet watcher, then around your head with a "whoa" face. End with a toss, clap, catch.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Drops it on the hand switch behind his back. **Fix:** Use a bigger ball and slow it down. Bring his hands closer together behind his back by bending forward slightly.
  - *5yo:* Gets frustrated with drops. **Fix:** Count catches, not drops. "Dropping means you're learning; pick it up and keep orbiting."
  - *7yo:* Rushes and the figure-8 falls apart. **Fix:** "Slow orbit first, then speed." Count 5 clean before he can go fast.
  - *7yo:* Tosses too high on the clap catch. **Fix:** "Toss to just above your head." Let him pick the height. Lower is easier.
- **Make it fun:** They're planets and the ball is their moon. "Moon speed" changes: slow, medium, rocket. Then a "two-clap challenge" record.
- **Why it works:** Ball-handling around the body builds hand coordination and body awareness (knowing where your hands are without looking). Moving from a big ball to a small ball is a simple **progression from easy to hard** that matches each boy's level.

## Skill Work A: Umbrella Fly Balls (8 min) · Cue: **Umbrella**
- **Setup:** Open grass. Balloon, beach ball, soft tennis balls. Gloves for the tennis ball stage only.
- **Steps:**
  1. **Stage 1 (Balloon):** Toss a balloon high. They move to get **under** it and catch it with both hands **above their eyes and slightly in front of the forehead**.
  2. **Stage 2 (Beach ball):** Toss it high. Same: get under it, hands up like an umbrella, catch above the forehead.
  3. **Stage 3 (Tennis ball):** 7yo with glove, 5yo bare hands with a soft ball. Underhand toss up higher, starting right in front of him, then 2 to 3 steps away so he has to move.
  4. Call "**Mine!**" before every catch (a habit for team play).
  5. Glove up, throwing hand next to it to cover the ball after the catch.
- **Silent demo:** Toss a balloon to yourself, shuffle under it while pointing at it, then hold your hands up open above your head like an umbrella and catch it with a little umbrella "pop" gesture. Mouth "Mine!" Repeat with the beach ball.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Holds his hands at belly level and the ball hits his face or chest. **Fix:** Stay on the balloon or beach ball longer. Start each rep with his hands *already up* as an umbrella. This builds the picture before speed comes in.
  - *5yo:* Turns his head away from the ball. **Fix:** Use only soft balls (foam or balloons). Never a hard ball overhead at this age. Toss it lower.
  - *7yo:* Runs with his glove up the whole way, which is slow. **Fix:** "Run with your arms, then open the umbrella when you get there."
  - *7yo:* Catches below his chest, basket style, too often. **Fix:** "Catch it where your umbrella shades your eyes." Basket catches are fine low; high balls need the umbrella.
  - *7yo:* Misjudges and the ball sails over him. **Fix:** "First step back, then come in." Being slightly too deep is easier to fix than being too shallow.
- **Make it fun:** "Rainstorm!" You toss several balloons or soft balls one after another and they try to keep "dry" by catching every drop. Count catches.
- **Why it works:** Balloons and beach balls slow the ball down so the brain can learn to track and get under it. That's **errorless learning**: getting the pattern right before adding the speed and fear of a smaller ball. The umbrella picture tells them both *where* to be (under it) and *where* to catch (above the eyes). Calling "Mine!" teaches communication without a separate lesson.
- 🎥 **Video:** [Teaching kids fly balls](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_QC-pBhoZWg) · *Verified title: "How to Teach Kids How to Catch Fly Balls" (Coach Dan Blewett).* A coach's guide for parents on teaching young kids fly balls.

## Skill Work B: Tape Run-Throughs (5 min) · Cue: **Tape**
- **Setup:** A home spot and a "first base" 30 to 40 feet away (5yo) or 45 to 55 feet away (7yo). Hold or tie a streamer or toilet paper strip as "finish tape" **4 big steps past first base**.
- **Steps:**
  1. Start in a batting stance at home. Swing a pretend bat, drop it gently (no throwing bats!), and sprint.
  2. Step on the **front edge of the base** without slowing down.
  3. Keep sprinting through the finish **tape** past the base.
  4. After the tape, slow down, glance right (to see if the ball got away) and walk back in foul territory.
  5. 4 to 6 reps.
  6. *7yo progression (rounding first):* when you yell "Gap!" during his run, he curves his path a little to the right before the base (a banana shape), touches the inside corner of the bag and heads toward second for 3 steps, then looks to you.
- **Silent demo:** Sprint from home, step on the front edge of the base, and burst through the tape with arms up like winning a race. Then walk back and point at the tape: *that's* the finish line, not the base.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Stops right on the base, or jumps onto it. **Fix:** Move the tape a bit farther and make breaking the tape the goal. Cheer the tape, not the base.
  - *5yo:* Runs toward the pitcher or wanders. **Fix:** Lay a line of cones down the baseline and say "Run the cone road."
  - *7yo:* Slows down a few steps before the base. **Fix:** Time him with a phone from home to the tape. "Slowing down adds time." Kids love beating a time.
  - *7yo:* On rounding, takes a wide loop. **Fix:** Put a cone where the banana curve should start (about two-thirds of the way down the line). "Bend at the cone."
- **Make it fun:** Olympic finish-line race. Commentate: "...and he BREAKS THE TAPE!" Time the 7yo and chart his best time. The 5yo races the tape, which you can hold and "pull away" playfully.
- **Why it works:** Kids slow down at a base because the base looks like the finish. Moving the finish line past the base (**a constraint on the environment**) fixes it in a few reps without explanation. It also builds on Week 1's "run to first."
- 🎥 **Video:** [Running through first](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b0xQ3p4L6sU) · *Verified title: "Little League Tee Ball Baserunning Drill. Helping tee ball players to run through first base." (Schupak Sports).* It's a tee-ball drill aimed at exactly this skill.

## Game: The Backyard Triple Crown (8 min) · Cue: **Next**
- **Setup:** A circuit:
  1. **Grounder station:** You roll a grounder. Kid Chomps it.
  2. **Throw station:** From where he fields it, he throws (Scoot) into the laundry basket.
  3. **Run station:** He sprints from home through first base and the Tape.
  4. **Fly ball station:** You toss a soft fly ball (Umbrella) as he comes back; he catches it.
  - Scoring: 1 point for each station done well (4 points max per trip).
- **Steps:**
  1. Brothers take turns. Each trip takes about 30 to 40 seconds.
  2. They're a **team** against "Dad's Number." Set a target total (e.g., 20 points in 6 trips combined).
  3. "Next!" is the call to move to the next station without stopping.
  4. 5yo: shorter distances, bigger ball, beach ball on the fly station. 7yo: full distances, tennis ball.
- **Silent demo:** Walk the whole circuit once in slow motion, doing each move in a small pantomime (chomp, scoot throw, run, umbrella) and pointing to the next station each time. Finish with arms raised.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Forgets the order and wanders. **Fix:** Number the stations with cones (1, 2, 3, 4) or put a toy at each one. Walk with him the first time.
  - *5yo:* Gets upset if he misses a station. **Fix:** It's team scoring, so his brother's points help. Celebrate his effort out loud. Points are only one part.
  - *7yo:* Rushes and gets sloppy (no chomp, wild throws). **Fix:** "Rushed points don't count." Only clean reps score. Speed doesn't matter.
  - *7yo:* Bosses his brother. **Fix:** Give him the official "scorekeeper" job for his brother's turn: he can *only* say good things.
- **Make it fun:** Give it a big "championship" feel: announce each player by name, make a crown out of paper for the end. If they beat Dad's Number, they get to pick next week's warm-up game.
- **Why it works:** This is **whole practice**: the skills from the whole week chained together in a game-like order, which is how they show up in real baseball. Team scoring against a target number avoids a 7-vs-5 contest the younger one will always lose, which keeps the motivation **cooperative**. Mixing different skills in one game (**variable practice**) feels harder now but makes learning stick better later.

## Cool-down: Highlight Replay (3 min) · Cue: **Replay**
- **Setup:** Lie on the grass with eyes closed.
- **Steps:**
  1. "Picture your best play of the week in slow motion, like a TV replay."
  2. 3 slow breaths while they "watch" it.
  3. Each one says his play out loud and one cue word that went with it ("I Chomped a grounder!").
  4. Finish with a high five and one sentence of specific praise from you ("You stayed low on every grounder today").
- **Silent demo:** Lie down, close your eyes, then mime a slow-motion catch with your arms while smiling, then open your eyes and give a thumbs up.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Can't think of one. **Fix:** Offer two choices: "Was it your umbrella catch or breaking the tape?"
  - *7yo:* Picks only something he failed at. **Fix:** "Tell me one great one first, then one you want to fix next week."
- **Make it fun:** Do it like an announcer: "And here's the replay... he's under it... CAUGHT IT!"
- **Why it works:** Picturing the movement (**mental rehearsal**) and recalling the cue word strengthens memory of the skill. Ending on a success they chose themselves builds confidence and makes them want to come back.
