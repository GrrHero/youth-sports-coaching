# Week 3 · Session 2: Soccer: First Touch, Shots & Shielding (26 min)

[← Session 1](session-1.md) · [Week 3 overview](README.md) · [Session 3 →](session-3.md)

**Equipment:** 2 soccer balls (size 3), 6 to 8 cones, a small goal (pop-up goal, two buckets, or two shoes), a "treasure" square marked with 4 cones (about 4 x 4 big steps).

## Warm-up: Piano Toe Taps (4 min) · Cue: **Piano**
- **Setup:** Each boy has a ball in front of him.
- **Steps:**
  1. Lightly tap the top of the ball with the bottom of one foot, then the other, back and forth.
  2. Light, quick taps, "like playing piano keys," without moving the ball.
  3. Rounds of 20 seconds, 4 rounds with short rests.
  4. *7yo:* taps while slowly circling the ball, then 10 taps and go forward.
- **Silent demo:** Do it slow, light and bouncy, tapping the ball like a piano key, with fingers pretending to play piano in the air. Speed up for 3 seconds, then smile and point at their balls.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Stomps the ball and it shoots away. **Fix:** "Quiet piano." He should barely touch it. If balance is the problem, let him hold your hand.
  - *5yo:* Uses only his good foot. **Fix:** Count only the alternating taps: "Left–right is one point."
  - *7yo:* Leans back and stands flat-footed. **Fix:** "Stay on your tiptoes, light as a cat." Tap your own toes with a finger.
  - *7yo:* Gets sloppy going fast. **Fix:** Count only "clean" taps where the ball stays still.
- **Make it fun:** Count taps out loud. Beat-your-record each round. Play music and tap to the beat.
- **Why it works:** Lots of quick, light touches build foot-to-ball feel (soccer coaches call it "ball mastery"). The record-beating game gives each boy his own goal, so the 5yo isn't racing his brother.

## Skill Work A: Pillow First Touch (6 min) · Cue: **Pillow**
- **Setup:** You 4 to 5 steps (5yo) or 6 to 8 steps (7yo) in front of the kid. Behind him, two "gates" made of 2 cones each, one to his left and one to his right, about 2 steps away.
- **Steps:**
  1. You pass (roll) the ball to him.
  2. He meets it with the **inside of his foot**, ankle firm, foot just off the ground.
  3. As the ball arrives, he gives his foot back a little, "like catching an egg on a pillow," so the ball stops or slows close to him.
  4. *5yo goal:* stop the ball within one step of his body.
  5. *7yo goal:* you call "Left!" or "Right!" while the ball is rolling, and his first touch pushes it **through that gate**.
  6. 10 to 15 reps per boy, then switch feet.
- **Silent demo:** Have a boy roll the ball to you. Show a stiff "wall" foot first: the ball bounces away and you make a sad face. Then a soft pillow foot: the ball dies at your feet and you make a happy face. Both versions, twice. The contrast teaches it.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Kicks at the ball as it comes, so it flies back. **Fix:** Use slower rolls. "Make it sleep" means the ball should stop and stay. Let him stop it with the sole of his foot at first. That's a legit first touch too.
  - *5yo:* Waits flat-footed and the ball goes under his foot. **Fix:** "Meet it." Step toward the ball as it comes, and walk forward with him a few times.
  - *7yo:* Stops it dead under his feet every time, so it's stuck. **Fix:** That's why we have gates. "First touch goes somewhere." Praise touches that go into space.
  - *7yo:* Watches only the ball and misses your call. **Fix:** Hold up a hand signal (left or right arm) instead of calling. Now he has to glance up, which is a real game skill.
- **Make it fun:** The ball is a "wild egg." If it bounces more than 2 steps away, it "cracked." Count unbroken eggs. For the 7yo, a gate touch scores 2.
- **Why it works:** Showing the wrong way and the right way side by side (**contrast**) is one of the fastest ways to show kids what "soft" feels like. The gates are a **constraint**: they force a directional first touch without you explaining it. The 7yo's late call adds a quick decision, so he's learning to think while he moves.
- 🎥 **Video:** [First touch into space](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cvJjbP80cR0) · *Verified title: "Passing Drill for Youth Players: First Touch into Space" (Showtime Soccer Training).* It shows taking the first touch away from pressure, a good model for the 7yo version.

## Skill Work B: Shoelace Shots (6 min) · Cue: **Shoelaces**
- **Setup:** A goal (pop-up net, two buckets, or a fence section). A ball placed still about 6 steps out (5yo) or 8 to 10 steps out (7yo). Line up 4 to 5 balls.
- **Steps:**
  1. Short run-up from slightly to the side (not straight behind the ball).
  2. Non-kicking foot lands **beside** the ball, pointing at the goal.
  3. Kicking foot: **toe pointed down**, ankle stiff, and he strikes the middle of the ball with his **shoelaces**.
  4. Follow through toward the goal and land on the kicking foot.
  5. *7yo:* once he's hitting it clean, add a Pillow touch first: you pass, he touches into space, then shoots.
- **Silent demo:** Point at your own shoelaces, then point at the ball. Point your toe down hard and wiggle it to show it's locked. Walk through the kick in slow motion, then do one at full speed into the goal and celebrate.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Toe-pokes (kicks with his big toe). **Fix:** Put a small sticker or a bit of tape on his shoelaces: "The sticker has to touch the ball." That gives him an outside target instead of a body rule.
  - *5yo:* Swings and misses or tops the ball. **Fix:** Shorten the run-up to 1 or 2 steps. Start with a still ball every time. Big ball (size 4) if you have one.
  - *7yo:* Leans way back and the ball flies over the goal. **Fix:** "Chest over the ball, like you're peeking at it." Put a cone behind the goal as a "no sky-balls" marker: over the cone = zero points.
  - *7yo:* Stands right behind the ball and pokes it with his toe. **Fix:** Start his run-up from a cone at a slight angle (about 45°). The angle makes a laces strike natural.
- **Make it fun:** Target zones. Hang shirts or towels in the corners of the goal: corner = 3 points, anywhere in = 1. Let them name their shot ("The Thunder Rocket!").
- **Why it works:** Putting attention on the shoelaces meeting the ball is an **external focus**, which is better than "point your toe, lock your ankle." The sticker trick turns it into a game. Starting with a still ball, then adding a first touch (for the 7yo) is a simple **part-to-whole progression**.
- 🎥 **Video:** [Kids shooting basics](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AwbuY5mAq-g) · *Verified title: "Kids soccer SHOOTING basics ⚽️" (Prime Coaching Sport).* Short, kid-level shooting technique.

## Game: Treasure Keeper (7 min) · Cue: **Shell**
- **Setup:** The 4 x 4 step cone square. One ball, which is the treasure. One "keeper" (with the ball) and one "pirate" (you, at first).
- **Steps:**
  1. The keeper has the ball inside the square. The pirate tries to steal it or knock it out.
  2. The keeper **turns his back or side to the pirate**, knees bent, arms out a little for balance (no pushing), ball on the foot **farthest from the pirate**, and uses the sole of his foot to roll the ball away from the pirate.
  3. He's the turtle and his body is the shell. The ball is hidden underneath.
  4. Keep it for 10 seconds (5yo) or 15 seconds (7yo) = treasure saved, 1 point.
  5. Rounds of 3 to 4, then rest.
  6. *Brother vs. brother:* when the 7yo is the pirate, he must keep his hands behind his back and can only *walk*. When the 5yo is the pirate, he can run.
- **Silent demo:** You keep the ball and have the 7yo be the pirate. Turn your back to him, bend your knees, put one arm out, and roll the ball away with your sole each time he reaches. Look over your shoulder at him (show that you watch the pirate). Do it slowly with a big grin.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Faces the pirate with the ball in front, which is an easy steal. **Fix:** Turn him gently by the shoulders: "Shell toward the pirate." Play in "slow-motion pirate" mode so he has time to turn.
  - *5yo:* Picks up the ball with his hands. **Fix:** Laugh, then "Turtles don't have hands!" Restart.
  - *7yo:* Stands straight up, so he's easy to bump off the ball. **Fix:** "Low turtle. Sit in a chair." Lower in the knees means harder to move.
  - *7yo:* Pushes with his arm or grabs. **Fix:** Arm out for *feeling* where the pirate is, not pushing. If he pushes, the pirate gets the treasure. Quick, no lecture.
- **Make it fun:** Pirate voice ("Arrrr, give me yer treasure!"). Count the 10 seconds out loud like a countdown. Let the 7yo be the pirate against you, so he gets to "attack" too.
- **Why it works:** Shielding is a whole-body skill that's hard to explain and easy to *feel* under gentle pressure. Starting with you as a slow pirate gives a **just-right challenge**. Rules like "the 7yo walks with hands behind his back" make the brother matchup fair, and that kind of rule is how you'll balance mixed skill levels with your group at the fields too.

## Cool-down: Sleepy Sole Rolls (3 min) · Cue: **Sleepy**
- **Setup:** Standing next to a fence or holding your hand, ball under one foot.
- **Steps:**
  1. Slowly roll the ball forward and back under the sole, then side to side, then in little circles. 30 seconds each foot.
  2. Sit down and roll the ball slowly down each leg like a rolling pin.
  3. Finish: "Which one was harder today, Pillow, Shoelaces, or Shell?"
- **Silent demo:** Yawn dramatically, then roll the ball super slowly under your foot with your eyes half closed.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Rolls it fast and loses it. **Fix:** "Sleepy speed, slower than a snail." Do it right beside him.
  - *7yo:* Turns it into a trick contest. **Fix:** Fine for 10 seconds. Then "Now the slowest roll wins."
- **Make it fun:** The ball is falling asleep. Whoever rolls it the slowest without stopping "tucks it in" best.
- **Why it works:** It keeps light contact with the ball while heart rates come down, and the question at the end gets them reflecting in their own words.
