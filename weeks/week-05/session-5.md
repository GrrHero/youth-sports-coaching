# Week 5 · Session 5: Mixed Backyard Day: Goonies Treasure Trail (29 min)

[← Session 4](session-4.md) · [Week 5 overview](README.md)

**Equipment:** A bat, wiffle balls (only), a tee, 4 to 6 cones for gates, a pop-up net if you have one, gloves, a base, a soccer ball and small goal, a foam football and a hula hoop, the chalk ladder, a playground ball or junior basketball, a laundry basket (the "hoop"), and "gold coins" (poker chips, pennies or bottle caps) plus a bucket (the treasure chest).

**Levels this session:**
- **5yo:** Hits off the tee, then short front toss from 8 to 10 feet, at a big middle gate. Pass-and-pivot from 2 to 3 steps away with two-hand passes, a jump stop and a simple turn. Trail stations at short distances.
- **7yo:** Short coach pitch (soft overhand) from 15 to 20 feet at the middle gate, then opposite field. Chest and bounce passes from 4 to 5 steps, a jump stop and a pivot on a "glued" foot. Trail stations at full distance.

> **Safety note for you:** short coach pitch puts you close to the hitter. **Wiffle balls only.** Pitch from one knee (or a bucket) or from behind a pop-up net if you have one, and turn your shoulder away after each pitch. Wear a cap and sunglasses. The waiting brother stays in the "Bat Bubble" (behind a cone 3 big steps back and off to the side). For the passing, do it on grass or a driveway with no cars coming. Fingers stay spread, not pointed at the ball, so a pass doesn't jam a finger.

## Warm-up: Four-Corner Sport Switch (4 min) · Cue: **Switch**
- **Setup:** 4 cones in a square, about 10 steps apart. Each corner is a sport: Baseball, Soccer, Football, Basketball.
- **Steps:**
  1. You call a corner and a move, and they travel there doing it:
     - **Baseball:** shuffle to the corner like going to first base, then stretch at the cone.
     - **Soccer:** run with high knees, then pretend to make a one-two pass.
     - **Football:** sidewinder shuffle, then a pretend rollout throw.
     - **Basketball:** skip there, jump stop with both feet at the cone and pivot.
  2. When you call "**Switch!**", they change direction to a new corner.
  3. 2 rounds through all corners, then a sprint to whatever corner you call last.
  4. *7yo add-on:* he calls the corners for one round.
- **Silent demo:** Do each corner's move for 3 seconds with an exaggerated face and point to the next corner. On "switch," do a dramatic stop-and-turn.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Forgets which corner is which. **Fix:** Put an object at each cone (glove, soccer ball, football, basketball). He runs to the object.
  - *5yo:* Gets silly and stops moving. **Fix:** Call the next corner fast; silly is fine as long as he's moving.
  - *7yo:* Rushes and skips the move at the corner. **Fix:** "No move, no corner." He goes back to the last corner if he skips it.
  - *7yo:* Thinks it's too easy. **Fix:** Let him invent a fifth move for one of the corners.
- **Make it fun:** Call silly switches ("Switch... like a penguin!").
- **Why it works:** A **dynamic warm-up** that reviews this week's movements (stretch, one-two, rollout, jump stop) in a few seconds each, so the body and brain both warm up. Quick direction changes on "switch" add a little **reaction** training.

## Skill Work A: Dance-with-the-Pitcher Gate Hitting (8 min) · Cue: **Dance**
- **Setup:** Home plate. A "middle gate" of two cones 30 to 40 feet out, straight up the middle (Laser Gate from Week 4), and an opposite-field gate for the 7yo. You pitch from 15 to 20 feet (7yo) on a knee or behind a net, or front toss from 8 to 10 feet (5yo).
- **Steps:**
  1. Hitter in his stance.
  2. As your pitching arm goes back, he starts his small load (weight shifts a little onto his back leg). As your arm comes forward, he steps toward you. That's the **dance**: your arm and his body move together, so he's ready when the ball arrives.
  3. He swings at good pitches, aiming through the middle gate.
  4. Through the gate = 2 points. Hit anywhere fair = 1. No comment on misses.
  5. Rounds of 6 pitches, then swap.
  6. *5yo:* starts off the tee for 3 swings ("dance" with an imaginary pitcher: you swing your arm, then he swings), then front toss.
  7. *7yo:* soft overhand from 15 to 20 feet with a real pitching motion. After 2 rounds, pitch some to the outside half and he aims at the opposite-field gate.
- **Silent demo:** Have the 7yo mime a pitching motion while you're the hitter. As his arm goes back, shift a little onto your back leg; as his arm comes forward, step and swing. Do it in slow motion twice, moving together like dance partners. Then point at his arm and at your feet: "we move together."
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Starts the swing after the ball is already past. **Fix:** Go back to the tee, then a slower, higher toss. Say "Dance" as your arm starts back, so he starts early.
  - *5yo:* Steps away from the plate (toward his dugout side). **Fix:** Lay a jump rope on the ground from his front foot toward you: "Step on the rope." (Afraid of the ball? See Section 7C.)
  - *7yo:* Swings at every pitch, including bad ones. **Fix:** "Good pitches only." A swing at a pitch above his shoulders or in the dirt = minus 1. Ask: "Was that one in your zone?"
  - *7yo:* Lunges forward and swings with just his arms. **Fix:** Don't explain weight shift. Move the pitch a little slower and ask: "Where was your weight when you hit your best one, on your back leg or way out front?"
- **Make it fun:** Play music and literally dance in between rounds. Keep a "gate score" per round; each boy chases his own best.
- **Why it works:** Coach pitch is new timing: a moving arm and a ball coming from farther away. Linking the hitter's movement to the pitcher's arm is a **rhythm cue**, and rhythm helps kids start their swing on time instead of reacting late. The gate keeps the focus **external** (where the ball goes), the same as Week 4.

## Skill Work B: Pass-and-Pivot Partners (6 min) · Cue: **Glue**
- **Setup:** Two lines of cones (or chalk lines) 2 to 3 steps apart (5yo) or 4 to 5 steps apart (7yo). One playground ball or junior basketball. You and one boy face each other.
- **Steps:**
  1. **Chest pass:** hold the ball at the chest with fingers spread on the sides, thumbs behind. Step toward the partner and push the ball straight to his chest, finishing with **thumbs pointing down**.
  2. **Bounce pass:** same push, but aim at a spot on the ground about two-thirds of the way to the partner so it bounces up to his waist.
  3. **Catch + jump stop:** the receiver catches with both hands, hops and lands on both feet at the same time ("jump stop").
  4. **Pivot:** he picks one foot to **glue** to the ground (the pivot foot) and turns on the ball of that foot, stepping around with the other foot, like a door swinging on a hinge. Then he passes back.
  5. 8 to 10 passes each type, swapping partners between the brothers and you.
  6. *5yo:* two-hand chest pass from 2 to 3 steps, bounce pass into a hula hoop on the ground, catch, and a simple quarter turn on a glued foot.
  7. *7yo:* chest and bounce passes from 4 to 5 steps, and a half-turn pivot (front pivot) before passing back. Add a "defender" (you) with arms up; he pivots away from you to make a bounce pass to his brother.
- **Silent demo:** Catch a pass, jump stop with a loud double-foot "thump," then put a finger on one shoe and pretend to pour glue on it. Swivel on that foot slowly in both directions, the other foot stepping around. Lift the glued foot once, gasp, and shake your head ("traveling!"). Then a chest pass with thumbs down, held in the finish.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Throws the ball underhand from his belly. **Fix:** Fine for a start. "Push it from your chest like you're pushing a door open." Stand closer.
  - *5yo:* Picks up his glued foot when he turns. **Fix:** Put a paper plate under his pivot foot: "The plate never leaves." Celebrate any turn where it stays.
  - *7yo:* Bounce pass bounces too close to himself and comes up at his partner's knees. **Fix:** Put a chalk "X" two-thirds of the way across. "Hit the X."
  - *7yo:* Pivots on his heel and gets stuck. **Fix:** "Pivot on your tiptoes, like squishing a bug." (That's the Week 1 hitting picture, now for basketball.)
- **Make it fun:** Count passes in a row without a drop. Then play "Monkey in the Middle" gently: you're the monkey, and the boys use pivots and bounce passes to keep the ball away from you.
- **Why it works:** Pivoting and passing is a **basketball-friendly** skill set (catch, stop, protect the ball, pass), but it also teaches body control and balance for every sport. The plate under the pivot foot is a **constraint** that teaches the rule without a lecture, and "thumbs down" gives a clear picture of a good push.
- 🎥 **Video:** [Chest and bounce passes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sKd6aAN8CeY) · *Verified title: "The Two-Handed Pass: Chest and Bounce | Basketball" (SIKANA English).* A slow, clear demo of hand position, stepping into the pass and where to bounce the ball. Adult demonstrators, so it's for you.

## Game: One-Eyed Willy's Treasure Trail (8 min) · Cue: **Gold**
- **Setup:** A trail of 5 stations, numbered with cones, with the treasure chest (bucket) at the end:
  1. **Ladder Lagoon:** a clean trip through the chalk ladder (5yo one foot per box; 7yo in-in-out-out).
  2. **First Base Fort:** you throw from 15 feet; he catches with a foot on the base (Bag and Stretch).
  3. **One-Two Cove:** dribble, pass to you, run past a cone, get it back and score in the small goal.
  4. **Rollout Rock:** roll out and throw to the hoop ahead of the walking brother (or you).
  5. **Pivot Pirate Ship:** catch your pass, jump stop, pivot and bounce-pass into the laundry basket "hoop" lying on its side (5yo: two-hand chest pass into the basket from 2 steps).
  - Each clean station = 1 **gold** coin into the chest.
- **Steps:**
  1. Brothers take turns going through the whole trail (about 45 seconds each).
  2. They're a **team** trying to reach One-Eyed Willy's number (for example, 20 gold coins in 6 trips combined).
  3. "Clean" means done right, not fast.
  4. *5yo:* short distances and big balls. *7yo:* full distances.
- **Silent demo:** Walk the trail once in slow motion, miming each station and dropping a pretend coin in the chest after each. Shake the chest and do a pirate "Arrr!"
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Gets lost between stations. **Fix:** Draw chalk arrows on the ground between stations, like a treasure map. Walk with him the first time.
  - *5yo:* Upset when he misses a coin. **Fix:** Team treasure: his brother's coins count for him too. Give a "bravery coin" for a great try.
  - *7yo:* Rushes and gets sloppy. **Fix:** "Only clean ones earn gold." Speed doesn't score.
  - *7yo:* Criticizes his brother. **Fix:** Make him the "treasure keeper" on his brother's trip: he hands out the coins and has to say one specific nice thing at each station ("You squared up!").
- **Make it fun:** Pirate voices, a hand-drawn treasure map, and a real small prize if they hit the number (they pick the movie or dessert). "Goonies never say die!" when someone misses and tries again.
- **Why it works:** Switching between five different skills is **interleaved practice**. It feels harder than repeating one skill, but it leads to better long-term learning and helps them use the right skill at the right time. Team scoring keeps it cooperative, and the 7yo's "treasure keeper" job practices praising a teammate, a skill your team needs as much as any throw.

## Cool-down: Drop Anchor Breathing (3 min) · Cue: **Anchor**
- **Setup:** Sit cross-legged facing each other.
- **Steps:**
  1. Put both hands on the belly: that's the anchor.
  2. Breathe in slowly through the nose and feel the anchor rise (the ship lifts on a wave). Breathe out slowly through the mouth and feel it drop. 5 slow breaths.
  3. Each boy names his favorite play of the week and the cue word that goes with it.
  4. Finish with one piece of specific effort praise from you to each boy, using the job he did ("You kept your foot on the bag all week and made your brother's throws into outs").
- **Silent demo:** Hands on your belly, breathe in slowly with your eyes closed and your belly rising, then let it sink with a long, slow "ahhh." Make a little wave motion with one hand.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Breathes fast and giggles. **Fix:** Laugh with him, then "Can the anchor go down slowly?" Put your hand on his to feel it together.
  - *7yo:* Can't pick a favorite play. **Fix:** Offer two: "The bang-bang out at first, or the one-two goal?"
- **Make it fun:** End with "Goonies never say die!" all together, hands in.
- **Why it works:** Belly breathing slows the heart rate and calms the body. Naming the play and its cue word is **retrieval practice**, which is what makes the week's learning stick.
