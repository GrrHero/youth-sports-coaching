# Week 5 · Session 2: Soccer: One-Two, Magnet & Two Against the Giant (27 min)

[← Session 1](session-1.md) · [Week 5 overview](README.md) · [Session 3 →](session-3.md)

**Equipment:** 2 soccer balls (size 3), 8 to 10 cones, 2 small goals (pop-up goals or two pairs of shoes as posts), a wall, garage door or rebounder if you have one.

**Levels this session:**
- **5yo:** Passes to a **still** partner (you or the garage door) and runs to get the ball back, at walking-to-jogging speed. In the 2v1 game he's on a team with his brother, and you defend in "slow giant" mode.
- **7yo:** Plays the one-two around a cone, then around **you** as a live defender. In 2v1 he decides: dribble if you stay with the pass, pass if you come to him.

## Warm-up: Postman Pass-and-Move (4 min) · Cue: **Move**
- **Setup:** A square of 4 cones about 8 big steps apart: these are the "houses." Each boy stands at a house. You stand in the middle with one ball.
- **Steps:**
  1. You pass the ball (the "letter") to one boy.
  2. He stops it and passes it back to you with the inside of his foot (Lock from Week 2).
  3. As soon as the ball leaves his foot, he **moves** to a new, empty house. The next pass goes to his new address.
  4. Go back and forth between the boys. About 60 seconds a round, 3 rounds.
  5. *7yo add-on:* he has to move *before* you pass to him (he calls "Here!" when he's at his new house).
  6. *5yo:* he can pick up the ball and roll it back with his hands on the first round, then use his feet.
- **Silent demo:** Have the 7yo pass to you. Pass it back, then immediately jog to an empty cone, turn and open your arms like "I'm here." Point at your feet, then at the empty cone you ran to. Do it twice.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Passes, then stands and watches the ball. **Fix:** That's the most common habit in kids' soccer. Make a silly rule: "After your pass, the ground under you turns to lava." Point to an empty house.
  - *5yo:* Passes too hard and you have to chase it. **Fix:** "Mail is gentle so it doesn't break." Move closer.
  - *7yo:* Moves to a house where the pass is blocked by you. **Fix:** Stand in the way and say nothing. Ask: "Could I pass to you there?" He'll pick a spot where he can see you.
  - *7yo:* Kicks with his toe. **Fix:** Point at the side of your foot: "Lock," the Week 2 word. One reminder only.
- **Make it fun:** You're a cranky postman who complains when no one is home ("Nobody home again? I'll eat this letter!"). Count perfect deliveries in a row.
- **Why it works:** The give-and-go starts with one habit: **pass, then move**. Practicing it in a simple, no-pressure pattern turns it into a reflex before you add a defender. It also warms up passing and receiving with lots of touches.

## Skill Work A: Bounce-Back One-Two (7 min) · Cue: **One-two**
- **Setup:** A cone "defender" in the middle, a start line 5 big steps before it, and a pair of cones (a gate or small goal) 6 big steps past it. You stand off to one side of the cone, level with it, as the "wall." For the 5yo, the garage door or a rebounder can be the wall.
- **Steps:**
  1. **Whole first (1 to 2 minutes):** play a quick 2v1: both brothers against you. Watch what happens. Usually both kids dribble and nobody passes, or they pass and then stand still.
  2. Now the part: the boy dribbles toward the cone.
  3. Before he reaches it, he passes to you (pass number **one**).
  4. Then he runs past the other side of the cone.
  5. You pass the ball back into the space in front of him (pass number **two**). He collects it and dribbles or shoots through the gate.
  6. 6 to 8 reps, then switch sides so he passes with the other foot.
  7. *5yo:* he passes against the garage door (or to you standing still), runs to where it bounces back, and dribbles through the gate. Walking pace.
  8. *7yo:* after a few reps, you become a live defender who steps toward him. He passes to his brother (now the "wall") and runs past you for the return.
- **Silent demo:** Dribble slowly at the cone, pass to the 7yo standing beside it, hold up one finger. Run past the cone with a sneaky face, point at the open space in front of you, receive his pass and hold up two fingers. Then dribble through the gate and celebrate.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Passes, then waits for the ball to come back to where he is. **Fix:** "The ball comes back to where you're GOING." Put a "treasure spot" (a shoe) past the cone; he runs there after his pass.
  - *5yo:* Passes too early or too far from the wall. **Fix:** Move him closer. Celebrate any pass that hits the wall or reaches you.
  - *7yo:* Runs into the cone, or the same way as his pass. **Fix:** "Your pass goes one way around the defender, you go the other." Draw a little "V" in the air.
  - *7yo:* Passes from too far away, so the defender has time to recover. **Fix:** Ask: "Where's the best moment to pass, far away or right when the defender comes to you?" Let him test both.
- **Make it fun:** He's a pinball bouncing off the bumper (you or the wall). Make pinball sounds. Count one-twos that end in a goal.
- **Why it works:** The one-two (also called a wall pass) is the simplest way to beat a defender **without** dribbling past him, since the ball goes around one side and the player around the other. Starting with the 2v1 "whole" shows the boys the problem before they see the solution, which is **whole-part-whole** in action.
- 🎥 **Video:** [How to do a wall pass](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L7gfjaX2ve0) · *Verified title: "Coaching Soccer ~ How to do a Wall Pass + Online Soccer Academy" (Online Soccer Academy).* A clear coaching demo of the pass-run-receive pattern with a cone defender. The players are older, so it's for you.

## Skill Work B: 2v1 Magnet (6 min) · Cue: **Magnet**
- **Setup:** A channel 8 steps wide and 15 steps long. You (the defender) stand in the middle. The brothers start at one end with one ball, side by side and 4 to 5 steps apart. A small goal at the far end.
- **Steps:**
  1. The boy with the ball dribbles straight at you, like a **magnet** pulling you toward him.
  2. **If you come to him,** he passes to his brother, who is now free.
  3. **If you stay with his brother,** he keeps dribbling and shoots.
  4. Score = 1 point. You steal it = your point.
  5. Start in "slow giant" mode: you only walk, and you clearly commit to one boy. Then get sneakier.
  6. Swap who starts with the ball each rep.
  7. *5yo:* he can just dribble forward and pass when you say "Pass!" from a distance (a scaffold), then try deciding on his own.
  8. *7yo:* you start faking: lean toward him, then step back to the brother. He has to read your *body*, not your words.
- **Silent demo:** With the 7yo as the defender, dribble straight at him. When he steps toward you, pass to the 5yo and point at the open space. Next rep, when he stays with the 5yo, keep dribbling and shoot. Then point at the defender and hold up both hands like a scale: him coming to you = pass, him staying away = dribble.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Never passes, just dribbles into you. **Fix:** "Pass bonus": goals scored after a pass are worth 3. Then he'll pass every time, which is fine for a while. Decision-making comes later at 5.
  - *5yo:* Passes but his brother isn't ready. **Fix:** Teach the receiver's job: "Show your hands and say 'Here!'" (Rule 1: teach the getter's job first.)
  - *7yo:* Always passes or always dribbles, no matter what you do. **Fix:** Freeze for 10 seconds and ask: "Where am I standing? Who's free?" Then replay that moment.
  - *7yo:* Dribbles wide around you, so you never commit. **Fix:** "Magnets go straight at the defender." Narrow the channel.
- **Make it fun:** You're a slow, dramatic giant who gets dizzy when the boys pass around you, and falls over after a great pass.
- **Why it works:** A 2v1 is a **decision**, not a technique: pass or dribble depends on what the defender does. That kind of decision can only be learned against a live defender (**perception-action coupling**). Dribbling *at* the defender is the hidden key; it forces him to commit, which makes the decision easy to read.
- 🎥 **Video:** [2v1 coaching tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x4PA7k8AnhI) · *Verified title: "CATZCoach: 2v1" (AYSO Soccer).* An AYSO coaching clip on the 2v1: dribble at the defender, pass if he takes away the dribble, dribble if he takes away the pass. It's made for coaches and older players, so watch it yourself.

## Game: Two Against the Giant (7 min) · Cue: **Buddy**
- **Setup:** A field about 15 by 20 big steps. Two small goals at the far end, about 10 steps apart. You are the Giant. The brothers are a team with one ball.
- **Steps:**
  1. The brothers attack together against you, the Giant.
  2. A goal counts 1. A goal after a pass between the buddies counts 2. A goal after a one-two counts 3.
  3. If you win the ball, you dribble it back to the start line and the brothers try to win it back (2v1 defending).
  4. Rounds of 2 minutes, then a water break.
  5. *Handicaps:* the Giant has to keep one hand on his head (round 1), then walk only (round 2), then free (round 3, only if the boys are winning easily).
- **Silent demo:** Have the 7yo be the Giant for 20 seconds. You and the 5yo pass to each other around him with exaggerated "high-five" gestures after each pass. Point at the two goals.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Chases the ball wherever it goes, even when his brother has it. **Fix:** "Find a space where your buddy can see you." Point to the open space and say "Buddy!"
  - *5yo:* Upset when the Giant steals the ball. **Fix:** The Giant drops it after a 3-second roar and the boys get it back. Keep his success high.
  - *7yo:* Hogs the ball and doesn't pass to his little brother. **Fix:** Use the scoring: passes make goals worth more. If that's not enough, add "every goal needs a pass."
  - *7yo:* Gets frustrated with his brother's passes. **Fix:** Make him "the captain who makes his buddy look good." Praise him every time he gives an easy pass.
- **Make it fun:** Giant voice ("Fee-fi-fo-fum!"). When the brothers score, the Giant falls asleep for 5 seconds and snores.
- **Why it works:** This is a **small-sided game** with a scoring **constraint** that rewards passing. The brothers *want* to pass because it scores more, not because you told them to. That's the cleanest way to grow teamwork in young kids, and it's the same trick you can use with your team (see Week 4, Section 7A).

## Cool-down: Seesaw Stretch (3 min) · Cue: **Seesaw**
- **Setup:** The brothers sit facing each other on the grass, legs out in a "V," feet touching. They hold hands (or both hold a soccer ball between them).
- **Steps:**
  1. One leans back slowly while the other leans forward gently: the seesaw goes down on one side. Then switch.
  2. 6 slow seesaws, about 3 seconds each way.
  3. Let go and lie back for 3 slow breaths.
  4. Question: "When the Giant came to you, what did you do?"
- **Silent demo:** Sit with one boy and seesaw slowly with an exaggerated "wheee" whisper. Show it fast once and shake your head, then slow again and nod.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Pulls hard and his brother falls forward. **Fix:** "Seesaws are gentle." You sit behind him and slow the rhythm with your hands on his shoulders.
  - *7yo:* Turns it into a tug-of-war. **Fix:** "Can you make it so smooth that the ball doesn't wobble?" Use the ball between them.
- **Make it fun:** Sing "Row, Row, Row Your Boat" slowly while seesawing.
- **Why it works:** It's a partner stretch for the hamstrings and inner thighs after lots of passing and running, and it ends a teamwork day with teamwork. The question gets them to name the 2v1 decision rule in their own words.
