Warm-up: Animal Walks (5 min)

Bear crawl, frog jumps, crab walk and flamingo stand (one foot), 10–15 yards each. Call out the animals in random order. - Why: builds the strength, balance and coordination that every sport needs, and it doesn't feel like practice. - 5yo: shorter distances, and it's fine if the crab walk falls apart. - 7yo: "Can you bear crawl backward?" - Fun: you do it too. Kids LOVE seeing Dad bear crawl.

Skill 1: Running Form — "Cheek to Cheek" (8 min)

Break it down: 1. Arms only (sitting): Sit on the grass, legs out, and pump the arms. The hand swings from the cheek of the face back to the cheek of the bum. Elbows bent like an "L." 2. Arms + march: Stand up and march in place. Opposite arm goes with the opposite knee (right arm forward when the left knee is up). 3. Jog: Jog 15 yards with the arms working. 4. Run: Short races, 15–20 yards.

Cue words: "Cheek to cheek." Later (7yo, or next week): "Knees up."

Demo: Stand beside them (both facing the same way), pump your arms slowly and exaggerate it. Then do the "wrong" way (T-rex arms, or arms swinging across your body) and ask, "Which one looks faster?"

Common errors and fixes: | Error | Looks like | Fix | |---|---|---| | T-rex arms | Arms tight to the chest, barely moving | "Cheek to cheek!" Have them sit and pump (step 1) again | | Windshield wipers | Arms swinging across the body | "Swing like you're drawing on the wall beside you." Stand them next to a fence or wall and say, "Don't touch it!" | | Same arm/same leg | Rhythm looks off | Go back to a slow march and count "right arm, left knee" | | Head bobbing or looking back | Slows down, wobbles | "Eyes on the finish line." Put a cone or Dad at the finish | | Flat, slappy feet | Loud stomping | "Quiet, sneaky feet." Run like a ninja |

5yo vs. 7yo: The 5yo only needs arms moving opposite the legs, and "Cheek to cheek" can wait. The 7yo works on cheek to cheek plus a quick start: "Lean like you're about to fall, then go!"

Fun: "Beat the Ball." You roll a ball and they race it to a cone. Or "Dad's Shadow": they copy your running style, then you switch to silly styles (robot, penguin) and back to "fast style."

Skill 2: Throwing Intro — "Point, Step, Throw" (8 min)

Break it down (do the part, then the whole): 1. Side-on: Stand like you're "looking over your glove shoulder." Glove-side shoulder points at the target. 2. Point: Glove arm points at the target ("aim the laser"). 3. Arm back and up: Throwing hand goes back and up, ball away from the head ("show the ball to the fence behind you"). 4. Step and throw: Step toward the target with the glove-side foot and throw overhand. 5. Follow-through: Throwing hand finishes by the opposite knee or pocket.

Cue words: "Point, step, throw." (The 5yo might only get "Point!")

Demo: Stand beside them facing the target, the same way they face. Do 3 silent throws slowly, exaggerating the point and the step. Then: "Watch my glove hand... point... and my front foot... step... throw!"

Common errors and fixes: | Error | Looks like | Fix | |---|---|---| | Facing the target square | Chest faces target, arm-only push throw | Put a line on the ground (rope, chalk, hose). Feet go on the line, which points at the target. Say "Look over your shoulder" | | Wrong foot steps | Steps with the throwing-side foot | Put a sock or cone in front of the correct foot: "Step on the sock." | | No step at all | Throws standing still | "Step on the sock" again, or "Step like you're stepping over a puddle" | | Pushing/shot-put throw | Ball starts by the ear and gets pushed | Practice "show the ball to the fence behind you." Kneeling throws (on the throwing-side knee) isolate the arm | | Sidearm | Arm comes around flat | "Throw over the top, like throwing over a fence" (have a real fence or bench to throw over) | | Throws soft and careful | Lobs | Encourage hard throws at a big target like a fence or wall. Accuracy comes later |

5yo vs. 7yo: - 5yo: Tennis or foam ball, 10–15 ft away from a BIG target (garage door, fence). Only cue: "Point!" Celebrate any step. Catching will be hard for him, so throw at targets instead of to a partner. - 7yo: Real or soft baseball, 20–30 ft, a smaller target (a bucket, a chalk circle). Cues: "Point, step, throw." Then add a challenge: "Hit me in the chest 3 times in a row."

Fun: "Knock Down the Castle." Stack empty plastic bottles or cups on a bench or bucket. Points for each one knocked down. The 5yo stands closer. You keep score and narrate like a ballgame announcer.

Finale: Freeze Tag with Flamingos (4 min)

When tagged, you freeze on one foot like a flamingo. A teammate unfreezes you with a high five. Balance work disguised as tag.

Huddle

"What made the throws go farther?" (Guided discovery: let them say "stepping!")
