Week 2 · Session 5: Teach-Back Day & the Triple Threat Course

← Session 4 · Week 2 overview

Gear: Everything: cones, a ball of each kind, a target, a tee, a goal. 20-min version: shorten the teach-back to 5 min and the course to 2 rounds.

Warm-up: "Movement Dice" (4 min)

Steps: 1. Use a real die or call a number: 1 = skip, 2 = hop on your left foot, 3 = hop on your right foot, 4 = side shuffle, 5 = Timber Start sprint, 6 = Ninja jumps. 2. Each roll gets 15 seconds of that movement across the yard. 3. The boys take turns rolling.

Cue: Use the movement's own cue: "Step-hop," "Wide," "Timber," "Ninja." Silent demo: Roll the die and act out the movement while holding up the number of fingers. Errors & fixes: The 5yo forgets which number is which. Fix: Tape picture cards to a fence (stick figures are fine). The 7yo goes sloppy and fast. Fix: "Coach's eye. I'm watching for your best one." 5 vs 7: The 5yo gets "hop on any foot." The 7yo has to do the one called. Fun: Rolling a 6 also means Dad has to do it. Why it works: Mixing up the week's movements in random order makes them recall each skill on the spot, which helps them remember it better than repeating one move.

Skill work: "Coach Big Brother" (the full teach-back, 10 min)

Goal: The 7yo teaches his brother one skill from this week. You coach the coach. Steps for Dad: 1. Let the 7yo choose what to teach (likely the step throw, passing, or the Basket catch). 2. Prep him privately for 60 seconds: "Coaches use 1 word and a silent demo. What's your word? Show me your demo." 3. He runs it: word → demo → his brother tries → he gives one piece of praise and one tip. 4. You stay quiet and only step in with a question: "What did you see him do well?" 5. Switch: the 5yo "teaches" Dad something, such as how to skip or the Ninja landing. You do it badly on purpose so he can fix you.

Cue: "Coach's word?" (prompting the 7yo to use his cue). Silent demo: You model the coaching itself: act out "show, don't tell" by miming zipping your lips, then demoing. Errors & fixes: - 7yo: Lectures for 2 minutes. Fix: Give the "zipper" signal (a zip-lips gesture you taught him in prep). "One word and a demo, coach!" - 7yo: Criticizes harshly ("No! You're doing it wrong!"). Fix: Pull him aside: "Good coaches find something good first. What's one good thing?" Then let him try again. - 5yo: Won't listen to his brother. Fix: Make it a game: "Can you stump Coach Big Brother? Get it right 3 times and HE has to do 5 jumping jacks." - 5yo, teaching Dad: Gets shy. Fix: Ask "Is this right?" and do it obviously wrong. Kids can't resist correcting a grown-up.

5 vs 7: The 7yo teaches a real skill. The 5yo "teaches" a movement, mostly for the fun of being in charge. Fun: Give him a whistle or a clipboard, call him "Coach [name]," and have the 5yo call you "Rookie." Why it works: Explaining a skill makes the 7yo understand it better himself. He also feels what coaching is like, so he may be more patient when you coach him. And the 5yo gets to feel competent by being the one who knows.

Game: "Triple Threat Course" (5 min)

Steps: 1. Station 1: throw at a target 3 times (Belly / Step). 2. Station 2: dribble through 4 cones and shoot (Lock / Steer + Boom). 3. Station 3: run a route and catch a toss (Five / Basket). 4. Finish: Timber Start sprint to the finish cone. 5. Time each boy against his own last time, not against his brother.

Cue: Whichever cue fits the station they're on. Silent demo: Walk the course once in slow motion, pointing at each station. Errors & fixes: The 5yo gets lost between stations. Fix: You run beside him as his "tour guide." The 7yo rushes and gets sloppy. Fix: "Good form adds a bonus 5 seconds off your time" (you decide, generously). Fun: Have a podium (a bucket) and both boys win something different: "Fastest" and "Most Improved." Rotate who wins what. Why it works: Timing against himself keeps the 5yo from comparing himself to his older brother. Covering all four sports in one run shows them how the skills connect.

Kid's Choice ×2 (6 min)

5yo picks first.

Cool-down: "Week Wrap Circle" (4 min)

Steps: 1. Sit in a circle for a butterfly stretch, then reach for your toes. 2. Each person says one cue word from this week and shows it. 3. Each person says one thing they got better at and one thing they want to learn next. 4. Do a team cheer ("1-2-3, ATHLETES!").

Cue: "Best thing." Silent demo: You go first: show "Timber" with a little lean and say "I got better at being a patient coach." Errors & fixes: The 5yo says "everything" or "nothing." Fix: Offer two choices: "Was it catching or skipping?" The 7yo only lists flaws. Fix: "One thing that got better first." Why it works: People remember the end of an experience most, so a warm finish makes them look forward to next week. Saying out loud what they want to learn next gives them a say in Week 3.
