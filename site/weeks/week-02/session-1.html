Week 2 · Session 1: Speed Starts & Belly-Button Throws

Week 2 overview · Session 2 →

Gear: 4 cones (or shoes), tennis balls/soft baseballs, a bucket or laundry basket, a fence/wall or big target. 20-min version: shorten the skill block to 6 min and play one Choice game only.

Warm-up: "Timber Starts" (5 min)

Goal: 7yo learns the lean-and-go start. 5yo practices cheek-to-cheek arms. Steps: 1. Stand tall with feet together and arms ready. "Be a tree." 2. Lean forward from the ankles with the body straight like a board. Don't bend at the waist. 3. When you're about to tip over, step and sprint 10 big steps. 4. Walk back. Do 4–6 reps.

Cue: "Timber!" You yell it, they fall and go. Silent demo: Stand stiff, slowly tip forward with a wobbly face, then explode into 3 sprint steps. Don't say anything. Raise your eyebrows at them: your turn. Errors & fixes: - 5yo: Arms flap sideways or go across his body. Fix: Skip the lean for now. Have him do "pocket to cheek" arm swings standing still for 5 seconds, then run. If you need a reminder, say "Cheeks!" (from Week 1). - 7yo: Bends at the waist (butt sticks out) instead of leaning straight. Fix: "Can I push you over like a board?" Put your hand on his chest, have him lean into it, then pull your hand away so he has to go. - 7yo: Takes a tiny stutter step backward first. Fix: Put a shoe behind his heels. "Don't touch the shoe."

5yo vs 7yo: The 5yo does standing starts with an arm focus, and the lean is optional. The 7yo does the full lean and you time 2 reps. Fun: You're the "lumberjack" yelling Timber. Add fake-outs like "Tim… BURRITO!" (no going), then "TIMBER!" Why it works: The lean lets gravity start the sprint, so it uses a natural reflex instead of a list of instructions. It also gives one boy something to work on while the other works on something different, inside the same game.

📺 Video (for you): Top 10 Sprint Acceleration Drills [#10 Falling Sprint] — Overtime Athletes. Watch the falling-sprint section. It's demoed by older athletes, so shrink it to 10 steps for your boys.

Skill work: "Belly-Button Throws" (10 min)

Goal: 7yo turns his hips (belly button to target) and adds distance. 5yo steps every time and throws to Dad. Steps (building on Point-Step-Throw): 1. Stand sideways and point the glove at the target, same as last week. 2. Step toward the target. 3. New piece: spin the belly button so it faces the target, then the arm comes through. 4. Finish with the throwing hand near the opposite knee ("put it in your other pocket").

Cue: "Belly!" Silent demo: Draw a smiley face on a sticky note or piece of tape and stick it on your shirt over your belly button. Throw slowly, exaggerating the smiley turning to "look at" the target. Then put a sticker on his shirt too. Errors & fixes: - 5yo: Steps with the same foot as his throwing arm, or doesn't step at all. Fix: Put a "stepping spot" (cone or shoe) in front of his opposite foot. "Squish the cone with this foot, then throw." At 5, the consistent step is the whole goal. Leave the hips alone. - 5yo: Throws to the sky or the ground. Fix: Kneel down and hold your glove at his chest height as a big target. "Hit my glove." Being closer fixes most of it. - 7yo: Arm-only throw; the belly still faces sideways when the ball leaves. Fix: Guided discovery: "Throw once with your belly frozen and once with your belly turning. Which went farther?" Let him find the answer. - 7yo: Spins so hard he falls off to the side. Fix: "Finish like a statue for 2 seconds." Balance comes before power.

5yo vs 7yo: The 5yo throws to Dad at 15 ft, kneeling target, and you count steps, not hits. The 7yo throws at a fence target, and every 3 hits out of 5 earns one giant step back (the distance ladder). Fun: The 7yo's distance becomes "levels" like a video game (Level 1 = 20 ft … Level 5 = 40 ft). The 5yo gets a "Dad flinch": every throw that hits your glove, you fall over dramatically. Why it works: One new piece on top of a known pattern keeps the challenge just hard enough. Earning distance by hitting targets makes him keep accuracy while he adds power, instead of trading one for the other.

📺 Video (for you): How To Teach 6-8 Year Olds To Catch A Baseball — Antonelli Baseball. It's a catching video, but watch how he keeps the drills short, close, and confidence-first. That's the same mindset for throwing this week.

Game: "Turnaround Races" (5 min)

Goal: 7yo races with a turn. Both boys use their new start. Steps: 1. Put a cone 10–15 steps away. 2. On "Timber!" sprint to the cone, touch it with your hand, turn, and sprint back. 3. Turn trick: take little "chop" steps as you get near the cone, get low, touch, and push back.

Cue: "Chop!" Silent demo: Jog to the cone, take exaggerated tiny steps, crouch, touch, and push off. Then run it full speed so they see the difference. Errors & fixes: - 5yo: Runs past the cone or circles wide around it. Fix: Put a stuffed animal on the cone. "Give him a high five." A target to touch makes him stop. - 7yo: Big steps into the turn, so he slides or stumbles. Fix: Race him twice, once with "big steps at the cone" and once with "chop steps." Ask which was faster.

5yo vs 7yo: The 5yo gets a head start (a closer cone or a "go early" pass). The 7yo races Dad, and Dad has to do it hopping. Fun: Commentate like a sports announcer. Do "rematch" rounds where the loser picks the next race rule (backwards back, crab back). Why it works: Races give instant, honest feedback with no lecture needed. The handicaps keep both boys in the game so the younger one doesn't quit.

5yo picks first today.

Cool-down: "Melting Snowman" (3 min)

Steps: 1. Stand tall like a snowman and take 2 big "snow breaths" (in through the nose for 3, out through the mouth for 4). 2. The sun comes out: melt slowly to the ground over 10 seconds. 3. Lie down, then hug your knees and rock 5 times. 4. Teach-back: "Tell me today's throwing word and show me with no ball."

Cue: "Melt." Silent demo: You melt first, as dramatically as possible. Errors & fixes: 5yo drops instantly. Fix: Count down out loud from 10, and he melts one "drip" per number. 7yo rushes. Fix: Make it a contest: "Slowest melt wins." 5 vs 7: The 5yo just melts. The 7yo also explains "Belly" in his own words. Fun: Make sound effects (sizzle, drip). Why it works: Slow breathing helps tired kids settle down. Saying the cue one last time helps it stick, and the same ending ritual tells them practice is over in a good way.
