Week 2 · Session 2: Locked Ankles, Both Feet, First Shots

← Session 1 · Week 2 overview · Session 3 →

Gear: 1 ball each, 6–8 cones, a small goal (2 cones, a pop-up goal, or a lawn chair), 4 water bottles. 20-min version: combine warm-up and Choice. Play one Choice game as the warm-up.

Warm-up: "Skip & Hop Trail" (5 min)

Goal: Both boys work on skipping. 7yo adds one-foot hops. 5yo works on quiet landings. Steps: 1. Lay a line of 5 cones as the "trail." 2. Lap 1: step-hop, step-hop (skip) along the trail. 3. Lap 2 (7yo): 3 hops on the right foot, 3 on the left, alternating between cones. 4. Lap 3 (everyone): "Ninja jumps." Jump over each cone and land without a sound.

Cue: "Step-hop" for skipping and "Ninja" for landing. Use one cue per lap, never both at once. Silent demo: Skip in slow motion while tapping your leg on each hop. For Ninja, land with a deep knee bend and a finger to your lips. Errors & fixes: - 5yo: Gallops (same foot always leads) instead of skipping. Fix: Totally normal at 5. Hold his hand, skip together slowly, and say "step-hop, step-hop" in rhythm. Clap the beat. Some 5-year-olds just aren't ready to skip yet, and that's fine. - 5yo: Lands loud and stiff-legged. Fix: "Can you land so quietly I can't hear you?" Close your eyes and listen. Kids love fooling you. - 7yo: Hops are flat-footed, or he puts the other foot down. Fix: "Lift your other foot like a flamingo" (Week 1 language). Shrink the hops to tiny ones first.

5 vs 7: The 5yo skips and does Ninja jumps. The 7yo does all three laps, with hops on both feet. Fun: You're the "sleeping giant" at the end of the trail. Loud landings wake you up and you chase them. Why it works: Skipping and hopping build the rhythm, balance, and push-off that running and kicking depend on. Practicing quiet landings teaches soft knees, which protects joints and builds body control.

📺 Video (for you): Teaching Kids How to Skip — T.E.A.M. 4 Kids Pediatric Therapy. A pediatric therapy team shows the build-up from marching to galloping to hopping, which is perfect if the 5yo is stuck.

Skill work, 7yo: "Gate Passing" (10 min, split with the 5yo block below)

Goal: Inside-foot pass with a locked ankle, using both feet. Steps: 1. Make a "gate" of 2 cones 3 steps wide between you and him, 6–8 steps apart. 2. Plant the non-kicking foot beside the ball, toes pointing at the target. 3. Turn the kicking foot out like a duck and lock the ankle, toes up. 4. Push through the middle of the ball and follow through to the target. 5. Do 5 passes with the right foot, 5 with the left, then alternate.

Cue: "Lock!" Silent demo: Show a floppy ankle with an exaggerated wobbly pass that misses badly. Then lock your ankle, tap it with your hand, and make a crisp pass through the gate. Point at your ankle. Let him guess what changed. Errors & fixes: - 7yo: Toe-pokes. Fix: "Toe goes up to the sky, like you're showing me the bottom of your shoe." Touch the inside of his foot: "This is the passing spot." - 7yo: Plant foot is too far behind the ball, so the pass pops up. Fix: Put a coin or bottle cap next to the ball. "Plant foot lands on the coin." - 7yo: Weak left foot means he won't use it. Fix: "Left-foot passes through the gate count double." Praise effort out loud: "Brave left foot!"

Fun: Each pass through the gate is a point. Shrink the gate after every 5 points. Why it works: The locked ankle gives a flat, solid kicking surface, so the ball goes where he aims. Shrinking the gate raises the difficulty only after he succeeds. Practicing both feet now means he won't be one-footed for life.

Skill work, 5yo: "Cone Slalom" (during the same 10 min)

Goal: Dribble around cones with small touches, then a first shot. Steps: 1. Space 4 cones about 2 big steps apart in a line. 2. Use small taps (Week 1's small-touch dribbling) to weave in and out. 3. After the last cone, shoot at the goal.

Cue: "Steer." The ball is the car and the foot is the steering wheel. Silent demo: Make car noises while you weave slowly. Bump a cone "by accident," make a crash noise, laugh, and keep going. That shows mistakes are okay. Errors & fixes: - 5yo: Kicks the ball way ahead, then chases it. Fix: "The ball is on a leash. It has to stay close enough to pet." Bring the cones closer together. - 5yo: Skips cones to go straight to the goal. Fix: Put a "toll" on each cone, like a high five from Dad, before the next one. - 5yo: Picks the ball up. Fix: Laugh it off: "Oops, hands are sleeping!" Have him put his hands on his head while he dribbles.

Running both at once: Set the 5yo's slalom up next to the 7yo's gate. Pass with the 7yo while the 5yo does a lap, and cheer the 5yo's shot each time he finishes. That keeps both busy with no waiting. Why it works: Cones give him a clear path to follow, so he gets practice steering the ball without needing you to correct him. Ending with a shot gives every lap a payoff.

📺 Video (for you): U6 Gates Activity — Cal South Soccer. A state soccer association's U6 game for dribbling through cone gates. Steal the setup for the 5yo.

Game: "Soccer Bowling + Bonus Shot" (5 min)

Steps: 1. Set up 4 water bottles as pins. 2. 7yo: knock down pins with locked-ankle passes from 5–6 steps, alternating feet. 3. 5yo: dribble up close (2 steps away) and pass or shoot at the pins. 4. After every pin, the kicker gets a bonus shot on Dad in goal.

Cue: "Lock" for the 7yo and "Boom!" for the 5yo shot. "Boom" is his celebration word, and any contact counts. Silent demo: Line up, show the locked ankle, knock a pin, then do a silly celebration dance. Errors & fixes: - 5yo: Gets frustrated after misses. Fix: Move the start closer until he knocks one down, then celebrate wildly. - 7yo: Blasts with his laces (power over accuracy). Fix: "Pins only fall to passes, not rockets. Rockets are for bonus shots."

Fun: You play a "terrible goalie" who dives too late about 2 out of 3 times. Why it works: Knocking a pin over shows right away whether the pass was accurate. The bonus shot rewards good technique with something fun.

Kid's Choice ×2 (6 min)

7yo picks first today.

Cool-down: "Ball Roll Stretch" (3 min)

Steps: 1. Sit with legs straight out, ball on your shins. 2. Roll the ball slowly down to your toes and back, 5 times. (This stretches the hamstrings.) 3. Make a butterfly (soles together) and roll the ball in a circle around your feet. 4. Teach-back: the 7yo shows his brother "Lock" by pointing at his own ankle and doing one slow pass.

Cue: "Slow roll." Silent demo: Roll as slowly as a snail, making a snail face. Errors & fixes: The 5yo rolls fast and the ball escapes. Fix: "The ball is asleep. Don't wake it." The 7yo bends his knees to cheat. Fix: Play "keep your knees glued to the ground." 5 vs 7: Same stretch for both. Only the 7yo does the teach-back. Why it works: A gentle stretch at the end of a session is a calm, low-key way to finish. Teaching the cue makes the 7yo put it into his own words.
