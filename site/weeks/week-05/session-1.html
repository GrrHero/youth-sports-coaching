Week 5 · Session 1: Baseball: First Base Boss & Glove-Down Tags (28 min)

Week 5 overview · Session 2 →

Equipment: 2 gloves, a base (or a towel or flattened cardboard) for first base and a second one for "second base," 10 to 12 soft balls (foam or soft-core "safety" balls for the 5yo, wiffle or tennis balls for the 7yo), 4 cones, a stuffed animal or a pillow (the "runner" for the 5yo's tags).

Levels this session: - 5yo: Plays first base on a towel. You toss soft underhand throws from 10 to 12 feet, right at his chest at first, then a little to each side. He keeps one foot on the towel and catches with two hands. Tags a stuffed animal you slide along the grass toward the base. - 7yo: Plays a real first base. Takes 30 to 40 foot throws from his brother or you, including throws a step off-line, and stretches toward each one with his glove-side foot. Tags you as you walk or "slide" into second (soft balls only).

Safety note for you: use only foam or soft-core balls for the 5yo, and tennis or wiffle balls for any throw where a kid is catching from his brother; a wild throw from a 7yo is hard to read. For tags, nobody slides on the driveway: tag plays happen on grass, and you do the sliding (or slow-motion "pretend slides") so no kid's face or hands end up near a sliding foot. Teach the tagger to tag with the back of the glove with the ball squeezed inside, and to keep his own feet beside the base, not on top of it.

Warm-up: Find the Bag (4 min) · Cue: Bag

  • Setup: First base (towel or base) and a cone 4 big steps away toward where second base would be: that's where a first baseman stands while the pitch is thrown. You stand about 15 feet away, where a shortstop would be.
  • Steps: 1. The boy starts at the cone in a ready position (Popcorn from Week 3). 2. You call "Grounder!" and point somewhere else on the field (not at him). That means the ball wasn't hit to him. 3. He jogs (5yo) or sprints (7yo) to the bag, puts his throwing-hand foot on the front edge of the bag, turns his chest to face you, and shows a big glove target with his hands up. 4. You check: foot on the bag, chest at you, glove up. Then back to the cone. 5. 6 to 8 reps each; brothers can take turns being the "shortstop" who calls it. 6. 7yo add-on: call it from different places (third base side, second base side). He turns his chest to face the caller each time.
  • Silent demo: Stand at the cone. Mime the ball being hit away with a look over your shoulder, then trot to the bag. Tap the front edge of the base with your throwing-side foot (exaggerate it: tap-tap), turn your chest to the "shortstop," and hold up a big glove like a stop sign. Point at your foot, then at your chest, then at the glove. Twice.
  • Errors & fixes:
  • 5yo: Stands on the middle of the towel with both feet. Fix: Totally fine at 5. "One foot on the bag, one foot ready to move." Put a sticker or a leaf on the front edge of the towel: "Your foot touches the sticker."
  • 5yo: Faces you with his back to the bag and loses it. Fix: Walk it with him once, holding hands. Then make the bag big (a whole beach towel). Success first, precision later.
  • 7yo: Stretches his foot out before there's even a throw. Fix: "Square first, stretch later." Count only reps where he's balanced and facing you with both feet under him before your throw.
  • 7yo: Puts his glove-side foot on the bag. Fix: Don't explain left and right. Ask: "Which foot would let you reach farther toward the ball?" Let him try both and find it.
  • Make it fun: You're the radio announcer: "Ground ball to short... and the first baseman is THERE!" Time him from the cone to a correct setup and try to beat his best.
  • Why it works: Most 8U plays at first fail before the throw even happens: the first baseman isn't at the bag or isn't facing the thrower. Practicing the setup alone, with no ball, is a part practice step that makes the catch much easier. The "Bag" cue gives one clear picture: get there and get set.
  • 🎥 Video: First base footwork for youth players · Verified title: "Youth Baseball 1st Base Footwork and Drills" (Antonelli Baseball). A coach and an 8-year-old first baseman show sprinting to the bag, the throwing-hand foot on the front edge, squaring to the thrower and then stretching. Good for you; the 7yo can watch the first two minutes with you.

Skill Work A: Stretch & Squeeze at First (8 min) · Cue: Stretch

  • Setup: First base as before. The thrower (you at first, then the brother) stands 10 to 12 feet away (5yo catching) or 30 to 40 feet away (7yo catching), roughly where shortstop or third base would be. Two cones on each side of the thrower so you can vary where throws come from.
  • Steps: 1. Whole first (1 to 2 minutes): roll a grounder to the 7yo, he fields and throws to his brother at first, the way they did in Week 4. Watch the first baseman. Usually he's lunging early, off the bag, or catching one-handed. 2. Now the part: first baseman sets up at the bag with Bag (foot on, chest square, glove up). 3. You throw. Only when the ball is in the air does he step his glove-side foot toward the ball and reach his glove out to meet it: the Stretch. 4. He catches it out in front with the throwing hand coming over to squeeze it. His back foot stays touching the bag. 5. Throw some straight, some a step to the glove side, some a step to the throwing-hand side (he shuffles his foot on the bag to the other corner if needed). 6. 5yo: soft underhand tosses at his chest first. Then a little to each side. His "stretch" can be just a reach with both hands while his foot stays on the towel. 7. 7yo: real throws from his brother after a grounder (Field-and-Fire from Week 4), then from you overhand with some off-line.
  • Silent demo: Have the 7yo throw to you. Set up on the bag, wait with your glove up, and only when the ball leaves his hand, take a big, slow step toward the ball, reach your glove out, catch and squeeze with your other hand over it. Freeze in the stretch with a big proud face, then point at your back foot still touching the bag. Show it once wrong (stepping out early, then the throw goes the other way and you can't reach it, with a dramatic "oops"), then right again.
  • Errors & fixes:
  • 5yo: Turns his head or closes his eyes as the ball comes. Fix: Softer ball and shorter toss. "Watch the ball land in your glove like a bird landing in a nest." Celebrate every catch, even trapped ones. If he still flinches, see Section 7C.
  • 5yo: Steps off the towel to go get the ball. Fix: That's a natural instinct. Make it a game: "Hot lava everywhere except the towel. One foot always stays on the island."
  • 7yo: Stretches before the throw, then can't adjust when the throw goes the other way. Fix: Say nothing, just throw to the other side a few times. He'll discover why to wait. Then ask: "When's the best time to stretch?"
  • 7yo: Leans his head and chest way out over his front leg and loses balance. Fix: "Sink your legs, keep your eyes behind the glove." Put a ball cap on his head: if it falls off, he leaned too far.
  • Make it fun: Every catch with a foot on the bag is an "OUT!" with a big umpire call from the thrower. Count outs in a row. Ten in a row = the first baseman picks the next song.
  • Why it works: Waiting for the throw before stretching is a perception-action skill: you can only learn when to move by seeing real throws, including bad ones. Mixing throw locations is variable practice, which builds a more adaptable catcher than 20 perfect throws. Starting with a 1-minute "whole" version shows you (and him) exactly why the stretch matters.

Skill Work B: Glove-Down Tags (6 min) · Cue: Doorstep

  • Setup: A second base (towel or base) on grass. The tagger stands beside the base, not on it, on the side the throw is coming from. A "runner" starts 15 feet away: a stuffed animal or pillow you drag or slide along the grass (5yo), or you walking in or doing a slow-motion pretend slide (7yo).
  • Steps: 1. You (or the brother) toss the ball to the tagger at the base. 2. He catches, squeezes the ball in his glove with his throwing hand covering it. 3. He drops the glove straight down to the front of the base, the Doorstep: the spot where the runner has to arrive. Back of the glove facing the runner. 4. The runner "tags himself" by sliding into the glove. Then the tagger pulls the glove up and away and shows the ball: "OUT!" 5. 6 reps each, then swap. 6. 5yo: the stuffed-animal runner. No real runner at all for him this week. 7. 7yo: you are the runner. Walk at first, then a slow-motion "slide" on the grass where your foot arrives at the doorstep. Mix in a "sneaky" runner who goes for the back corner of the base so he has to move his glove.
  • Silent demo: Stand beside the base. Catch a toss, squeeze it, then drop your glove straight down to the front edge of the base like closing a door. Freeze. Have the 7yo slide his hand into the glove like a runner. Pull the glove up, hold up the ball, and give a huge silent "out" signal (fist pump). Then show the wrong way once: reaching way out to chase the runner, with a goofy wobble.
  • Errors & fixes:
  • 5yo: Swipes at the runner instead of putting the glove down. Fix: "The doormat waits; the runner comes to you." Put a paper plate on the front edge of the base. The glove goes on the plate.
  • 5yo: Drops the ball when the runner touches the glove. Fix: "Two hands squeeze the ball like a juice box." Practice catch-and-squeeze without a runner a few times.
  • 7yo: Reaches up high to tag the runner's chest. Fix: Do your slow-motion slide under his high tag and call "Safe!" He'll figure out that the glove has to be at the bag.
  • 7yo: Stands on top of the base and gets his feet in the way. Fix: "Base is for the runner." Put a cone where his feet belong, beside the base.
  • Make it fun: You're a sneaky runner with silly names ("Here comes Speedy the Sock!"). For the 5yo, the stuffed animal "cries" dramatically when it's tagged out.
  • Why it works: Kids naturally reach toward a runner, which is slow and misses slides. A fixed target (the doorstep) is an external focus that replaces a long explanation about glove angles. Using a stuffed animal for the 5yo takes the fear and the collision out of the skill completely; it's scaled equipment, the same idea as a lower hoop or a smaller ball.

Game: Bang-Bang at First (7 min) · Cue: Bang

  • Setup: Home plate (you), a short "shortstop" spot 20 to 30 feet from first, and first base. One boy fields, the other plays first base. You are the hitter and the runner.
  • Steps: 1. You roll (or softly hit with a fungo-style tap) a grounder to the fielder, then run to first. 2. Fielder fields and throws to first (Tick-tock from Week 4). 3. First baseman uses Bag and Stretch to catch it with his foot on the base. 4. Ball caught at the bag before you touch it = OUT, a point for the brothers' team. You beat it = SAFE, a point for you. 5. Close plays are "bang-bang" plays, and the first baseman makes the call: "Bang-bang... OUT!" 6. Swap jobs every 4 plays. First team to 7. 7. 5yo at first: you run slowly (backward or skipping); the 7yo's throw comes from 20 feet. 5yo fielding: he throws from 15 feet and can take a step or two closer before throwing.
  • Silent demo: Walk through one play in slow motion with no ball: point to the fielder, mime his throw, then hop to the bag yourself, stretch, catch and freeze as the slow "runner" (the 7yo) arrives. Then point at both brothers and yourself and hold up two fingers vs. one: "you two against me."
  • Errors & fixes:
  • 5yo: At first base, he runs toward the thrower to get the ball sooner. Fix: "The bag is your home. Outs only happen at home." Lay a hula hoop around the base: he must stay in the hoop.
  • 5yo: Gets sad when Dad is safe. Fix: Run slower and make your "safe" calls rare. It's a team game, so cheer the brothers' outs like a stadium crowd.
  • 7yo: Throws hard at his little brother and he can't catch it. Fix: "A good throw is a catchable throw. Your job is to make your brother look great." Only catches count as outs, so a hard, wild throw costs his team.
  • 7yo: Argues calls. Fix: The first baseman's call stands, no matter who is at first. That's a nice lesson in trusting a teammate.
  • Make it fun: Big stadium noise and a "bang-bang" chant on every close play. If the brothers win, they get to make you run the bases in slow motion while they "announce" you.
  • Why it works: It joins three skills (field, throw, receive) into one team play, which is exactly what happens in a real game. The brothers are teammates against you, so the 7yo learns that his throw is only good if his brother can catch it. That's the week's theme in one game. Your running speed keeps the outs around 60 to 70 percent, so both boys stay motivated.

Cool-down: Bear Hug Stretch (3 min) · Cue: Hug

  • Setup: Standing on the grass, gloves off.
  • Steps: 1. Give yourself a huge bear hug, hands reaching around to your back. Hold 3 slow breaths. Open the arms wide, then hug again with the other arm on top. 2. Hug one knee to your chest while standing (hold Dad's shoulder for balance). Then the other. 3. Sit down and hug both knees, rocking gently. 4. Question: "When was the right time to stretch for the throw?"
  • Silent demo: Wrap your arms around yourself with a sleepy, happy bear face and sway a little. Then hug a knee and wobble on purpose before steadying.
  • Errors & fixes:
  • 5yo: Hugs too fast and falls over on the knee hug. Fix: Let him hold your hand, or do the knee hug sitting down.
  • 7yo: Rushes through. Fix: "Bears hug for 3 slow breaths." Count out loud together.
  • Make it fun: Finish with a real family bear hug with a growl.
  • Why it works: It stretches the shoulders and upper back after throwing and the hips after lots of stretching at first base. The question gets him to put the key idea (wait, then stretch) into his own words, which is what makes it stick.
