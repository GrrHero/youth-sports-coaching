Week 5 · Session 3: Flag Football: QB on the Move & Cut-Off Angles (27 min)

← Session 2 · Week 5 overview · Session 4 →

Equipment: Flag belts, 1 junior or foam football (foam for the 5yo), 8 to 10 cones, 2 hula hoops (or towels) as landing spots.

Levels this session: - 5yo: Walks along the rollout lane, stops, turns his chest to the hoop and throws (a stop-and-throw). As a defender, walks, then jogs, to a cone where he'll meet you, and pulls a flag. - 7yo: Jogs the rollout lane and throws while still moving, before he passes the second cone. As a defender, takes an angle toward the meeting point when you pick one of two finish cones late.

Warm-up: Sidewinder Shuffle Toss (5 min) · Cue: Sidewinder

  • Setup: Two lines of cones about 4 steps apart (5yo) or 6 steps apart (7yo), running 15 steps down the yard like a road with two lanes.
  • Steps: 1. You and one boy face each other, one in each lane. 2. Both of you side-shuffle down the lanes together, like sidewinder snakes, staying across from each other. 3. While shuffling, toss the ball back and forth (underhand for the 5yo; overhand "phone" throws for the 7yo). 4. At the end, switch direction so he shuffles leading with his other side. 5. 3 to 4 trips each.
  • Silent demo: Shuffle sideways with wide, smooth steps, your chest facing your partner the whole time, and toss the ball while your feet keep moving. Freeze, point at your chest, then at your partner.
  • Errors & fixes:
  • 5yo: Stops his feet to throw or catch. Fix: Totally fine at first. Then "Snakes never stop!" Shorten the distance and slow the shuffle way down.
  • 5yo: Crosses his feet and trips. Fix: "Feet are magnets that never touch." Slow it down to a walk-shuffle.
  • 7yo: Turns his chest down the road (running forward) instead of facing you. Fix: "Sidewinders look at their friend." Throw only when his chest faces you.
  • 7yo: Throws behind you as you move. Fix: "Lead me, like Week 4." Ask: "Where will I be when the ball gets there?"
  • Make it fun: Hiss like a snake on every catch. Count catches in one trip and try to beat it.
  • Why it works: Throwing and catching while moving sideways is new and awkward; doing it slowly in a warm-up pre-teaches the rollout throw in Skill Work A. It also warms up the hips and shoulders for the session.

Skill Work A: Rollout Rocket Throws (7 min) · Cue: Square

  • Setup: A QB start cone. A "rollout lane" marked by 2 cones going sideways from the QB: one 2 steps to his throwing-hand side, one 6 steps. A receiver (you or the brother) starts 5 steps in front of the lane. A hoop as a landing spot about 6 steps (5yo) or 10 steps (7yo) downfield.
  • Steps: 1. Whole first (1 minute): you rush him slowly from the side and say "Throw it!" Watch: most kids run away and throw backward, or throw off-balance with their chest pointing the wrong way. 2. Now the part: on "Hut!", the QB rolls out along the lane, holding the ball up high by his chest with two hands. 3. Before he throws, he turns his chest and front shoulder to the receiver: Square. 4. Throw to the hoop ahead of the receiver (Week 4's lead pass). 5. 5yo: walks the lane, stops at the second cone, squares up and throws to the hoop with you walking toward it. A stop-and-throw is a real skill at 5. 6. 7yo: jogs the lane and throws before passing the second cone, without stopping. His feet keep moving; his chest turns. 7. 6 throws rolling to his throwing-hand side. 7yo only: then 4 rolling the other way, which is much harder (he has to turn his hips more).
  • Silent demo: Roll out slowly with the ball high, then do an exaggerated turn of your chest toward the receiver, with your front shoulder pointing at the hoop. Throw and freeze, then point at your chest and the hoop: "both point the same way." Show once without squaring (throwing sideways across your body, ball fluttering), make a confused face, then the correct way again.
  • Errors & fixes:
  • 5yo: Throws while still facing the sideline, so the ball goes sideways. Fix: "Belly button looks at the hoop, then throw." Put a sticker on his shirt over his belly: "Show your sticker to the hoop."
  • 5yo: Drops the ball while moving. Fix: Two hands on the ball up high. Walk the lane a couple of times with no throw at all: just carry and square.
  • 7yo: Throws backward across his body when he rolls left (for a righty). Fix: Start with only his strong side until it's smooth. When you add the other side, ask: "How can you turn so your chest still points at him?"
  • 7yo: Stops completely to throw. Fix: Put the second cone closer and add a fun rule: if he's still holding the ball when he reaches it, you "sack" him with a gentle flag pull.
  • Make it fun: He names his plays ("Rocket Rollout!"). A catch in the hoop = touchdown (6 points); a catch anywhere else = 3.
  • Why it works: In flag football, most 7- and 8-year-old QBs get "sacked" because they can only throw from a still stance. A rollout keeps the play alive. Square is an external cue (chest toward a target) rather than an internal one ("rotate your hips"), and external cues help kids learn faster. Starting with his strong side, then adding the other, is a simple-to-complex progression.
  • 🎥 Video: NFL FLAG QB position · Verified title: "NFL FLAG Football Quarterback (QB) Position" (NFL FLAG). Covers the one-step drop, pump fake and the rollout, plus the rule that the QB can't cross the line of scrimmage before throwing. The players are older, so it's for you.

Skill Work B: Cut-Off Corner (6 min) · Cue: Meet

  • Setup: A "T" of cones: you (the runner) start at the bottom of the T, and run straight up toward a finish cone 10 steps away. The defender starts at a cone 5 steps to the side and 2 steps ahead of you. A bright cone (or a hat) halfway along your path is the meeting point.
  • Steps: 1. On "Go!", you run toward the finish cone with flags on. 2. The defender doesn't run at you. He runs to the meeting point, the place where your path and his path cross, and pulls your flag there. 3. Start walking, then half speed, then (7yo) full speed. First get the path right, then add speed. 4. 5yo: walking, then jogging. You make sure he gets there in time. The meeting point stays marked. 5. 7yo: take the meeting-point cone away after a few reps. Then add a second finish cone off to the other side and pick one at the last second; he has to adjust his angle.
  • Silent demo: Let the 7yo be the runner. Point at him, then at the meeting cone, and nod. Walk to the meeting cone and pull his flag as he arrives. Then show it the wrong way: chase him from behind, and when he reaches the finish cone first, shrug with a sad face. Then the right way again with a thumbs-up.
  • Errors & fixes:
  • 5yo: Runs straight at you and ends up chasing you. Fix: Put a toy or treat on the meeting cone: "Run to the treasure, then grab the flag." Walk it together, holding hands, once.
  • 5yo: Reaches the cone, then freezes. Fix: "At the meeting place, grab!" Slow down so you arrive right when he does.
  • 7yo: Overruns the meeting point and you cut back behind him. Fix: "Chop, chop, GRAB" from Week 1: short, quick steps as he arrives. Ask: "What happened to your feet right before I got past?"
  • 7yo: Waits too long before starting his angle. Fix: Let him lose a few. Then ask: "When should you start running to the meeting place, early or late?"
  • Make it fun: You're a runaway train and he's the bridge operator who has to close the gate before the train passes. Train sounds required.
  • Why it works: Most missed flags at this age aren't speed problems; they're geometry problems: the defender runs at where the runner is. The marked meeting point is a visual scaffold you remove later, and walking it first gets the path right before speed makes it harder. It's the defensive mirror of last week's lead pass ("throw to where he's going" / "run to where he's going").
  • 🎥 Video: Pursuit angle drill · Verified title: "Youth Flag Football Drill | Defense Pursuit Drill | Cut off the runner | Where they are going | flag" (Flag Football with Coach D). A youth flag coach sets up a T-shaped pursuit drill and walks it, then half speed, then full speed, just like this. Useful for team practice too.

Game: Scramble Showdown (6 min) · Cue: Scramble

  • Setup: A line of scrimmage (cones) and an end zone 15 steps (5yo) or 20 steps (7yo) away. One boy is QB, the other is the rusher and then the defender. You are the receiver.
  • Steps: 1. On "Hut!", the rusher counts out loud "One Mississippi, two Mississippi, three Mississippi" from 5 steps away, then rushes the QB. 2. The QB scrambles out on a rollout, squares up and throws to you somewhere down the field. 3. If you catch it, the rusher turns into a defender: he takes a cut-off angle to the meeting point and tries to pull your flag before you reach the end zone. 4. Touchdown = 6 for the QB. Flag pulled = 3 for the defender. A "sack" (QB's flag pulled before he throws) = 2 for the defender. 5. Swap after every 2 plays. 6. Handicaps: the 7yo rushing his brother counts to 5 Mississippi. The 5yo rushing his brother counts to 2.
  • Silent demo: Walk one play in slow motion: you as QB rolling out and squaring up, the 7yo slowly rushing, a slow throw to the 5yo, then the 7yo cutting across to the meeting point. Point at each of the three jobs as it happens.
  • Errors & fixes:
  • 5yo: As QB, panics and throws immediately. Fix: Fine. Make it a short, easy catch for you. Or give him a longer count.
  • 5yo: As defender, chases you from behind. Fix: Say "Meet!" and point at a spot in front of you. Run slowly toward it so he can get there.
  • 7yo: Crosses the line of scrimmage before throwing. Fix: "Real-game rule: throw from behind the line." A throw from past it doesn't count. That's an actual flag football rule, so it's good to learn now.
  • 7yo: Rushes his little brother too hard and too soon. Fix: The count is the rule. A rusher who goes early gives the QB 6 points.
  • Make it fun: Play-by-play commentary ("He's scrambling... he's looking... he FIRES!"). Celebrate every great angle with a "Cut off!" chant.
  • Why it works: The rusher creates real time pressure, which is the reason a QB needs to roll out at all. Switching from rusher to pursuit defender in the same play teaches the boys to transition, a big idea in every invasion sport (soccer and basketball too).

Cool-down: Kneel-Down Stretch (3 min) · Cue: Kneel

  • Setup: On soft grass.
  • Steps: 1. "Victory formation": kneel on one knee like a QB taking a knee to end the game. Slowly shift your hips forward until you feel a stretch in the front of the down leg. Hold 3 breaths. Switch knees. 2. On both knees, sit back onto the heels, arms stretched forward on the grass. 3 slow breaths. 3. Question: "When you were the defender, where did you run, to where I was or where I was going?"
  • Silent demo: Take a knee with the football held like you just won the Super Bowl, then slowly lean your hips forward with a calm face.
  • Errors & fixes:
  • 5yo: Wobbles on one knee. Fix: Let him hold the football on the grass for balance, or hold your hand.
  • 7yo: Leans way forward with his back bent. Fix: "Tall like a QB, hips move, not your head."
  • Make it fun: "The game's over, we won!" Do a quiet "crowd cheer" in whispers.
  • Why it works: It stretches the hip flexors after lots of sprinting and shuffling, and the slow breaths bring the heart rate down. The question has him repeat the week's key defensive idea in his own words.
