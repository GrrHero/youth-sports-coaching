Week 5 · Session 4: Athleticism: Hot Feet, Rope Rookies & Dribble Traffic (28 min)

← Session 3 · Week 5 overview · Session 5 →

Equipment: A playground ball or junior basketball for each boy (a size 5 / 27.5" youth basketball for the 7yo; a bouncy playground ball for the 5yo), sidewalk chalk (or a rope ladder or sticks on grass), 2 jump ropes (sized so the handles reach the armpits when he stands on the middle; a longer rope for the "snake"), 3 pieces of colored paper (green, yellow, red), 4 cones. Sneakers on, flat driveway or patio.

Levels this session: - 5yo: Dribbles with two hands or does bounce-and-catch while walking. Chalk ladder with one foot in each box, then two feet in each box, at his own speed. Jump rope: hops over a rope lying on the ground, then "swing, stop, step over" with the rope. - 7yo: Dribbles with one hand, then the other, walking and changing speed. Chalk ladder with lateral and in-in-out-out patterns. Jump rope: single jumps with a slow turn, counting his best streak.

Safety note for you: give each jumper a "rope bubble" (at least two big steps from anyone else), and the waiting brother stands well outside it. Ropes never go around necks or get used as a leash, and they're put away (not left on the ground) after the session. Do the ladder and rope on a flat, dry surface; stop the rope work when landings get loud or he starts missing a lot. Tired legs trip. If you do this in the garage after dark, clear the floor first.

Warm-up: Dribble Traffic Cop (5 min) · Cue: Push

  • Setup: An open driveway "road" with cones at each end. You're the traffic cop with three colored papers.
  • Steps: 1. Each boy dribbles his ball in place first: push the ball down with the soft pads of his fingers (not slapping with a flat palm), at about belly height. 2. Then you hold up a color:
    • Green: dribble while walking down the road.
    • Yellow: dribble in place.
    • Red: catch the ball and hold it tight against the chest ("park the car"). 3. 60 to 90 seconds per round, 3 rounds. 4. 5yo: two-hand dribbles (push down, catch, push down), or bounce-and-catch. Walking only. 5. 7yo: one hand on green, switch hands on yellow. Last round: "speed bump" (a cone) he has to dribble around.
  • Silent demo: Dribble slowly in place, exaggerating your fingers pushing down like pressing a big soft button. Show a flat-hand slap once with a loud smack and a frown, then go back to the soft push with a smile. Then walk-dribble and catch-and-hold on an imaginary red light.
  • Errors & fixes:
  • 5yo: Slaps the ball and it bounces away. Fix: Bigger, bouncier ball, and two hands. "Push it like you're pushing a doorbell." Celebrate 3 in a row.
  • 5yo: Stares at the ball the whole time. Fix: Totally normal at 5. Don't fix it this week. The color game will make him peek up on his own.
  • 7yo: Dribbles too high (above his chest) and loses it when walking. Fix: "Keep it at your belly button." Hold your hand at belly height as a "ceiling."
  • 7yo: Only uses his strong hand. Fix: Weak-hand green lights get a "bonus round." Don't force it, reward it.
  • Make it fun: Car horn noises, a siren for "red," and sometimes a "flashing yellow" (you wiggle the paper) where they dribble as low as they can.
  • Why it works: Dribbling a ball while moving and watching a signal trains hand-eye coordination and attention split (ball and signal at the same time), skills that show up in every sport with a ball. It's a light, basketball-friendly warm-up that also warms up wrists and fingers for the jump rope later.
  • 🎥 Video: Kids' dribbling basics · Verified title: "Basketball Dribbling Drills For 6-9 Year Olds" (Basketball Coach Allen). Stationary dribbling, crossovers and high/low dribbles at the right level for the 7yo. You can watch it with him.

Skill Work A: Chalk Ladder Lightning (7 min) · Cue: Hot feet

  • Setup: Draw a ladder on the driveway with chalk: 8 to 10 boxes in a row, each about the size of one of his shoes heel to toe, plus a little extra (about 15 to 18 inches). On grass, use a rope ladder or sticks.
  • Steps: 1. Run-through: one foot in each box, light and quick, like the ground is hot. 2. Two feet in: both feet touch in every box (right-left in, right-left in). 3. 7yo: Sideways two-in: standing beside the ladder, step sideways into each box with both feet. 4. 7yo: In-in-out-out: facing the ladder from the side, step in-in (both feet in the box), out-out (both feet back out), then the next box. 5. 2 to 3 trips per pattern, walking back as rest. Quality first: no lines touched. 6. 5yo: only patterns 1 and 2, at his speed. Hold his hand on the first trip of each.
  • Silent demo: Walk the pattern in slow motion first, pointing down at each box as your foot lands. Then do it quick and light, tiptoes barely touching like the ground is a hot pan, with an "ow-ow-ow" face. Show one heavy, stompy trip and shake your head.
  • Errors & fixes:
  • 5yo: Skips boxes or jumps two at a time. Fix: Make the boxes smaller and put a chalk dot in each one: "Squish every dot."
  • 5yo: Gets frustrated with two-feet-in. Fix: Go back to the run-through and make it a race against the clock. Success first, then try two-in again next time.
  • 7yo: Speeds up and gets sloppy (lines touched). Fix: "Clean, then quick." Only clean trips count toward his timed best.
  • 7yo: Stands up tall and stiff. Fix: "Athlete stance: a little bend in the knees, arms ready." Pump your arms with him.
  • Make it fun: The ladder is a bridge over a lava river: lines are "cracks." For the 7yo, time clean trips and let him try to beat his record. Let each boy invent one pattern for Dad to try.
  • Why it works: Quick, light foot contacts in different patterns build footwork and rhythm, and the sideways patterns train the lateral movement used in every sport: shuffling to a grounder, defending a dribbler, pivoting in basketball. The patterns also make him plan his next step while moving (thinking + moving), and the hopscotch from last week was the stepping stone.

Skill Work B: Rope Rookie Jumps (6 min) · Cue: Swing-hop

  • Setup: Each boy has a rope (sized to his armpits) and his own space. A flat driveway or patio.
  • Steps: 1. Jump without the rope turning: lay the rope flat on the ground and do 10 little two-foot hops over it, forward and back, on the balls of the feet (Pogo from Week 4). 2. Swing, stop: hold both handles with the rope behind his heels. Swing it over his head so it lands on the ground in front of his toes. Stop. 3. Swing, stop, hop: after it lands in front, he hops over it. That's one swing-hop. 4. Repeat swing-stop-hop slowly 5 to 10 times. When it's smooth, take away the stop: swing-hop, swing-hop. 5. 5yo: steps 1 to 3 only. Stepping over the rope instead of hopping is fine. 6. 7yo: steps 1 to 4, then count his best streak of continuous jumps. Try 3, then 5, then 10.
  • Silent demo: Show the rope behind your heels, swing it over slowly and freeze it on the ground in front of your toes. Point at it, hop over, then grin. Do it 3 times slowly, then once smoothly with no stop. Show your hands low by your hips: small wrist circles, not big arm windmills.
  • Errors & fixes:
  • 5yo: Jumps before the rope arrives, or swings it wildly. Fix: Totally normal. Stay on "swing, stop" and just cheer the rope landing in front. A pool noodle or a rope cut in half and held in each hand (no rope to jump at all, just the swing motion) works too.
  • 5yo: Gets frustrated quickly. Fix: Very short turns (30 to 60 seconds), and let him go back to hopping over the rope on the ground. Effort praise: "You kept trying after it caught your feet. That's how jumpers learn!"
  • 7yo: Big arm circles that make the rope hit the ground hard and early. Fix: "Elbows stay close, wrists do the work." Have him hold a sock under each arm.
  • 7yo: Jumps really high and tires out fast. Fix: "Little bunny hops just high enough." Pogo bounces from Week 4: small and springy.
  • Make it fun: Count out loud together, and keep a "streak board" in chalk. For the 7yo, try a jump rope rhyme ("Teddy bear, teddy bear, turn around...").
  • Why it works: Jumping rope combines timing, rhythm and coordination (hands and feet in sync), and kids who learn it early tend to have quicker feet in other sports. Breaking it into jump, then swing, then put them together is part-whole learning, the progression most PE teachers use, because trying the whole thing first is usually too hard for beginners.
  • 🎥 Video: Teaching a kid to jump rope · Verified title: "How to Teach a Kid to Jump Rope for the First Time | Kindergarten PE Lesson |" (The PE Specialist). A PE teacher's kindergarten progression (jump first, then the swing, then put them together) with tips on short bursts and encouragement. Great for the 5yo's level; watch it first.

Game: Snake & Helicopter (6 min) · Cue: Snake

  • Setup: Grass or a soft surface. A long jump rope (or a regular rope) held by you at one end, lying on the ground.
  • Steps: 1. Snake: you wiggle the rope on the ground side to side like a snake. The boys take turns jumping over it without getting "bitten" (touched). 2. Make the snake wiggle faster, then add "jumping snake" waves (small up-and-down ripples). 3. Helicopter (7yo, and the 5yo if he's ready): you crouch in the middle and swing the rope slowly in a low circle along the ground. The boys stand in the circle and jump as the rope passes. 4. Bitten = 1 point to the snake. Everyone tries to keep the snake to 3 points or fewer per round. 5. 5yo: slow, small snake wiggles, and he can jump with you holding his hand. 6. 7yo: faster waves, and in Helicopter he tries to jump 10 in a row.
  • Silent demo: Lay the rope on the ground and have the 7yo wiggle it slowly. Jump over it with your knees up high and a scared face, then land and celebrate. Point at the rope and hiss.
  • Errors & fixes:
  • 5yo: Steps on the rope or freezes. Fix: Slower snake. Let him be the snake handler for a round (it's great for seeing the timing).
  • 5yo: Upset when he gets bitten. Fix: "Snake bites are just tickles." Make the snake "sleepy" (very slow) for him.
  • 7yo: Jumps way too early in Helicopter. Fix: "Watch the rope, jump when it's one step away." Slow your swing.
  • 7yo: Gets competitive with his brother's points. Fix: It's the boys against the snake. Their bites add up together, so they cheer each other.
  • Make it fun: Big hissing sounds. The snake has a name (let the 5yo name it). When the boys beat the round, the snake "falls asleep" and they have to tiptoe past it.
  • Why it works: It's jump-rope timing without the hard part (turning the rope), so both boys get lots of successful jumps. Jumping a moving object trains reaction and timing, and the team scoring against the snake keeps a 5 vs. 7 contest out of it.

Cool-down: Sunset Stretch (4 min) · Cue: Sunset

  • Setup: Standing, then sitting on the grass.
  • Steps: 1. Reach both arms high like the sun at noon. Breathe in. 2. Slowly lower the arms out to the sides, like the sun setting, while breathing out. 4 slow sunsets. 3. Sit and stretch the legs out, reaching for the toes ("the sun goes down behind the hills"). 3 slow breaths. 4. Lie back and look at the sky for 3 breaths. 5. Question: "Which was harder for your feet, the ladder or the rope? What helped?"
  • Silent demo: Slowly raise your arms high, take a big breath, then lower them in a slow arc with a sleepy, peaceful face.
  • Errors & fixes:
  • 5yo: Flaps the arms quickly. Fix: "The sun sets slowly." Do it right beside him at his speed.
  • 7yo: Wants to show off one more jump rope streak. Fix: "Save it for Saturday's treasure trail." That gives him something to look forward to.
  • Make it fun: With the early sunsets this week, do it outside right as the real sun goes down, and see if they can match it.
  • Why it works: Slow arm movements with long breaths out bring the heart rate down after a jumpy session. The question invites him to notice what helped, and his answer tells you which skill to put into Saturday's game.
