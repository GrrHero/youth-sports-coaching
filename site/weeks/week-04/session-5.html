Week 4 · Session 5: Mixed Backyard Day: Halloween Haunted Gauntlet (28 min)

← Session 4 · Week 4 overview

Equipment: Wiffle balls, a bat, a tee (optional), 2 orange balls or small pumpkins (or orange cones) as "pumpkin targets," a laundry basket on a chair, gloves, a soccer ball, a small goal, a foam football, a hula hoop, the hopscotch grid from Friday, a bedsheet and a chair (the "ghost"), a bucket and small "treat" tokens (wrapped candies, poker chips or bottle caps).

Levels this session: - 5yo: Hits off a tee or very short front toss toward the center pumpkin only. Gauntlet stations at the short distances, walking-speed fakes against the ghost. - 7yo: Front toss, calls his own pumpkin (center or opposite side). Gauntlet stations at full distance and speed.

Warm-up: Monster Mash Moves (4 min) · Cue: Monster

  • Setup: Open grass, two cones 15 steps apart.
  • Steps: 1. You call a monster and they travel between the cones moving like it:
    • Zombie: stiff-legged walk, arms out in front (stretches the back of the legs)
    • Werewolf: bear crawl on all fours
    • Vampire: side shuffle with a "cape" (arms out)
    • Mummy: high-knee march, arms straight out
    • Frankenstein: big lunging steps 2. One trip each, then a final "Monster Dash" sprint. 3. 7yo add-on: on the second half of each trip, he does it backward.
  • Silent demo: Do each monster for 3 seconds with an over-the-top face. They'll copy the face as much as the move, and that's fine.
  • Errors & fixes:
  • 5yo: Gets too silly and stops moving. Fix: Silly is good. Keep trips short and call the next monster quickly.
  • 5yo: Can't bear-crawl the full way. Fix: Half the distance, then stand up and howl.
  • 7yo: Rushes the lunges with tiny steps. Fix: "Frankenstein takes giant steps." Count steps: fewer is better.
  • 7yo: Says it's babyish. Fix: Let him invent and lead one monster move. Ownership beats cool-factor.
  • Make it fun: Monster sounds. Each boy invents a new monster for the last trip.
  • Why it works: A dynamic warm-up (stretching while moving) gets the whole body ready, and the Halloween theme makes it fun enough that they don't notice it's a warm-up. Leading a monster gives the 7yo some ownership, a strong motivator at his age.

Skill Work A: Pumpkin Patch Spray Hitting (7 min) · Cue: Pumpkin

  • Setup: Home plate. Pumpkin targets: one straight up the middle (30 to 40 feet out) and, for the 7yo, one on the opposite-field side. Same safety setup as Session 1 (wiffle balls only, you off the line).
  • Steps: 1. Before each swing the hitter calls his pumpkin ("Center!" or "Other side!"). 2. 5yo: hits off the tee or a very short front toss toward the center pumpkin only. 3. 7yo: front toss. When he calls the opposite side, you toss to the outside half. 4. Ball rolls or flies within 2 big steps of the called pumpkin = 3 treat tokens. Right part of the field but not close = 1. 5. Rounds of 5 swings, alternating brothers.
  • Silent demo: Stand at the plate, point your bat at a pumpkin like Babe Ruth calling his shot, then swing and hold your finish with your chest facing that pumpkin.
  • Errors & fixes:
  • 5yo: Looks at the pumpkin instead of the ball during the swing. Fix: "Point at the pumpkin, then eyes go to the ball." Use the tee so there's no timing to worry about.
  • 5yo: Forgets to call. Fix: You call it for him and point. Calling is a bonus, not essential at 5.
  • 7yo: Calls the opposite side but pulls it anyway. Fix: Ask: "Where did you hit it, out front or a little deeper?" Remind him of Monday's gate. Toss a bit farther outside.
  • 7yo: Gets angry when he misses a called shot. Fix: "Even big-leaguers miss their spot most of the time. Let's count how many you get." Normalize missing.
  • Make it fun: The pumpkins are haunted. A ball near one "wakes it up" with a spooky sound effect from you.
  • Why it works: It reviews Monday's directional hitting a few days later. Spacing practice out like this (rather than cramming) helps it stick. Calling the shot adds intention: he makes a plan and tests it, which makes him notice cause and effect.
  • 🎥 Video: Hitting to the opposite field · Verified title: "Top 5 Ways to Teaching Hitting to the Opposite Field" (Dominate The Diamond). Five youth-coach drills for opposite-field hitting, including "target practice" (calling a spot). For you, with ideas for the 7yo and your team.

Skill Work B: Ghost Defender Finishing (6 min) · Cue: Boo

  • Setup: A bedsheet draped over a chair (or a cone with a pillowcase) is the "ghost" defender, 8 steps from the start. A goal 5 steps behind the ghost.
  • Steps: 1. Dribble at the ghost. 2. About 2 steps away, you whisper "Boo!" (or he says it himself). That's the moment to do his move: the Trick fake or the Slice from Tuesday. 3. Get past the ghost and shoot. 4. 5yo: walking speed; any move counts, even just dribbling around the ghost. 5. 7yo: jogging speed, and he has to use a different move each rep (Trick, then Slice, then his own invention). 6. 6 to 8 reps each.
  • Silent demo: Dribble toward the ghost, mouth "Boo!" with a scared face, do a big fake, slip past and shoot. Then turn and wave bye-bye to the ghost.
  • Errors & fixes:
  • 5yo: Does his move too far away or right on top of the ghost. Fix: Put a "boo spot" (a leaf or small pumpkin) 2 steps before it.
  • 5yo: Forgets to shoot after getting past. Fix: "Ghosts hate goals!" The goal is the reason to beat the ghost.
  • 7yo: Does the move, then slows down and stops before shooting. Fix: "Get past, then quick shot." Count shots taken within 2 touches of beating the ghost.
  • 7yo: Always uses the same move. Fix: That's why the rule says a different move each rep. Praise new ones loudly.
  • Make it fun: The ghost "talks" in your spooky voice ("You can't get past meeee..."). A goal = 2 treats.
  • Why it works: It repeats Tuesday's moves with a new look (a ghost instead of a troll). That's variable practice: same skill, different setting. "Boo" is a timing trigger. It tells him when to do the move, which is the hardest part of 1v1 for young kids.

Game: Haunted House Gauntlet (8 min) · Cue: Treat

  • Setup: A circuit of 4 stations, numbered with cones: 1. Hopscotch Crypt: hop through Friday's grid (5yo classic pattern; 7yo single leg only). 2. Graveyard Grounder: you roll a grounder; he fields it and throws to the basket at first (Tick-tock). 3. Ghost Pass: he throws the foam football to the hoop ahead of the walking or jogging brother (or you). 4. Ghost Goal: dribble past the ghost and shoot.
  • Each clean station = 1 treat token into the team bucket.
  • Steps: 1. Brothers take turns going through the whole circuit (about 40 seconds each). 2. They're a team trying to fill the bucket to "the Monster's Number" (for example, 20 treats in 6 trips combined). 3. Clean means done right, not fast. 4. 5yo: short distances, big balls. 7yo: full distances.
  • Silent demo: Walk the circuit once in slow motion, miming each station (hop, field-and-throw, pass, dribble-and-shoot) and dropping a pretend treat in the bucket after each. Hold up the bucket and shake it.
  • Errors & fixes:
  • 5yo: Gets lost between stations. Fix: Put a Halloween decoration or toy at each station. Walk with him the first time.
  • 5yo: Upset when he misses a treat. Fix: It's team scoring: "Your brother's treats count for you too." Give a bonus treat for great effort.
  • 7yo: Rushes and gets sloppy. Fix: "Only clean ones get a treat." Speed doesn't score.
  • 7yo: Criticizes his brother. Fix: Make him the "treat giver" on his brother's trip: he drops in the treats and has to say one nice thing at each station.
  • Make it fun: If they beat the Monster's Number, they keep the candy (or pick a reward). Halloween decorations and spooky music.
  • Why it works: This is interleaved practice: switching between skills from different sports. It feels harder than repeating one skill, but it leads to better long-term learning. Team scoring keeps a 5-vs-7 contest out of it, and running the week's skills in a game-like order shows them how the pieces fit together.

Cool-down: Jack-o'-Lantern Candle (3 min) · Cue: Candle

  • Setup: Sit cross-legged facing each other.
  • Steps: 1. Each boy holds up one finger as the candle inside a jack-o'-lantern. 2. Breathe in slowly through the nose ("smell the pumpkin pie"). 3. Breathe out slowly through the mouth so the candle flickers but doesn't go out: a long, soft breath. 4. 5 slow breaths. 5. Each boy names his best play of the week and the cue word that went with it. 6. Finish with one specific piece of praise from you.
  • Silent demo: Hold up your finger candle, take a big slow sniff, then blow out super gently while wiggling your finger like a flickering flame. Smile.
  • Errors & fixes:
  • 5yo: Blows hard ("I blew it out!"). Fix: Laugh, then "Can you make it flicker, not go out?" Show the soft breath again.
  • 7yo: Can't pick a best play. Fix: Offer two: "The laser to the opposite field, or the pick six?"
  • Make it fun: End by pretending to pinch out each candle with a "tssss" sound.
  • Why it works: A long, slow breath out is the most calming part of breathing. Naming their best play and its cue word is retrieval practice: pulling the memory up is what makes it stick.
