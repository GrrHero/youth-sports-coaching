# Week 3 Home Training Plan: "Catch It, Then Send It"
**Week of Monday, Oct 19, 2026 · For Greg's boys (7 & 5)**

---

## 1. Week Overview

**Theme: Receive and redirect.** Last week was about moving the body well: leaning starts, hip turns, shuffling, cutting and running routes. This week the boys learn to **take the ball in** (ground balls, fly balls, a soccer pass, a handoff) and then **send it somewhere on purpose** (a throw at a target, a shot, a pass, a sprint through the bag). Almost every sport works this way. Catch, control, then act.

**What carries over from Week 2:**

| Week 2 skill | Week 3 next step |
|---|---|
| Belly hip-turn throws + distance ladder | Throwing **at a target** with follow-through; **crow hop** intro for the 7yo |
| Shuffle & snag backhand, mirror shuffle | **Ready position** and **alligator grounders** (field it in front, two hands) |
| Big-to-little ball catching | **Fly balls**: balloon, then beach ball, then tennis ball, getting under it |
| Gate passing, cone slalom, soccer bowling | **First touch** (receiving) into space, **laces shooting**, **1v1 shielding** |
| Laces grip, basket catch, route running | **QB throwing motion**, **handoffs**, **evasion** (no flag-guarding), **backpedal** |
| Turnaround races, freeze cuts | Run **through** first base; **jump-and-stick landings**, **lateral bounds**, **reaction catches** |

**Suggested schedule** (move days around as needed; 20 to 30 minutes each):
- **Mon 10/19:** Session 1, Baseball: Ground Balls & Target Throws
- **Tue 10/20:** Session 2, Soccer: First Touch, Shots & Shielding
- **Thu 10/22:** Session 3, Flag Football: Handoffs, Throws & Evasion
- **Fri 10/23:** Session 4, Athleticism: Jump, Land, Bound, React
- **Sat or Sun 10/24–25:** Session 5, Mixed Backyard Day: Fly Balls, Run-Throughs & the Triple Crown

**Three rules for the whole week:**
1. **Play first, talk second.** Get them moving inside 30 seconds. Say no more than 10 to 15 words before the first rep.
2. **One cue word per activity.** Use only that word while they're moving. Save longer explanations for water breaks, and only if they ask.
3. **Two levels, one activity.** Both boys always do the same game. The 7yo gets a harder version (farther, faster, moving) and the 5yo gets an easier one (closer, slower, bigger ball). Nobody sits out.

**A quick note on the silent demo:** for each activity you'll see how to *show* it without talking. Kids this age copy what they see far better than they follow what they hear. Do the move slowly 2 or 3 times, a bit exaggerated, then once at normal speed. Then say the cue word and point at them. That's the whole instruction.

## 2. Session 1: Baseball: Ground Balls & Target Throws (25 min)

**Equipment:** 2 gloves, 8 to 10 tennis balls or soft-core baseballs, 4 cones (or shoes), a hula hoop or laundry basket, painter's tape or a bedsheet for a fence target.

### Warm-up: Popcorn Ready (5 min) · Cue: **Popcorn**
- **Setup:** Open grass about 15 x 15 steps. Gloves on.
- **Steps:**
  1. Kids jog or skip around the area.
  2. You clap once and call "Popcorn!"
  3. They "pop" into ready position: feet wider than shoulders, knees bent, bottom down like sitting on a low stool, glove open and out front, eyes up.
  4. Hold 2 seconds, then back to jogging. Do 8 to 10 pops.
  5. *7yo add-on:* after he pops, you point left or right and he takes 2 quick shuffles that way and pops again.
- **Silent demo:** Jog in a little circle. Clap your own hands, then drop into a big exaggerated ready position with your glove out. Hold it, grin, pop back up. Do it twice. Then point at them and clap.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Stands straight up with hands at his sides. **Fix:** Hold your hand flat at his belt height and say "Pop your bottom down to my hand." Now he has a physical target to reach.
  - *5yo:* Feet together. **Fix:** Lay two shoes on the grass as "feet spots" a bit wider than his shoulders and have him pop onto the shoes.
  - *7yo:* Bends at the waist with legs straight (back hunched, looks like touching toes). **Fix:** "Can you see my shirt logo?" If his chest drops, he can't. Keeping eyes up keeps the chest up.
  - *7yo:* Shuffles by crossing his feet. **Fix:** Tell him "Never let your shoes kiss." Slow it down to half speed for a few reps.
- **Make it fun:** They're corn kernels in a hot pan. Clap faster for "the pan is heating up!" Sometimes fake a clap (hands stop right before touching). Anyone who doesn't pop on a fake gets a cheer, not an out.
- **Why it works:** The ready position is the base for every fielding play. Turning it into a reaction game gives lots of reps without boredom. The fake clap trains attention and impulse control, a skill in itself at 5. The cue "Popcorn" is an *analogy* (exploding up into a shape), and analogies help young kids learn movements without a list of body-part rules to remember. Researchers call this implicit or analogy learning.

### Skill Work A: Alligator Grounders (7 min) · Cue: **Chomp**
- **Setup:** You kneel 10 to 12 feet away (5yo) or 15 to 20 feet away (7yo) with a pile of balls. The kid is in Popcorn ready position.
- **Steps:**
  1. Roll a slow ground ball straight at him.
  2. His glove goes **down to the grass**, open, fingers touching the ground, out in front of his feet (not under his body).
  3. As the ball rolls into the glove, his throwing hand comes down on top like an alligator's upper jaw: **Chomp!**
  4. He pulls the ball and glove up to his belt buckle together.
  5. He rolls it back to you (5yo) or makes a short throw back (7yo).
  6. 10 to 12 reps each, rotating brothers so one rests while the other goes.
- **Silent demo:** Kneel, roll a ball to yourself off a wall, or have the 7yo roll one to you. Make your glove hand the bottom jaw flat on the grass. Bring your top hand down in a big dramatic clap-chomp and pull it to your belt. Make a chomping face. Do it twice slowly.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Glove is up in the air and the ball rolls under ("through the gate"). **Fix:** Put a little grass or a leaf in his glove and say "Glove eats grass first." Or start with balls placed still on the ground and have him chomp them before you roll anything.
  - *5yo:* Turns his head away or closes his eyes. **Fix:** Use a softer ball (foam or squishy) and roll slower. He can't learn while he's scared, so make it impossible to get hurt.
  - *7yo:* Fields it between his feet or under his body. **Fix:** Put a cone a half-step in front of his toes and say "Chomp it at the cone." This pushes the catch out front where he can see it.
  - *7yo:* One-hands it and drops it. **Fix:** Say nothing about the hands. Award a point only when the top hand chomps. The scoring does the coaching.
- **Make it fun:** He's an alligator in a swamp and the balls are fish. Count "fish eaten" out loud. For the 7yo, a fish that gets past him "swims to the ocean" and you get a point. First to 10.
- **Why it works:** The alligator picture gives one simple idea (bottom jaw down, top jaw closes) instead of five separate rules. Starting slow and straight-on is close to **errorless learning**: early success builds the picture and the confidence, then you add difficulty. Rotating brothers every 3 or 4 reps keeps waiting time short. The resting brother can be the "fish counter."
- 🎥 **Video:** [Alligator hands drill](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jzilP9aE10Y) · *Verified title: "Alligator hands" (Coach DiLena Baseball).* It shows the glove-in-the-dirt and top-hand chomp with little kids.

### Skill Work B: Crow Hop Target Throws (7 min) · Cue: **Scoot**
- **Setup:** A target on a fence or wall: a hula hoop hung up, a bedsheet with a taped circle, or a laundry basket on its side. Throwing lines: about 15 feet (5yo) and 25 to 35 feet (7yo). A pile of tennis balls.
- **Steps (7yo, right-handed; flip for a lefty):**
  1. Start facing the target holding the ball.
  2. Small hop forward. The **right foot lands sideways** (turned 90 degrees, like standing on a skateboard) so his side faces the target. This is the "scoot."
  3. The left foot steps straight at the target.
  4. Throw, and let the throwing hand **finish down by the opposite (left) pocket**.
  5. Keep the hop *small*: a scoot, not a long jump.
- **Steps (5yo):** Same idea, simpler. A **side-step "scoot"** (right foot steps behind or next to the left, sideways), then step at the target and throw. No hop. The follow-through to the pocket matters more than the footwork for him.
- **Silent demo:** Stand sideways, slowly do: little hop, back foot lands sideways (point at your sideways foot), front foot steps at the target, throw, and freeze with your hand by your opposite pocket. Hold the freeze for 2 full seconds so they see the finish. Do it 3 times.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Throws with his feet square and only his arm (a "dart throw"). **Fix:** Lay a jump rope or bat on the ground pointing at the target. He stands sideways along it, "like walking on a balance beam," and steps along the line.
  - *5yo:* Stops his arm at release. **Fix:** Tape a small sticker on his opposite pants pocket: "Your hand has to touch the sticker after you throw."
  - *7yo:* Hop is huge and he falls off balance. **Fix:** "Scoot is a little mouse hop, not a kangaroo." Put two shoes about a foot apart; he hops from one to the other.
  - *7yo:* Back foot lands pointing at the target (no turn), so the hips never load. **Fix:** Draw or tape a line across his path. The back foot must land **on the line**, parallel to it.
- **Make it fun:** Points. Inside the hoop or circle = 3, hits the sheet or fence = 1. Five throws per round. After each round he picks: "Same distance, or back one giant step for double points?" Let the 5yo throw at a closer target with the same scoring so the totals stay close.
- **Why it works:** A target gives an **external focus** (attention on where the ball goes, not on the elbow). That reliably helps both accuracy and how well a kid learns the move. The crow hop is taught **whole-part-whole**: one full throw, then the hop alone between shoes, then the full throw again. The distance choice gives him **autonomy**, which keeps motivation high.
- 🎥 **Video:** [Crow hop for youth players](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=98nYdcKaSsM) · *Verified title: "Youth Baseball Drills: Throw with Crow Hop" (PlaySportsTV).* It's a short youth-focused demo of fielding into a crow hop and throw. Watch it once yourself; it's for you, not the boys.

### Game: Grounder Goalie (5 min) · Cue: **Middle**
- **Setup:** Two cones 4 big steps apart (5yo) or 6 big steps apart (7yo). That's the goal. The kid stands in the middle in ready position. You roll from 12 to 20 feet.
- **Steps:**
  1. You roll grounders, trying to "score" between the cones.
  2. He shuffles to get his **body in the middle** of the ball's path, then Chomps it.
  3. Clean field = point for him. Ball gets through = point for you.
  4. *7yo bonus:* after a clean field, he gets a crow-hop throw at the target for +1.
  5. First to 7. Then switch brothers.
- **Silent demo:** Have one boy roll at you. Take two quick shuffles so the ball comes right at your belly button, chomp it, and hold it up like a trophy. Then point at the spot between your feet and the ball's path to show "middle."
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Reaches with just the glove while his feet stay put. **Fix:** Roll balls *only* one step to his side, slowly, and celebrate when his feet move. Say "Middle!" before you roll, not after he misses.
  - *5yo:* Falls on the ball to stop it. **Fix:** Fine at first. It shows effort. Then: "Can you do it without your knees touching?"
  - *7yo:* Runs straight sideways and gets there late. **Fix:** "Get there first, then chomp." Roll a little slower so he can feel arriving early, then speed back up.
  - *7yo:* Gets mad when you score. **Fix:** Let him set the score cap ("first to how many?"). Lose a few close ones on purpose so the game stays even.
- **Make it fun:** You're the "evil striker" with a dramatic play-by-play voice. He's the world's best goalie. Keep the score loud and exciting.
- **Why it works:** This is a **constraints-led** game. The cones set the task, and the scoring rewards good positioning without a lecture. It's the same skill as the drill but now he has to *read* the ball and decide, which is what the real game asks.

### Cool-down: Glove Boat (3 min) · Cue: **Float**
- **Setup:** Both boys lie on their backs on the grass, gloves resting on their tummies.
- **Steps:**
  1. Slow breath in through the nose: the glove "boat" floats up.
  2. Slow breath out: the boat sinks down.
  3. Five slow breaths.
  4. Then one question each: "What did your alligator do best today?" (Ask before tell: let them name it.)
- **Silent demo:** Lie down next to them with your glove on your stomach and breathe big and slow. Point at your rising glove with wide eyes.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Giggles and breathes fast so the glove bounces. **Fix:** Fine for 1 or 2 breaths. Then whisper "Can you make it float like a sleepy boat?" Slowing your own voice brings his breathing down too.
  - *7yo:* Says "This is boring." **Fix:** Challenge him: "Can you keep the glove on for 5 breaths without touching it?"
- **Make it fun:** Narrate a quiet story: "The boats are floating on a calm lake... a little wave... now still."
- **Why it works:** Slow belly breathing helps kids settle after excitement and gives a clear, calm end to practice. The one-question recap gets them to recall what they learned, which helps it stick.

## 3. Session 2: Soccer: First Touch, Shots & Shielding (26 min)

**Equipment:** 2 soccer balls (size 3), 6 to 8 cones, a small goal (pop-up goal, two buckets, or two shoes), a "treasure" square marked with 4 cones (about 4 x 4 big steps).

### Warm-up: Piano Toe Taps (4 min) · Cue: **Piano**
- **Setup:** Each boy has a ball in front of him.
- **Steps:**
  1. Lightly tap the top of the ball with the bottom of one foot, then the other, back and forth.
  2. Light, quick taps, "like playing piano keys," without moving the ball.
  3. Rounds of 20 seconds, 4 rounds with short rests.
  4. *7yo:* taps while slowly circling the ball, then 10 taps and go forward.
- **Silent demo:** Do it slow, light and bouncy, tapping the ball like a piano key, with fingers pretending to play piano in the air. Speed up for 3 seconds, then smile and point at their balls.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Stomps the ball and it shoots away. **Fix:** "Quiet piano." He should barely touch it. If balance is the problem, let him hold your hand.
  - *5yo:* Uses only his good foot. **Fix:** Count only the alternating taps: "Left–right is one point."
  - *7yo:* Leans back and stands flat-footed. **Fix:** "Stay on your tiptoes, light as a cat." Tap your own toes with a finger.
  - *7yo:* Gets sloppy going fast. **Fix:** Count only "clean" taps where the ball stays still.
- **Make it fun:** Count taps out loud. Beat-your-record each round. Play music and tap to the beat.
- **Why it works:** Lots of quick, light touches build foot-to-ball feel (soccer coaches call it "ball mastery"). The record-beating game gives each boy his own goal, so the 5yo isn't racing his brother.

### Skill Work A: Pillow First Touch (6 min) · Cue: **Pillow**
- **Setup:** You 4 to 5 steps (5yo) or 6 to 8 steps (7yo) in front of the kid. Behind him, two "gates" made of 2 cones each, one to his left and one to his right, about 2 steps away.
- **Steps:**
  1. You pass (roll) the ball to him.
  2. He meets it with the **inside of his foot**, ankle firm, foot just off the ground.
  3. As the ball arrives, he gives his foot back a little, "like catching an egg on a pillow," so the ball stops or slows close to him.
  4. *5yo goal:* stop the ball within one step of his body.
  5. *7yo goal:* you call "Left!" or "Right!" while the ball is rolling, and his first touch pushes it **through that gate**.
  6. 10 to 15 reps per boy, then switch feet.
- **Silent demo:** Have a boy roll the ball to you. Show a stiff "wall" foot first: the ball bounces away and you make a sad face. Then a soft pillow foot: the ball dies at your feet and you make a happy face. Both versions, twice. The contrast teaches it.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Kicks at the ball as it comes, so it flies back. **Fix:** Use slower rolls. "Make it sleep" means the ball should stop and stay. Let him stop it with the sole of his foot at first. That's a legit first touch too.
  - *5yo:* Waits flat-footed and the ball goes under his foot. **Fix:** "Meet it." Step toward the ball as it comes, and walk forward with him a few times.
  - *7yo:* Stops it dead under his feet every time, so it's stuck. **Fix:** That's why we have gates. "First touch goes somewhere." Praise touches that go into space.
  - *7yo:* Watches only the ball and misses your call. **Fix:** Hold up a hand signal (left or right arm) instead of calling. Now he has to glance up, which is a real game skill.
- **Make it fun:** The ball is a "wild egg." If it bounces more than 2 steps away, it "cracked." Count unbroken eggs. For the 7yo, a gate touch scores 2.
- **Why it works:** Showing the wrong way and the right way side by side (**contrast**) is one of the fastest ways to show kids what "soft" feels like. The gates are a **constraint**: they force a directional first touch without you explaining it. The 7yo's late call adds a quick decision, so he's learning to think while he moves.
- 🎥 **Video:** [First touch into space](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cvJjbP80cR0) · *Verified title: "Passing Drill for Youth Players: First Touch into Space" (Showtime Soccer Training).* It shows taking the first touch away from pressure, a good model for the 7yo version.

### Skill Work B: Shoelace Shots (6 min) · Cue: **Shoelaces**
- **Setup:** A goal (pop-up net, two buckets, or a fence section). A ball placed still about 6 steps out (5yo) or 8 to 10 steps out (7yo). Line up 4 to 5 balls.
- **Steps:**
  1. Short run-up from slightly to the side (not straight behind the ball).
  2. Non-kicking foot lands **beside** the ball, pointing at the goal.
  3. Kicking foot: **toe pointed down**, ankle stiff, and he strikes the middle of the ball with his **shoelaces**.
  4. Follow through toward the goal and land on the kicking foot.
  5. *7yo:* once he's hitting it clean, add a Pillow touch first: you pass, he touches into space, then shoots.
- **Silent demo:** Point at your own shoelaces, then point at the ball. Point your toe down hard and wiggle it to show it's locked. Walk through the kick in slow motion, then do one at full speed into the goal and celebrate.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Toe-pokes (kicks with his big toe). **Fix:** Put a small sticker or a bit of tape on his shoelaces: "The sticker has to touch the ball." That gives him an outside target instead of a body rule.
  - *5yo:* Swings and misses or tops the ball. **Fix:** Shorten the run-up to 1 or 2 steps. Start with a still ball every time. Big ball (size 4) if you have one.
  - *7yo:* Leans way back and the ball flies over the goal. **Fix:** "Chest over the ball, like you're peeking at it." Put a cone behind the goal as a "no sky-balls" marker: over the cone = zero points.
  - *7yo:* Stands right behind the ball and pokes it with his toe. **Fix:** Start his run-up from a cone at a slight angle (about 45°). The angle makes a laces strike natural.
- **Make it fun:** Target zones. Hang shirts or towels in the corners of the goal: corner = 3 points, anywhere in = 1. Let them name their shot ("The Thunder Rocket!").
- **Why it works:** Putting attention on the shoelaces meeting the ball is an **external focus**, which is better than "point your toe, lock your ankle." The sticker trick turns it into a game. Starting with a still ball, then adding a first touch (for the 7yo) is a simple **part-to-whole progression**.
- 🎥 **Video:** [Kids shooting basics](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AwbuY5mAq-g) · *Verified title: "Kids soccer SHOOTING basics ⚽️" (Prime Coaching Sport).* Short, kid-level shooting technique.

### Game: Treasure Keeper (7 min) · Cue: **Shell**
- **Setup:** The 4 x 4 step cone square. One ball, which is the treasure. One "keeper" (with the ball) and one "pirate" (you, at first).
- **Steps:**
  1. The keeper has the ball inside the square. The pirate tries to steal it or knock it out.
  2. The keeper **turns his back or side to the pirate**, knees bent, arms out a little for balance (no pushing), ball on the foot **farthest from the pirate**, and uses the sole of his foot to roll the ball away from the pirate.
  3. He's the turtle and his body is the shell. The ball is hidden underneath.
  4. Keep it for 10 seconds (5yo) or 15 seconds (7yo) = treasure saved, 1 point.
  5. Rounds of 3 to 4, then rest.
  6. *Brother vs. brother:* when the 7yo is the pirate, he must keep his hands behind his back and can only *walk*. When the 5yo is the pirate, he can run.
- **Silent demo:** You keep the ball and have the 7yo be the pirate. Turn your back to him, bend your knees, put one arm out, and roll the ball away with your sole each time he reaches. Look over your shoulder at him (show that you watch the pirate). Do it slowly with a big grin.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Faces the pirate with the ball in front, which is an easy steal. **Fix:** Turn him gently by the shoulders: "Shell toward the pirate." Play in "slow-motion pirate" mode so he has time to turn.
  - *5yo:* Picks up the ball with his hands. **Fix:** Laugh, then "Turtles don't have hands!" Restart.
  - *7yo:* Stands straight up, so he's easy to bump off the ball. **Fix:** "Low turtle. Sit in a chair." Lower in the knees means harder to move.
  - *7yo:* Pushes with his arm or grabs. **Fix:** Arm out for *feeling* where the pirate is, not pushing. If he pushes, the pirate gets the treasure. Quick, no lecture.
- **Make it fun:** Pirate voice ("Arrrr, give me yer treasure!"). Count the 10 seconds out loud like a countdown. Let the 7yo be the pirate against you, so he gets to "attack" too.
- **Why it works:** Shielding is a whole-body skill that's hard to explain and easy to *feel* under gentle pressure. Starting with you as a slow pirate gives a **just-right challenge**. Rules like "the 7yo walks with hands behind his back" make the brother matchup fair, and that kind of rule is how you'll balance mixed skill levels with your group at the fields too.

### Cool-down: Sleepy Sole Rolls (3 min) · Cue: **Sleepy**
- **Setup:** Standing next to a fence or holding your hand, ball under one foot.
- **Steps:**
  1. Slowly roll the ball forward and back under the sole, then side to side, then in little circles. 30 seconds each foot.
  2. Sit down and roll the ball slowly down each leg like a rolling pin.
  3. Finish: "Which one was harder today, Pillow, Shoelaces, or Shell?"
- **Silent demo:** Yawn dramatically, then roll the ball super slowly under your foot with your eyes half closed.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Rolls it fast and loses it. **Fix:** "Sleepy speed, slower than a snail." Do it right beside him.
  - *7yo:* Turns it into a trick contest. **Fix:** Fine for 10 seconds. Then "Now the slowest roll wins."
- **Make it fun:** The ball is falling asleep. Whoever rolls it the slowest without stopping "tucks it in" best.
- **Why it works:** It keeps light contact with the ball while heart rates come down, and the question at the end gets them reflecting in their own words.

## 4. Session 3: Flag Football: Handoffs, Throws & Evasion (27 min)

**Equipment:** Flag belts (or rags tucked in waistbands), 1 junior or mini football (a foam football for the 5yo is ideal), 6 cones, a laundry basket or trash can for a throwing target.

> **Rule note for you:** In flag football, **flag guarding** (covering or swatting at your flags with your hands, or tucking the belt in) is a penalty. Teach the boys to escape with their **feet**, never their hands. That's what this session's evasion game trains.

### Warm-up: Moonwalk Backpedal (5 min) · Cue: **Moonwalk**
- **Setup:** Two cones about 10 steps apart in a straight line. Kids start at one cone facing you; you stand about 5 steps away.
- **Steps:**
  1. Ready stance: knees bent, chest slightly forward (over the toes, not leaning back), arms bent.
  2. As you walk toward them, they backpedal with **small, quick steps**, keeping the same gap between you.
  3. When you clap, they stop, plant, and sprint forward past you to the start cone.
  4. 5 or 6 reps.
  5. *7yo:* at the clap you point left or right, and he turns his hips that way and runs (like a defender turning to chase a receiver).
- **Silent demo:** Backpedal slowly in a crouch with tiny, quick steps and your chest forward. Then show the wrong way: lean back, big steps, wobble and nearly fall (comically). Then the right way again. Clap, then sprint forward.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Turns around and runs forward instead of backward. **Fix:** Walk it first: "Moonwalk walking" at walking speed, holding his hands as you walk forward together.
  - *5yo:* Trips over his heels. **Fix:** Shorter distance (5 steps) on soft grass. "Tiny mouse steps."
  - *7yo:* Leans back and steps big, so he's slow to stop. **Fix:** "Nose over your toes." Have him carry a ball out in front with both hands. It naturally pulls his chest forward.
  - *7yo:* Pops straight up when turning. **Fix:** "Stay short like you're under a table." Have him touch the grass on the turn.
- **Make it fun:** You're a robot "walking toward them," and they have to keep their "force field" gap. If you catch up and tap their shoulder, they owe you a silly dance.
- **Why it works:** The backpedal is the base of defensive football, and it's also great general coordination (moving backward is a weak spot for most young kids). Using you as a moving reference point is a natural **constraint**. They have to adjust their speed to match you, not to a rule.

### Skill Work A: Window Handoffs (7 min) · Cue: **Window**
- **Setup:** A cone for the quarterback (QB) and a cone 3 steps to the side and 1 step behind it for the running back (RB). Another cone 10 steps ahead is the "end zone."
- **Steps:**
  1. You start as QB with the ball. The kid is the RB.
  2. On "Hut!", the RB jogs forward past you.
  3. The RB makes a **window**: the **arm closest to the QB goes up** (elbow up at chest height), the other arm goes underneath across the belly. The gap between them is the window.
  4. The QB *places* the ball into the window. The RB doesn't grab.
  5. The RB clamps both arms around the ball and runs to the end zone.
  6. 6 to 8 reps, then the kid becomes QB and hands off to you or his brother.
  7. *5yo:* walking speed. *7yo:* jogging, then running, then you call "Left!" or "Right!" for which side he runs past.
- **Silent demo:** Make a big exaggerated window with your arms: near elbow up, other arm below. Look through it at them like a picture frame. Then have the 7yo slowly place a ball into your window and clamp down with a big "click."
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Holds his hands out to grab, so the ball bounces off. **Fix:** "No hands, just a window." Have him stand still with the window first, and you place the ball in 3 times. Then walk.
  - *5yo:* The near elbow drops and blocks the ball. **Fix:** Show both: elbow down, the ball hits his arm, "Oops!" Elbow up, the ball slides in, "Yes!" This exact problem is shown in the video below.
  - *7yo:* Looks at the ball instead of upfield. **Fix:** "Eyes on the end zone. The QB's job is to find your window." Put a funny object (stuffed animal) in the end zone that he has to look at.
  - *7yo (as QB):* Tosses the ball at the RB. **Fix:** "Place it like it's a birthday cake." Two hands, deliver it, then step away.
- **Make it fun:** Count clean handoffs in a row. Five in a row = "Super Bowl handoff" and he gets to spike the ball (once!). Let them call their own play names.
- **Why it works:** The "window" picture gives one clear shape to make, again **analogy learning**. Starting still, then walking, then jogging is a gradual build-up that keeps errors small. Swapping roles (the kid as QB) helps him understand the skill from both sides.
- 🎥 **Video:** [MOJO handoff drill](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pemHnTPHbUA) · *Verified title: "The Handoff | Fun Flag Football Drills by MOJO" (yougotmojo).* It shows the elbow-up window and the QB placing the ball. Exactly this drill.

### Skill Work B: Phone Call Throws (6 min) · Cue: **Phone**
- **Setup:** A laundry basket or trash can on its side as a target. 5yo at 3 to 4 steps; 7yo at 6 to 8 steps. Mini or foam football.
- **Steps:**
  1. Use the grip from last week (fingers on the laces).
  2. Bring the ball up **beside the ear like holding a phone**, elbow up at shoulder height.
  3. Front shoulder points at the target (stand sideways).
  4. Step toward the target with the opposite foot (right-handed = left foot).
  5. Throw and finish with the thumb pointing down and the hand by the opposite hip.
  6. Progression: **half-kneel** (throwing-side knee down) for 5 throws, then standing for 5, then a moving target (brother walking slowly across) for the 7yo.
- **Silent demo:** Hold the ball up to your ear and pretend to talk on it ("Hello?" with just your mouth, no sound). Then point your front shoulder, step, throw into the basket, and freeze with your thumb down. Do the "phone call" pose 2 or 3 times so they copy the start position.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Pushes the ball from his chest like a basketball pass. **Fix:** "Phone by your ear!" Start every throw from the phone pose, and you can gently lift his elbow.
  - *5yo:* Throws with his same-side foot forward. **Fix:** Work from the half-kneel position; it takes the feet out of it. Add the step later.
  - *7yo:* Elbow drops below the shoulder, so the ball wobbles and dies. **Fix:** "Elbow higher than the phone's antenna," or have him throw over a low obstacle (a bench or chair) in front of him.
  - *7yo:* Throws to where the moving brother *is*, not where he's going. **Fix:** "Throw to where he'll be" and put a cone where the brother is heading. He throws to the cone.
- **Make it fun:** Basket = touchdown (6 points). Hits the basket = field goal (3). Do a phone-call celebration after every touchdown ("Hello, Mom? I scored!").
- **Why it works:** The phone picture fixes the most common youth throwing error (pushing from the chest) without technical language. The half-kneel removes the footwork so the arm action can be learned first. That's a **part-practice** step, and then you go straight back to the whole throw. The target keeps attention outside the body.

### Game: Mr. Wolf Flag Escape (6 min) · Cue: **Zigzag**
- **Setup:** A "home" line (2 cones) and a wolf's den about 15 to 20 steps away. Kids wear flags. You're the wolf, standing at the den with your back to them.
- **Steps:**
  1. Kids call, "What time is it, Mr. Wolf?"
  2. You answer "3 o'clock!" and they take 3 steps toward you.
  3. Repeat until you shout **"Dinner time!"** Then you turn and chase.
  4. They run back to home and **zigzag** (sharp changes of direction, using last week's cuts) to keep you from pulling a flag. Hands stay away from the flags.
  5. Safe at home = 1 point. Flag pulled = he becomes your wolf helper next round.
  6. *5yo:* you're "Grandpa Wolf" (slower and dramatic) when chasing him. *7yo:* real effort, and after a few rounds he gets to be the wolf.
- **Silent demo:** Run a zigzag path across the yard with big, exaggerated cuts. Then wave your hands over your hips and shake your head "no" (no guarding). Point to your feet and nod "yes."
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Covers his flags with his hands. **Fix:** "Wolf rule: if hands touch flags, the wolf gets a free snack!" Make running and dodging the fun part.
  - *5yo:* Freezes when you chase. **Fix:** Chase slower, roar but don't reach right away. Let him escape the first few times so he learns that running works.
  - *7yo:* Runs in a straight line and gets caught. **Fix:** Ask before tell: "What could you do to make the wolf miss?" He'll usually say "turn" or "dodge." Then: "Show me."
  - *7yo:* Cuts by rounding off slowly. **Fix:** Remind him of last week's cutting: short steps before the cut, then push off hard.
- **Make it fun:** Huge wolf voice. The wolf is always "SO hungry." Let them choose the time ("13 o'clock!" is fine).
- **Why it works:** The steps toward the wolf build suspense, and the sudden chase is a natural **reaction trigger**. Evasion is learned **implicitly**: the boys figure out what works against a real chaser, rather than practicing a rehearsed juke. The "no hands" rule builds the right habit from day one.

### Cool-down: Starfish & Seashell (3 min) · Cue: **Starfish**
- **Setup:** Lie on the grass.
- **Steps:**
  1. **Starfish:** arms and legs stretched wide, big breath in (count 4).
  2. **Seashell:** curl into a small ball hugging the knees, breathe out (count 4).
  3. 4 to 5 rounds, then lie still for 3 breaths.
  4. Question: "When you were the wolf/running back, what was the trickiest part?"
- **Silent demo:** Spread out like a big starfish with a huge breath in. Curl tight into a ball with a slow breath out. Peek out from your shell.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Rolls around giggling. **Fix:** Fine. Join him for one round, then get slower and quieter. He'll follow your pace.
  - *7yo:* Rushes it. **Fix:** "Can you make your breath out last as long as my count?" Count slowly to 5.
- **Make it fun:** Ocean story: "A wave washes over the starfish... the seashell closes..."
- **Why it works:** Alternating stretch and curl is a gentle full-body stretch plus breath control, and it marks a clear end to the higher-energy chase game.

## 5. Session 4: Athleticism: Jump, Land, Bound, React (25 min)

**Equipment:** A jump rope or garden hose (for a line on the ground), 4 cones, 2 tennis balls, a broom and 2 chairs (for a crawl tunnel), a low step (curb or porch step, around 6 inches). Flat soft grass is a must for jumping.

### Warm-up: Puppet Skips (5 min) · Cue: **Puppet**
- **Setup:** Two cones 15 to 20 steps apart. For the 7yo, a "reach target" hanging up: a ribbon on a tree branch or your hand held up high halfway along.
- **Steps:**
  1. Skip between the cones (step–hop, step–hop).
  2. On each hop, drive the knee up and swing the **opposite arm up high**, as if a string pulls the knee and hand up together like a puppet.
  3. Go for *height*, not speed.
  4. 4 to 6 trips.
  5. *7yo:* on the middle skips, reach up to touch the ribbon or your hand.
- **Silent demo:** Do one normal low skip, then a big bouncy puppet skip: pretend to grab an invisible string above your head and "pull" your knee and opposite hand up. Exaggerate the height.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Can't skip yet and gallops instead (very normal at 5; skipping often clicks around 5 to 6). **Fix:** Let him gallop. Then say "Step–hop, step–hop" slowly while holding his hand and skipping with him. No pressure.
  - *5yo:* Same arm and same leg go up together. **Fix:** Don't correct it much yet. Focus on bouncing. The arm-leg pattern usually sorts itself out with practice.
  - *7yo:* Skips fast and low. **Fix:** "Skip so high you could see over a car." Add the ribbon to reach for. A target fixes it.
  - *7yo:* Arms flop at his sides. **Fix:** "The string pulls your hand to the sky." Make the reach target slightly higher than he can comfortably touch.
- **Make it fun:** You're the puppet master pulling invisible strings. When you "drop" the strings, they flop down like sleeping puppets, then pop back up for the next trip.
- **Why it works:** Skipping for height builds elasticity, rhythm and opposite-arm-leg coordination, which are the building blocks of sprinting and jumping. The reach target gives an **external focus**. For the 5yo, letting him gallop until skipping comes is **developmentally appropriate**. Forcing a skill before he's ready just frustrates him.

### Skill Work A: Whisper Landings (6 min) · Cue: **Whisper**
- **Setup:** The rope or hose as a line on flat grass. For the 5yo, the low step.
- **Steps:**
  1. Feet hip-width apart. Small dip, swing the arms and jump forward over the line.
  2. Land **as quietly as possible**, "a whisper, not a shout": on the balls of the feet rolling to the whole foot, knees bent and pointing over the toes, hips back, chest up, arms forward.
  3. **Freeze 3 seconds.**
  4. 2 rounds of 5 jumps with a rest between. Keep it low volume; quality over quantity.
  5. *5yo:* step or jump off the low step and land in a whisper freeze. Then jump over the line on the grass.
  6. *7yo:* forward jumps for distance with a stick, then a "quarter turn" jump (jump and turn to face the side) and stick.
- **Silent demo:** Do one loud, stiff-legged "THUD" landing and make a pained face holding your knees. Then do a silent, soft one and freeze, with a finger to your lips ("shh"). Repeat the quiet one twice.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Lands stiff-legged and bounces or tips over. **Fix:** "Land like a cat sneaking up on a mouse." Go back to stepping off the low step until his landings are quiet.
  - *5yo:* Can't hold the freeze and keeps hopping. **Fix:** Count the 3 seconds out loud together. Accept 1 or 2 seconds at first.
  - *7yo:* **Knees cave inward** on the landing (knock-kneed). This is the most important one to fix. **Fix:** "Knees point at your shoelaces," or put a small ball or stuffed toy between his knees that should *not* get squished. Shorter jumps until the knees stay out.
  - *7yo:* Lands on his heels. **Fix:** "Whisper means no heel sound." Have him jump onto a towel and try not to make it slide.
- **Make it fun:** You close your eyes and judge the loudness. Silent landing = 3 points, small sound = 1, THUD = 0. Each boy tries to "fool" your ears.
- **Why it works:** Learning to land *before* adding more jumping is good long-term athletic development and reduces knee strain later. The "quiet" target is an **external focus** that naturally creates soft, bent-knee landings without a list of joint instructions. The eyes-closed scoring makes it a game they can't fake.
- 🎥 **Video:** [Jumping and landing for kids](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=60N3vdH6NgI) · *Verified title: "Elementary physical education and assessment- jumping and landing" (Dr. Jordan Weber).* A PE-teacher view of what good jumping and landing look like at elementary age.

### Skill Work B: Skater Bounds (5 min) · Cue: **Skater**
- **Setup:** The same rope or hose line.
- **Steps:**
  1. **7yo:** Stand on one foot beside the line. Bound sideways over it and land on the *other* foot, knee bent, hips back, arms swinging across like a speed skater. Freeze 2 seconds. Bound back. 2 rounds of 6 (3 each leg).
  2. Progression for the 7yo: 3 bounds in a row with a stick on the last one.
  3. **5yo:** Two-foot side jumps over the line with a whisper freeze. If that's easy, try one foot to one foot with your hand for balance.
- **Silent demo:** Crouch slightly, swing your arms like a speed skater, then bound sideways and stick on one foot, holding still with arms out. Wobble a bit on purpose, then steady and smile.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Loses balance and puts the other foot down. **Fix:** Totally fine. He's 5. Hold his hand on the landing side, or let him do two-foot jumps.
  - *5yo:* Jumps forward instead of sideways. **Fix:** Have him face you while you stand at the end of the line. He jumps "past" you sideways and keeps looking at you.
  - *7yo:* Landing knee caves in. **Fix:** Same as Whisper: "Knee over your laces." Shorter bounds.
  - *7yo:* Lands upright and stiff. **Fix:** "Land like you're about to sit on a stool." Hold your hand out as the "stool" at hip height.
- **Make it fun:** They're Olympic speed skaters. Commentate like the Winter Olympics. Each clean stick = a "gold medal skate."
- **Why it works:** Sideways single-leg bounds build the balance and push-off that fielding, cutting and dodging all rely on. Differences between kids are expected. **Scaling the task** (two feet for the 5yo, one foot for the 7yo) keeps both of them working at their edge without failing.

### Game: Pounce (6 min) · Cue: **Pounce**
- **Setup:** You stand holding a tennis ball in each hand, arms out at shoulder height. The kid stands in ready position 3 to 4 steps away (5yo) or 5 to 6 steps away (7yo).
- **Steps:**
  1. You drop one ball (no warning).
  2. He **pounces**: reacts, runs in and catches it **after one bounce**.
  3. Caught = 1 point. Two bounces = 0, try again.
  4. 5 catches in a row = he steps back one step for the next round.
  5. *5yo:* only one ball (no choice), and you give a little nod before dropping. *7yo:* two balls, and you drop either. Later, he starts facing sideways.
- **Silent demo:** Have the 7yo hold the balls. You get in ready position, stare at his hands, then explode forward and catch one after a bounce. Freeze in a "pounce" pose like a cat.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Starts flat-footed and late. **Fix:** "Popcorn feet first." Remind him of Session 1's ready position. Move him closer so he succeeds.
  - *5yo:* Tries to catch it in the air before it bounces. **Fix:** Fine, but "Let it bounce once, then catch." Use a bouncier ball so the bounce is higher and easier.
  - *7yo:* Guesses and runs before the drop. **Fix:** That's a "false start" (no point). Hold the balls for different lengths of time so he can't guess the rhythm.
  - *7yo:* Gets there but stands up to catch, so it bounces past. **Fix:** "Stay low when you pounce, hands near the grass."
- **Make it fun:** He's a cheetah and the balls are rabbits. Keep a "cheetah record" chart on the fridge. The step-back challenge makes every round feel like leveling up.
- **Why it works:** This trains **reaction time, first-step quickness and hand-eye tracking**, the same things fielding a grounder needs. The adjustable distance keeps the success rate high but not too high (around 60 to 80% success is a good learning zone). The unpredictable drop forces real reading of the cue rather than guessing.

### Cool-down: Sloth Crawl Tunnel (3 min) · Cue: **Sloth**
- **Setup:** A broom resting across 2 chairs (about 2 feet high) as a "tunnel." A few couch cushions can extend it.
- **Steps:**
  1. Crawl through the tunnel *in slow motion* on hands and knees, opposite hand and knee moving together (right hand with left knee, left hand with right knee).
  2. 3 to 4 slow trips.
  3. Then lie on your back and hug your knees into your chest for 3 slow breaths, and lie in a long stretch for 3 more.
  4. Question: "Which jump felt the quietest today?"
- **Silent demo:** Crawl through slowly with a sleepy sloth face, deliberately moving opposite hand and knee. Point to your moving hand and opposite knee.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Crawls fast, same-side hand and knee (ok at first). **Fix:** "Sloth speed." Go slowly beside him and tap the hand and knee that should move together.
  - *7yo:* Makes it a race. **Fix:** Reverse it: "Slowest sloth wins, but you can't stop."
- **Make it fun:** They're sloths crawling along a branch, and the floor is "hot lava jungle." Make slow sloth noises.
- **Why it works:** Opposite-arm-opposite-leg crawling builds coordination between both sides of the body (the same pattern as running). Doing it slowly brings the heart rate down while still moving, which is an easy cool-down kids don't fight.

## 6. Session 5: Mixed Backyard Day: Fly Balls, Run-Throughs & the Triple Crown (28 min)

**Equipment:** A balloon, a beach ball (or large playground ball), soft tennis balls, gloves, a base (or a towel or cardboard), a streamer or toilet paper strip, a laundry basket, 4 to 6 cones.

### Warm-up: Orbit Ball Wraps (4 min) · Cue: **Orbit**
- **Setup:** Each boy holds a ball. 5yo: a big playground or soccer ball. 7yo: a tennis ball.
- **Steps:**
  1. Pass the ball around your waist hand to hand, "orbiting" like a moon, 5 times each direction.
  2. Around the knees, 5 times. Around the head, 5 times.
  3. *7yo:* figure-8 through and around the legs, then toss it up, clap once, catch. Then try two claps.
  4. *5yo:* waist and knees only, then toss to himself and catch with two hands.
- **Silent demo:** Slowly orbit the ball around your waist with your head following it like a planet watcher, then around your head with a "whoa" face. End with a toss, clap, catch.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Drops it on the hand switch behind his back. **Fix:** Use a bigger ball and slow it down. Bring his hands closer together behind his back by bending forward slightly.
  - *5yo:* Gets frustrated with drops. **Fix:** Count catches, not drops. "Dropping means you're learning; pick it up and keep orbiting."
  - *7yo:* Rushes and the figure-8 falls apart. **Fix:** "Slow orbit first, then speed." Count 5 clean before he can go fast.
  - *7yo:* Tosses too high on the clap catch. **Fix:** "Toss to just above your head." Let him pick the height. Lower is easier.
- **Make it fun:** They're planets and the ball is their moon. "Moon speed" changes: slow, medium, rocket. Then a "two-clap challenge" record.
- **Why it works:** Ball-handling around the body builds hand coordination and body awareness (knowing where your hands are without looking). Moving from a big ball to a small ball is a simple **progression from easy to hard** that matches each boy's level.

### Skill Work A: Umbrella Fly Balls (8 min) · Cue: **Umbrella**
- **Setup:** Open grass. Balloon, beach ball, soft tennis balls. Gloves for the tennis ball stage only.
- **Steps:**
  1. **Stage 1 (Balloon):** Toss a balloon high. They move to get **under** it and catch it with both hands **above their eyes and slightly in front of the forehead**.
  2. **Stage 2 (Beach ball):** Toss it high. Same: get under it, hands up like an umbrella, catch above the forehead.
  3. **Stage 3 (Tennis ball):** 7yo with glove, 5yo bare hands with a soft ball. Underhand toss up higher, starting right in front of him, then 2 to 3 steps away so he has to move.
  4. Call "**Mine!**" before every catch (a habit for team play).
  5. Glove up, throwing hand next to it to cover the ball after the catch.
- **Silent demo:** Toss a balloon to yourself, shuffle under it while pointing at it, then hold your hands up open above your head like an umbrella and catch it with a little umbrella "pop" gesture. Mouth "Mine!" Repeat with the beach ball.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Holds his hands at belly level and the ball hits his face or chest. **Fix:** Stay on the balloon or beach ball longer. Start each rep with his hands *already up* as an umbrella. This builds the picture before speed comes in.
  - *5yo:* Turns his head away from the ball. **Fix:** Use only soft balls (foam or balloons). Never a hard ball overhead at this age. Toss it lower.
  - *7yo:* Runs with his glove up the whole way, which is slow. **Fix:** "Run with your arms, then open the umbrella when you get there."
  - *7yo:* Catches below his chest, basket style, too often. **Fix:** "Catch it where your umbrella shades your eyes." Basket catches are fine low; high balls need the umbrella.
  - *7yo:* Misjudges and the ball sails over him. **Fix:** "First step back, then come in." Being slightly too deep is easier to fix than being too shallow.
- **Make it fun:** "Rainstorm!" You toss several balloons or soft balls one after another and they try to keep "dry" by catching every drop. Count catches.
- **Why it works:** Balloons and beach balls slow the ball down so the brain can learn to track and get under it. That's **errorless learning**: getting the pattern right before adding the speed and fear of a smaller ball. The umbrella picture tells them both *where* to be (under it) and *where* to catch (above the eyes). Calling "Mine!" teaches communication without a separate lesson.
- 🎥 **Video:** [Teaching kids fly balls](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_QC-pBhoZWg) · *Verified title: "How to Teach Kids How to Catch Fly Balls" (Coach Dan Blewett).* A coach's guide for parents on teaching young kids fly balls.

### Skill Work B: Tape Run-Throughs (5 min) · Cue: **Tape**
- **Setup:** A home spot and a "first base" 30 to 40 feet away (5yo) or 45 to 55 feet away (7yo). Hold or tie a streamer or toilet paper strip as "finish tape" **4 big steps past first base**.
- **Steps:**
  1. Start in a batting stance at home. Swing a pretend bat, drop it gently (no throwing bats!), and sprint.
  2. Step on the **front edge of the base** without slowing down.
  3. Keep sprinting through the finish **tape** past the base.
  4. After the tape, slow down, glance right (to see if the ball got away) and walk back in foul territory.
  5. 4 to 6 reps.
  6. *7yo progression (rounding first):* when you yell "Gap!" during his run, he curves his path a little to the right before the base (a banana shape), touches the inside corner of the bag and heads toward second for 3 steps, then looks to you.
- **Silent demo:** Sprint from home, step on the front edge of the base, and burst through the tape with arms up like winning a race. Then walk back and point at the tape: *that's* the finish line, not the base.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Stops right on the base, or jumps onto it. **Fix:** Move the tape a bit farther and make breaking the tape the goal. Cheer the tape, not the base.
  - *5yo:* Runs toward the pitcher or wanders. **Fix:** Lay a line of cones down the baseline and say "Run the cone road."
  - *7yo:* Slows down a few steps before the base. **Fix:** Time him with a phone from home to the tape. "Slowing down adds time." Kids love beating a time.
  - *7yo:* On rounding, takes a wide loop. **Fix:** Put a cone where the banana curve should start (about two-thirds of the way down the line). "Bend at the cone."
- **Make it fun:** Olympic finish-line race. Commentate: "...and he BREAKS THE TAPE!" Time the 7yo and chart his best time. The 5yo races the tape, which you can hold and "pull away" playfully.
- **Why it works:** Kids slow down at a base because the base looks like the finish. Moving the finish line past the base (**a constraint on the environment**) fixes it in a few reps without explanation. It also builds on Week 1's "run to first."
- 🎥 **Video:** [Running through first](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b0xQ3p4L6sU) · *Verified title: "Little League Tee Ball Baserunning Drill. Helping tee ball players to run through first base." (Schupak Sports).* It's a tee-ball drill aimed at exactly this skill.

### Game: The Backyard Triple Crown (8 min) · Cue: **Next**
- **Setup:** A circuit:
  1. **Grounder station:** You roll a grounder. Kid Chomps it.
  2. **Throw station:** From where he fields it, he throws (Scoot) into the laundry basket.
  3. **Run station:** He sprints from home through first base and the Tape.
  4. **Fly ball station:** You toss a soft fly ball (Umbrella) as he comes back; he catches it.
  - Scoring: 1 point for each station done well (4 points max per trip).
- **Steps:**
  1. Brothers take turns. Each trip takes about 30 to 40 seconds.
  2. They're a **team** against "Dad's Number." Set a target total (e.g., 20 points in 6 trips combined).
  3. "Next!" is the call to move to the next station without stopping.
  4. 5yo: shorter distances, bigger ball, beach ball on the fly station. 7yo: full distances, tennis ball.
- **Silent demo:** Walk the whole circuit once in slow motion, doing each move in a small pantomime (chomp, scoot throw, run, umbrella) and pointing to the next station each time. Finish with arms raised.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Forgets the order and wanders. **Fix:** Number the stations with cones (1, 2, 3, 4) or put a toy at each one. Walk with him the first time.
  - *5yo:* Gets upset if he misses a station. **Fix:** It's team scoring, so his brother's points help. Celebrate his effort out loud. Points are only one part.
  - *7yo:* Rushes and gets sloppy (no chomp, wild throws). **Fix:** "Rushed points don't count." Only clean reps score. Speed doesn't matter.
  - *7yo:* Bosses his brother. **Fix:** Give him the official "scorekeeper" job for his brother's turn: he can *only* say good things.
- **Make it fun:** Give it a big "championship" feel: announce each player by name, make a crown out of paper for the end. If they beat Dad's Number, they get to pick next week's warm-up game.
- **Why it works:** This is **whole practice**: the skills from the whole week chained together in a game-like order, which is how they show up in real baseball. Team scoring against a target number avoids a 7-vs-5 contest the younger one will always lose, which keeps the motivation **cooperative**. Mixing different skills in one game (**variable practice**) feels harder now but makes learning stick better later.

### Cool-down: Highlight Replay (3 min) · Cue: **Replay**
- **Setup:** Lie on the grass with eyes closed.
- **Steps:**
  1. "Picture your best play of the week in slow motion, like a TV replay."
  2. 3 slow breaths while they "watch" it.
  3. Each one says his play out loud and one cue word that went with it ("I Chomped a grounder!").
  4. Finish with a high five and one sentence of specific praise from you ("You stayed low on every grounder today").
- **Silent demo:** Lie down, close your eyes, then mime a slow-motion catch with your arms while smiling, then open your eyes and give a thumbs up.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Can't think of one. **Fix:** Offer two choices: "Was it your umbrella catch or breaking the tape?"
  - *7yo:* Picks only something he failed at. **Fix:** "Tell me one great one first, then one you want to fix next week."
- **Make it fun:** Do it like an announcer: "And here's the replay... he's under it... CAUGHT IT!"
- **Why it works:** Picturing the movement (**mental rehearsal**) and recalling the cue word strengthens memory of the skill. Ending on a success they chose themselves builds confidence and makes them want to come back.

## 7. Coaching Groups at the Fields: New Tools for This Week

These build on the basics you already have (freeze signal, stations, tears, positive language).

### A. Kill the line
Lines are where attention and behavior fall apart. If you have 10 kids in one line, each kid gets about 1 rep every 2 minutes and spends the rest bored, poking the kid in front.
- **Rule of thumb: no more than 2 or 3 kids waiting per ball.** If you have 12 kids, run 4 groups of 3, or have everyone do the drill in pairs at once.
- **One ball per pair, not one ball per coach.** For grounders: kids pair up 15 feet apart and roll grounders to each other (Chomp drill). You walk between pairs and coach.
- **Give the waiting kid a job:** counter, ball-feeder, "umpire," or "next up practice" (doing Popcorn in place while waiting).
- **Count reps, not minutes.** At the end of a drill ask: "How many grounders did each of you get?" If the answer is under 10, the drill was too line-heavy.

### B. Bat safety spacing (the "Bat Bubble")
Swinging bats are the biggest injury risk at youth baseball practice.
- **One bat out per hitting station.** All other bats lie flat in the dugout or a marked "bat bed." Kids may *only* pick up a bat at the station.
- **Mark the hitting zone** with a hula hoop or 4 cones. Only the hitter stands inside. The next hitter waits *behind a cone at least 3 big steps back and off to the side*, not behind the hitter's back.
- **Check before you swing:** teach every hitter to hold the bat out at arm's length and turn slowly in a full circle before swinging ("Bubble check"). If the bat touches anything, someone's too close.
- **Space hitting stations far apart** (at least 20 to 25 feet), all **facing the same direction** toward a fence or net, so no one is ever in another station's swing path or hit path.
- **Coach positions:** when you toss, stand at a front diagonal (not straight ahead) behind an L-screen or out of the line of the ball when possible. Use soft balls for front toss with young kids.
- **"Drop it, don't throw it."** Teach a bat drop after contact from day one, and practice it as part of baserunning drills.

### C. Pairing mixed ages and skill levels
- **Big Buddy / Little Buddy** for teaching moments: an older or stronger kid feeds balls or demonstrates for a younger one. It gives the advanced kid a role and responsibility, not boredom.
- **Similar-level pairs** for competitive games: kids learn most when the challenge is close. A strong kid crushing a weak one teaches neither.
- **Challenge by choice:** set out cones at 3 distances (near, middle, far) and let each kid pick. Most kids choose well, and those who don't will move after a few misses. This handles the skill range without singling anyone out.
- **Handicap rules** for mixed pairs: the stronger player uses the weak hand, walks instead of runs, or needs 3 catches to the other's 1. (Just like the Treasure Keeper brother rules.)
- **Switch pairs every 10 to 15 minutes** so cliques don't form and nobody gets stuck with a mismatch.

### D. "Ask before tell"
Instead of giving the correction, ask a question that lets the kid find it.
- **Instead of:** "Get your glove down." **Ask:** "Where was your glove when the ball went under?"
- **Instead of:** "Step toward your target." **Ask:** "Where were your toes pointing on that throw?"
- **Instead of:** "Run through the bag." **Ask:** "Where's the finish line on first base?"
- **Then:** "Show me." Let them try again right away.
- Keep questions short and about **one thing**. If they don't know, offer two choices: "Was your glove up or down?"
- Why it works: kids remember what they figure out themselves much better than what they're told. Each question takes 5 seconds, so it doesn't eat practice time.

### E. The kid who won't participate
Common at 5 to 7: they're shy, scared, tired, overwhelmed, or upset about something else.
- **Don't force or bargain in front of everyone.** It turns into a standoff.
- **Give a job first:** "Can you be my ball counter?" or "Can you hold the cones for me?" Being *near* the activity with a role is step one.
- **Offer a tiny entry:** "Want to do just 2 grounders with me, just you and me?" Small, private, low-risk.
- **Invite, then walk away.** "We'd love you in the next round when you're ready." Then don't watch them. Kids often join once the pressure is off.
- **Pair them with a kind buddy** rather than putting them in front of the group.
- **Check in privately** later ("Was something hard today?"), and mention it to the parent calmly and without blame if it's a pattern.

### F. Working with parent helpers
Parents want to help but often over-coach or coach the wrong thing.
- **Give each helper ONE job and ONE cue word.** For example: "You run the grounder station. The only cue word is Chomp. Count each kid's reps." A short index card helps.
- **Brief them for 2 minutes before practice**, not during: what the station is, the cue, and "praise effort, give one correction at a time, then let them try again."
- **Ask for "no coaching from the fence."** Give fence parents a fun job instead (cheering for effort, timing runs).
- **Rotate kids, not helpers.** Helpers stay at their station and get better at running it.
- **Thank them in front of the kids.** It builds a team culture.

### G. A quick 3-part practice script (45 to 60 minutes)
Use "**Play, Practice, Play.**"
1. **Play (10 to 15 min):** Kids arrive and go straight into a game (e.g., Pounce, Grounder Goalie, a mini game). No lines, no lecture. Late arrivals just join in.
2. **Practice (20 to 25 min):** 2 or 3 short stations focused on **one theme** (e.g., "ground balls and throwing"). 6 to 8 minutes per station, one cue word per station, ask-before-tell coaching. Kids rotate on your freeze signal.
3. **Play (15 to 20 min):** A real game or game-like scrimmage using the theme. Let the game do the teaching and coach only with quick cues. End with a 1-minute huddle: "What did we work on?" plus one specific praise for the group.
