# Week 2 Coaching Plan: "Same Skills, One Step Further"
**For Greg | Boys ages 7 and 5 | 5 sessions, 20–30 min each**

## Before you start: what's new this week

Week 1 was about getting the basic pattern down. Week 2 is about **adding one piece at a time** to that pattern: a hip turn on the throw, a load on the swing, a cut on a run. You'll keep using the habits you already know (whole-part-whole, one cue, silent demo, guided discovery, effort praise, the coaching loop). Three things are new:

1. **Cool-downs every session.** They take 3–4 minutes. They calm the boys' bodies down and give the day a clear ending, which makes the next session easier to start. They also give you one last chance to repeat the cue word.
2. **Kid's Choice.** Each boy picks **one game per session** from the Choice Menu below. Kids who get a real choice try harder, and it costs you nothing.
3. **7-year-old teach-back.** Your 7yo explains or shows a skill to his brother. A short version happens in every cool-down, and the full version is Session 5. Teaching something is one of the fastest ways for a kid to learn it.

### Session shape (about 30 min; a 20-min version is listed for each session)
| Block | Time | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | 4–5 min | Movement skill + raise heart rate |
| Skill work | 8–10 min | One new piece per boy |
| Game | 5 min + Kid's Choice (2 × 3 min) | Use the skill under fun pressure |
| Cool-down | 3–4 min | Calm down, review the cue, teach-back |

### Kid's Choice Menu (print it or snap a photo)
Week 1 favorites: Flamingo Freeze Tag, Sharks & Minnows, Bottle Knockdown, Red/Green Light, Tail Tag, Balloon Keep-Up, Simon Says with Ball, Mini Flag Game.
Week 2 additions as you learn them: Turnaround Races, Soccer Bowling, Zone Derby, Route Call Touchdown, Triple Threat Course.

**Choice rules:** the 5yo picks first on Sessions 1, 3, and 5, and the 7yo picks first on 2 and 4. The chooser gets to be "rule boss" for one small tweak, like a smaller field or Dad hops on one foot. **Why it works:** a predictable turn order prevents fights before they start, and the tweak gives the chooser real ownership without letting the game fall apart.

### How to read each activity
Every activity uses the same layout: **Steps → Cue → Silent demo → Errors & fixes (5yo / 7yo) → 5 vs 7 → Fun → Why it works.** Say the cue word, show the demo, and get them moving. Keep talking to 20 seconds or less.

## SESSION 1: Speed Starts & Belly-Button Throws
**Gear:** 4 cones (or shoes), tennis balls/soft baseballs, a bucket or laundry basket, a fence/wall or big target.
**20-min version:** shorten the skill block to 6 min and play one Choice game only.

### Warm-up: "Timber Starts" (5 min)
**Goal:** 7yo learns the lean-and-go start. 5yo practices cheek-to-cheek arms.
**Steps:**
1. Stand tall with feet together and arms ready. "Be a tree."
2. Lean forward from the ankles with the body straight like a board. Don't bend at the waist.
3. When you're about to tip over, step and sprint 10 big steps.
4. Walk back. Do 4–6 reps.

**Cue:** **"Timber!"** You yell it, they fall and go.
**Silent demo:** Stand stiff, slowly tip forward with a wobbly face, then explode into 3 sprint steps. Don't say anything. Raise your eyebrows at them: your turn.
**Errors & fixes:**
- *5yo:* Arms flap sideways or go across his body. **Fix:** Skip the lean for now. Have him do "pocket to cheek" arm swings standing still for 5 seconds, then run. If you need a reminder, say "Cheeks!" (from Week 1).
- *7yo:* Bends at the waist (butt sticks out) instead of leaning straight. **Fix:** "Can I push you over like a board?" Put your hand on his chest, have him lean into it, then pull your hand away so he has to go.
- *7yo:* Takes a tiny stutter step backward first. **Fix:** Put a shoe behind his heels. "Don't touch the shoe."

**5yo vs 7yo:** The 5yo does standing starts with an arm focus, and the lean is optional. The 7yo does the full lean and you time 2 reps.
**Fun:** You're the "lumberjack" yelling Timber. Add fake-outs like "Tim… BURRITO!" (no going), then "TIMBER!"
**Why it works:** The lean lets gravity start the sprint, so it uses a natural reflex instead of a list of instructions. It also gives one boy something to work on while the other works on something different, inside the same game.

> 📺 **Video (for you):** [Top 10 Sprint Acceleration Drills [#10 Falling Sprint] — Overtime Athletes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=77YR0tOHn44). Watch the falling-sprint section. It's demoed by older athletes, so shrink it to 10 steps for your boys.

### Skill work: "Belly-Button Throws" (10 min)
**Goal:** 7yo turns his hips (belly button to target) and adds distance. 5yo steps every time and throws to Dad.
**Steps (building on Point-Step-Throw):**
1. Stand sideways and point the glove at the target, same as last week.
2. Step toward the target.
3. **New piece:** spin the belly button so it faces the target, then the arm comes through.
4. Finish with the throwing hand near the opposite knee ("put it in your other pocket").

**Cue:** **"Belly!"**
**Silent demo:** Draw a smiley face on a sticky note or piece of tape and stick it on your shirt over your belly button. Throw slowly, exaggerating the smiley turning to "look at" the target. Then put a sticker on his shirt too.
**Errors & fixes:**
- *5yo:* Steps with the same foot as his throwing arm, or doesn't step at all. **Fix:** Put a "stepping spot" (cone or shoe) in front of his opposite foot. "Squish the cone with this foot, then throw." At 5, the consistent step is the whole goal. Leave the hips alone.
- *5yo:* Throws to the sky or the ground. **Fix:** Kneel down and hold your glove at his chest height as a big target. "Hit my glove." Being closer fixes most of it.
- *7yo:* Arm-only throw; the belly still faces sideways when the ball leaves. **Fix:** Guided discovery: "Throw once with your belly frozen and once with your belly turning. Which went farther?" Let him find the answer.
- *7yo:* Spins so hard he falls off to the side. **Fix:** "Finish like a statue for 2 seconds." Balance comes before power.

**5yo vs 7yo:** The 5yo throws to Dad at 15 ft, kneeling target, and you count steps, not hits. The 7yo throws at a fence target, and every 3 hits out of 5 earns **one giant step back** (the distance ladder).
**Fun:** The 7yo's distance becomes "levels" like a video game (Level 1 = 20 ft … Level 5 = 40 ft). The 5yo gets a "Dad flinch": every throw that hits your glove, you fall over dramatically.
**Why it works:** One new piece on top of a known pattern keeps the challenge just hard enough. Earning distance by hitting targets makes him keep accuracy while he adds power, instead of trading one for the other.

> 📺 **Video (for you):** [How To Teach 6-8 Year Olds To Catch A Baseball — Antonelli Baseball](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6T5WWG1E8WM). It's a catching video, but watch how he keeps the drills short, close, and confidence-first. That's the same mindset for throwing this week.

### Game: "Turnaround Races" (5 min)
**Goal:** 7yo races with a turn. Both boys use their new start.
**Steps:**
1. Put a cone 10–15 steps away.
2. On "Timber!" sprint to the cone, touch it with your hand, turn, and sprint back.
3. **Turn trick:** take little "chop" steps as you get near the cone, get low, touch, and push back.

**Cue:** **"Chop!"**
**Silent demo:** Jog to the cone, take exaggerated tiny steps, crouch, touch, and push off. Then run it full speed so they see the difference.
**Errors & fixes:**
- *5yo:* Runs past the cone or circles wide around it. **Fix:** Put a stuffed animal on the cone. "Give him a high five." A target to touch makes him stop.
- *7yo:* Big steps into the turn, so he slides or stumbles. **Fix:** Race him twice, once with "big steps at the cone" and once with "chop steps." Ask which was faster.

**5yo vs 7yo:** The 5yo gets a head start (a closer cone or a "go early" pass). The 7yo races Dad, and Dad has to do it hopping.
**Fun:** Commentate like a sports announcer. Do "rematch" rounds where the loser picks the next race rule (backwards back, crab back).
**Why it works:** Races give instant, honest feedback with no lecture needed. The handicaps keep both boys in the game so the younger one doesn't quit.

### Kid's Choice ×2 (6 min)
5yo picks first today.

### Cool-down: "Melting Snowman" (3 min)
**Steps:**
1. Stand tall like a snowman and take 2 big "snow breaths" (in through the nose for 3, out through the mouth for 4).
2. The sun comes out: melt slowly to the ground over 10 seconds.
3. Lie down, then hug your knees and rock 5 times.
4. **Teach-back:** "Tell me today's throwing word and show me with no ball."

**Cue:** **"Melt."**
**Silent demo:** You melt first, as dramatically as possible.
**Errors & fixes:** *5yo* drops instantly. **Fix:** Count down out loud from 10, and he melts one "drip" per number. *7yo* rushes. **Fix:** Make it a contest: "Slowest melt wins."
**5 vs 7:** The 5yo just melts. The 7yo also explains "Belly" in his own words.
**Fun:** Make sound effects (sizzle, drip).
**Why it works:** Slow breathing helps tired kids settle down. Saying the cue one last time helps it stick, and the same ending ritual tells them practice is over in a good way.

## SESSION 2: Locked Ankles, Both Feet, First Shots
**Gear:** 1 ball each, 6–8 cones, a small goal (2 cones, a pop-up goal, or a lawn chair), 4 water bottles.
**20-min version:** combine warm-up and Choice. Play one Choice game as the warm-up.

### Warm-up: "Skip & Hop Trail" (5 min)
**Goal:** Both boys work on skipping. 7yo adds one-foot hops. 5yo works on quiet landings.
**Steps:**
1. Lay a line of 5 cones as the "trail."
2. Lap 1: step-hop, step-hop (skip) along the trail.
3. Lap 2 (7yo): 3 hops on the right foot, 3 on the left, alternating between cones.
4. Lap 3 (everyone): "Ninja jumps." Jump over each cone and land without a sound.

**Cue:** **"Step-hop"** for skipping and **"Ninja"** for landing. Use one cue per lap, never both at once.
**Silent demo:** Skip in slow motion while tapping your leg on each hop. For Ninja, land with a deep knee bend and a finger to your lips.
**Errors & fixes:**
- *5yo:* Gallops (same foot always leads) instead of skipping. **Fix:** Totally normal at 5. Hold his hand, skip together slowly, and say "step-hop, step-hop" in rhythm. Clap the beat. Some 5-year-olds just aren't ready to skip yet, and that's fine.
- *5yo:* Lands loud and stiff-legged. **Fix:** "Can you land so quietly I can't hear you?" Close your eyes and listen. Kids love fooling you.
- *7yo:* Hops are flat-footed, or he puts the other foot down. **Fix:** "Lift your other foot like a flamingo" (Week 1 language). Shrink the hops to tiny ones first.

**5 vs 7:** The 5yo skips and does Ninja jumps. The 7yo does all three laps, with hops on both feet.
**Fun:** You're the "sleeping giant" at the end of the trail. Loud landings wake you up and you chase them.
**Why it works:** Skipping and hopping build the rhythm, balance, and push-off that running and kicking depend on. Practicing quiet landings teaches soft knees, which protects joints and builds body control.

> 📺 **Video (for you):** [Teaching Kids How to Skip — T.E.A.M. 4 Kids Pediatric Therapy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UHctZqezc9U). A pediatric therapy team shows the build-up from marching to galloping to hopping, which is perfect if the 5yo is stuck.

### Skill work, 7yo: "Gate Passing" (10 min, split with the 5yo block below)
**Goal:** Inside-foot pass with a locked ankle, using both feet.
**Steps:**
1. Make a "gate" of 2 cones 3 steps wide between you and him, 6–8 steps apart.
2. Plant the non-kicking foot beside the ball, toes pointing at the target.
3. Turn the kicking foot out like a duck and lock the ankle, toes up.
4. Push through the middle of the ball and follow through to the target.
5. Do 5 passes with the right foot, 5 with the left, then alternate.

**Cue:** **"Lock!"**
**Silent demo:** Show a floppy ankle with an exaggerated wobbly pass that misses badly. Then lock your ankle, tap it with your hand, and make a crisp pass through the gate. Point at your ankle. Let him guess what changed.
**Errors & fixes:**
- *7yo:* Toe-pokes. **Fix:** "Toe goes up to the sky, like you're showing me the bottom of your shoe." Touch the inside of his foot: "This is the passing spot."
- *7yo:* Plant foot is too far behind the ball, so the pass pops up. **Fix:** Put a coin or bottle cap next to the ball. "Plant foot lands on the coin."
- *7yo:* Weak left foot means he won't use it. **Fix:** "Left-foot passes through the gate count double." Praise effort out loud: "Brave left foot!"

**Fun:** Each pass through the gate is a point. Shrink the gate after every 5 points.
**Why it works:** The locked ankle gives a flat, solid kicking surface, so the ball goes where he aims. Shrinking the gate raises the difficulty only after he succeeds. Practicing both feet now means he won't be one-footed for life.

> - [#4. How to teach: Passing › Inside foot | Soccer skills in PE (grade K-6) — Prime Coaching Sport](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yk5Gku_Ojas). Shows how a PE teacher *teaches* it to a group, using both feet.

### Skill work, 5yo: "Cone Slalom" (during the same 10 min)
**Goal:** Dribble around cones with small touches, then a first shot.
**Steps:**
1. Space 4 cones about 2 big steps apart in a line.
2. Use small taps (Week 1's small-touch dribbling) to weave in and out.
3. After the last cone, shoot at the goal.

**Cue:** **"Steer."** The ball is the car and the foot is the steering wheel.
**Silent demo:** Make car noises while you weave slowly. Bump a cone "by accident," make a crash noise, laugh, and keep going. That shows mistakes are okay.
**Errors & fixes:**
- *5yo:* Kicks the ball way ahead, then chases it. **Fix:** "The ball is on a leash. It has to stay close enough to pet." Bring the cones closer together.
- *5yo:* Skips cones to go straight to the goal. **Fix:** Put a "toll" on each cone, like a high five from Dad, before the next one.
- *5yo:* Picks the ball up. **Fix:** Laugh it off: "Oops, hands are sleeping!" Have him put his hands on his head while he dribbles.

**Running both at once:** Set the 5yo's slalom up next to the 7yo's gate. Pass with the 7yo while the 5yo does a lap, and cheer the 5yo's shot each time he finishes. That keeps both busy with no waiting.
**Why it works:** Cones give him a clear path to follow, so he gets practice steering the ball without needing you to correct him. Ending with a shot gives every lap a payoff.

> 📺 **Video (for you):** [U6 Gates Activity — Cal South Soccer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2y8aaghFp6U). A state soccer association's U6 game for dribbling through cone gates. Steal the setup for the 5yo.

### Game: "Soccer Bowling + Bonus Shot" (5 min)
**Steps:**
1. Set up 4 water bottles as pins.
2. **7yo:** knock down pins with locked-ankle passes from 5–6 steps, alternating feet.
3. **5yo:** dribble up close (2 steps away) and pass or shoot at the pins.
4. After every pin, the kicker gets a **bonus shot** on Dad in goal.

**Cue:** **"Lock"** for the 7yo and **"Boom!"** for the 5yo shot. "Boom" is his celebration word, and any contact counts.
**Silent demo:** Line up, show the locked ankle, knock a pin, then do a silly celebration dance.
**Errors & fixes:**
- *5yo:* Gets frustrated after misses. **Fix:** Move the start closer until he knocks one down, then celebrate wildly.
- *7yo:* Blasts with his laces (power over accuracy). **Fix:** "Pins only fall to passes, not rockets. Rockets are for bonus shots."

**Fun:** You play a "terrible goalie" who dives too late about 2 out of 3 times.
**Why it works:** Knocking a pin over shows right away whether the pass was accurate. The bonus shot rewards good technique with something fun.

### Kid's Choice ×2 (6 min)
7yo picks first today.

### Cool-down: "Ball Roll Stretch" (3 min)
**Steps:**
1. Sit with legs straight out, ball on your shins.
2. Roll the ball slowly down to your toes and back, 5 times. (This stretches the hamstrings.)
3. Make a butterfly (soles together) and roll the ball in a circle around your feet.
4. **Teach-back:** the 7yo shows his brother "Lock" by pointing at his own ankle and doing one slow pass.

**Cue:** **"Slow roll."**
**Silent demo:** Roll as slowly as a snail, making a snail face.
**Errors & fixes:** The *5yo* rolls fast and the ball escapes. **Fix:** "The ball is asleep. Don't wake it." The *7yo* bends his knees to cheat. **Fix:** Play "keep your knees glued to the ground."
**5 vs 7:** Same stretch for both. Only the 7yo does the teach-back.
**Why it works:** A gentle stretch at the end of a session is a calm, low-key way to finish. Teaching the cue makes the 7yo put it into his own words.

## SESSION 3: Moving to the Ball & Full Swing
**Gear:** Big playground ball, tennis balls, glove (7yo), tee, bat, wiffle or foam balls (about 10), cones for zones.
**20-min version:** spend 5 min on catching and 5 on hitting, and play Zone Derby as the only game.

### Warm-up: "Mirror Shuffle" (4 min)
**Goal:** 7yo learns side shuffles. 5yo practices moving his feet sideways (this sets up catching).
**Steps:**
1. Face each other about 5 steps apart, in a wide stance with knees bent ("sumo").
2. You shuffle left or right, and they mirror you.
3. Keep the feet wide: push, slide, push, slide. Don't let the heels click together.
4. Do 20-second rounds with a short rest between.

**Cue:** **"Wide."**
**Silent demo:** Shuffle slowly in a deep sumo stance. Then do a "bad" version with heels clicking, fall over on purpose, and shake your head.
**Errors & fixes:**
- *5yo:* Turns and runs instead of shuffling. **Fix:** Hold hands facing each other and shuffle together.
- *5yo:* Stands up tall. **Fix:** "Stay short enough to fit under the table."
- *7yo:* Crosses his feet or clicks his heels. **Fix:** Put a pool noodle or rope between his feet: "Don't step on the snake."

**5 vs 7:** The 5yo shuffles slowly and in only one direction at first. The 7yo changes direction on your fake-outs.
**Fun:** "Copycat" mode. If they mirror you for 20 seconds without losing you, they become the leader and you have to copy them.
**Why it works:** Mirroring makes them react to what they see, which is how fielding actually works, and it's more fun than following orders. A wide base gets their feet ready for the next drill.

### Skill work, 7yo: "Shuffle & Snag" (5 min)
**Goal:** Glove-side vs. backhand catches, and moving his feet to the ball.
**Steps:**
1. Stand 15 ft apart. Toss soft tennis balls to his glove side, then to his throwing-hand side.
2. **Glove side:** fingers up, like a high five.
3. **Backhand (across his body):** flip the glove so the thumb points down and the palm faces the ball.
4. **Moving step:** you toss a bit wider each time, and he shuffles ("Wide") so the ball comes to the middle when he can.

**Cue:** **"Feet."** Feet move first, then the glove.
**Silent demo:** Have him toss to you. Show a lazy reach where your feet stay planted and you barely miss. Then show shuffling over and catching in front of your chest. Next, show a backhand by flipping the glove in slow motion and freezing so he can see it.
**Guided discovery:** "When the ball goes way over here, which way does your glove have to turn?" Let him experiment before you tell him.
**Errors & fixes:**
- *7yo:* Reaches with his arm and leaves his feet planted. **Fix:** Toss a little wider than he can reach so he *has* to move. Praise every shuffle, even when he drops the ball.
- *7yo:* Turns his head away or closes his eyes on the backhand. **Fix:** Use a softer ball (foam), stand closer, and toss slower.
- *7yo:* Tries to backhand balls that are right in front of him. **Fix:** Explain it this way: "Backhand is only for balls you can't get in front of."

**Why it works:** Catching balls in a mix of spots builds real-game skill better than 20 catches in the same place. Saying "feet first" gives him one simple thing to focus on.

> 📺 **Video (for you):** [How To Teach 6-8 Year Olds To Catch A Baseball — Antonelli Baseball](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6T5WWG1E8WM). Same video as Session 1. It's right in your 7yo's age band.

### Skill work, 5yo: "Big Ball, Little Ball" (5 min, alongside the 7yo)
**Goal:** Move from a big ball to a tennis ball, catching with two hands.
**Steps:**
1. Kneel 6–8 ft away and toss a big playground ball underhand to his chest.
2. After 5 catches, switch to a mid-size ball (soccer or volleyball).
3. After 5 more, switch to a tennis ball, caught with two hands.
4. If he drops 3 in a row, go back down one ball size. No big deal.

**Cue:** **"Hands ready"** (hands out like a bowl before you toss). Keep using "fingers up" from Week 1 for high balls.
**Silent demo:** Have him toss the tennis ball to you. Show "sleeping hands" at your sides and miss it. Then show "awake hands" out in front, ready, and catch. Look at him and smile.
**Errors & fixes:**
- *5yo:* Traps the ball against his chest with his arms (a "hug catch"). **Fix:** Fine with the big ball. With the tennis ball, say "catch it with just your hands, like a frog grabbing a fly."
- *5yo:* Turns his head away. **Fix:** Stay with the bigger or softer ball longer. Fear means the ball is too hard or too fast, not that he isn't trying.
- *5yo:* Hands are ready too late. **Fix:** Say "Ready?", wait until his hands are up, *then* toss.

**Why it works:** Stepping down in ball size keeps him catching most of the time while the task gets harder. Going back a step after misses protects his confidence.

**Running both at once:** Alternate tosses: one to the 7yo, one to the 5yo. Or let the 7yo toss the big ball to his brother, which is a mini teach-back.

### Skill work, 7yo: "Rock-Step-Squish" swing + soft front toss (5 min)
**Goal:** A full swing (load, stride, squish) against soft front toss.
**Steps:**
1. **Rock (load):** hands and weight shift back slightly, like pulling back a slingshot.
2. **Step (stride):** a small, soft step toward the pitcher. "Step on a bug, don't stomp it."
3. **Squish:** back foot squishes the bug (Week 1) as the hips turn and the bat comes through.
4. Do 5 dry swings saying "rock, step, squish," then 5 off the tee, then **soft front toss.**
5. **Front toss setup:** you kneel 10–12 ft away, off at an angle or behind a net/screen. Use **wiffle or foam balls only.** Toss underhand, slowly, at his front hip.

**Cue:** **"Rock."** The rhythm phrase is "rock, step, squish," but "Rock" is the trigger you say as you toss.
**Silent demo:** Do the swing in slow motion three times, freezing at each part like a statue: rock (freeze), step (freeze), squish (freeze). Then do one smooth swing at full speed.
**Errors & fixes:**
- *7yo:* Huge stride, so his head drops and he lunges. **Fix:** Put a towel where the stride should land: "Step on the towel, not past it."
- *7yo:* Never loads; the swing is all arms. **Fix:** "Show me the slingshot." Have him hold the rock position for one second on dry swings.
- *7yo:* Swings too early or too late on toss. **Fix:** Say "Rock" as your tossing hand goes back, so his load matches your toss. Slow your toss down; don't speed up his swing.

**Safety:** The 5yo stands behind a cone line at least 10 big steps away, or is busy at his own tee (below).
**Why it works:** Freezing each part shows him where it goes, and the full-speed swing ties it back together. Saying "Rock" as you toss gives him a timing signal.

> 📺 **Video (for you):** [How to Soft Toss | YOUTH BASEBALL AND SOFTBALL — Dominate The Diamond](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rV3EQ5tHcNE). Shows where the toss should go and how to keep a steady tempo for youth hitters.

### Skill work, 5yo: "Dot Hitting" off the tee (5 min, at the same time)
**Goal:** Consistent contact off the tee.
**Steps:**
1. Put a sticker or marker dot on the back of the wiffle ball.
2. Stand with feet wide and knuckles lined up (from Week 1).
3. Eyes on the dot the whole time, then swing.
4. Say "hit!" when you see the bat touch the dot.

**Cue:** **"Dot."**
**Silent demo:** Point at your eye, then point at the dot. Swing slowly, keeping your head still and looking at where the ball *was*.
**Errors & fixes:**
- *5yo:* Hits the tee instead of the ball. **Fix:** Raise the tee to belly-button height, and move him so his belly button lines up with the front edge of the plate.
- *5yo:* Looks up to watch where the ball went before he hits it. **Fix:** "Find the dot after the ball is gone. Is the tee still there?" A game of looking at the tee after the swing keeps his head down.
- *5yo:* Stands too close and gets jammed. **Fix:** Put the bat on the ball first: "If the bat can touch it, you're in the right spot."

**Why it works:** Focusing on something outside the body (the dot) works better than thinking about body parts.

### Game: "Zone Derby" (5 min)
**Steps:**
1. Use cones to make 3 zones: close (1 pt), middle (2 pts), far (3 pts).
2. The 7yo hits 5 soft tosses and the 5yo hits 5 off the tee.
3. Score each hit by the zone it lands in, and count any contact as 1 point for the 5yo.
4. **Fielder rule:** whoever isn't batting fields, using "Feet" (7yo) or "Hands ready" (5yo). A clean catch steals 1 point.

**Cue:** Whatever the batter is working on: **"Rock"** for the 7yo, **"Dot"** for the 5yo.
**Silent demo:** Walk the zones and hold up 1, 2, and 3 fingers at each cone.
**Errors & fixes:** The *5yo* swings wildly to "go far." **Fix:** "Dots score, not muscles." The *7yo* gets upset over pop-ups. **Fix:** "Pop-ups are free practice for your brother's catching. That's a team point."
**Fun:** Do a home-run trot for any 3-point hit, with you playing crowd noise.
**Why it works:** Every turn involves hitting, fielding, or scoring, so nobody stands around.

### Kid's Choice ×2 (6 min)
5yo picks first. (If time is short, play one Choice game and save the other for next session.)

### Cool-down: "Glove Pillow" (3 min)
**Steps:**
1. Lie down with your glove (or a ball) as a pillow.
2. Arms up over your head for a "big stretch," then pull your knees to your chest.
3. Twist side to side with your knees together.
4. **Teach-back:** the 7yo says "rock, step, squish" while doing it slowly, and the 5yo copies.

**Cue:** **"Pillow."**
**Silent demo:** Lie down and fake-snore.
**Errors & fixes:** If they get giggly, that's fine; let it happen for 10 seconds, then whisper. Kids lower their voices to match a whisper.
**Why it works:** The 5yo copying "rock, step, squish" is his first look at the full swing, with no pressure.

## SESSION 4: Routes, Grips & Soft-Football Catches
**Gear:** Youth or junior-size football (a pee-wee/size 6 or smaller for the 7yo), a soft foam football (for the 5yo), cones, flags or tails (from Week 1).
**20-min version:** skip the Freeze Cuts warm-up; use Kid's Choice Tail Tag as the warm-up.

### Warm-up: "Freeze Cuts" (4 min)
**Goal:** Jogging, stopping fast, and cutting. This sets up routes.
**Steps:**
1. Jog freely around the yard.
2. "**Stop!**": stop in 2 chopping steps and freeze, low.
3. "**Left!/Right!**": plant the outside foot and cut that way.
4. "**Go!**": sprint 5 steps.

**Cue:** **"Plant."** Push off the outside foot to cut.
**Silent demo:** Jog, stomp your right foot, and cut left. Exaggerate the push and point at the foot that did it.
**Errors & fixes:**
- *5yo:* Mixes up left and right. **Fix:** Point instead of calling "left," or use landmarks: "Cut to the tree!"
- *5yo:* Can't stop and runs past. **Fix:** Slow the jog to a "walk-jog" and only call "Stop."
- *7yo:* Rounds his cuts (a curved banana path). **Fix:** Put a cone in front of him: "Cut at the cone like a corner of a box, not a circle."

**5 vs 7:** The 5yo does stop/go only. The 7yo does all commands.
**Fun:** Do it to music, like freeze dance. When the music stops, they freeze.
**Why it works:** Stopping fast and cutting are the moves routes are built from, and practicing them without a ball first makes the routes easier to learn.

### Skill work, 7yo: "Route Cards" + "Laces Grip" (10 min)
**Goal:** Run Go, Stop, In, and Out routes. Learn the QB grip.

**Part A, the routes (6 min). Steps:**
1. Line up at a cone (the line of scrimmage).
2. **Go:** sprint straight, look back for the ball.
3. **Stop:** run 5 steps, stop, turn around, and show your hands.
4. **In:** run 5 steps, plant, and cut toward the middle.
5. **Out:** run 5 steps, plant, and cut toward the sideline.
6. Walk each route first, then jog, then run it full speed.

**Cue:** **"Five."** Count five steps out loud, then make your move.
**Silent demo:** Draw the 4 routes in the dirt or with chalk as arrows. Walk each one, holding up fingers to count 1-2-3-4-5, then cut. Then point to a drawing and have him run it.
**Errors & fixes:**
- *7yo:* Cuts after 2 steps or after 12. **Fix:** Put a cone at 5 steps: "Cut at the cone." Remove the cone after he gets it right 3 times.
- *7yo:* Never looks back for the ball. **Fix:** Hold up 1–3 fingers right after his cut, and he has to shout the number.
- *7yo:* Drifts on the "Stop" route. **Fix:** "Freeze like Red Light (Week 1), then turn."

**Part B, the QB grip (4 min). Steps:**
1. Put your ring and pinky fingers on the laces.
2. Fingertips only, with a gap (daylight) between the palm and the ball.
3. Hold the ball up by your ear, and step toward the target as you throw (same as baseball).
4. Short throws only, 5–8 yards.

**Cue:** **"Laces."**
**Silent demo:** Hold the ball up to the sun and show the gap under your palm. Wiggle your fingers on the laces, then hand it to him and wait.
**Errors & fixes:**
- *7yo:* Palms the ball, or has hands too small for the ball. **Fix:** Use a smaller football. A ball that's too big causes bad grip habits. Ask: "Can you see sunlight under your palm?"
- *7yo:* Throws sidearm. **Fix:** "Start by your ear, like a phone call."
- *7yo:* Frustrated with no spiral. **Fix:** Wobbly passes that reach you are a win. Praise the step and the catchability, not the spin.

**Why it works:** Routes and the grip are two separate skills, so teaching them separately keeps it simple.

> 📺 **Videos (for you):**
> - [How to Catch a Football | NFL FLAG Drills — NFL FLAG](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=803CND760i4). Official NFL FLAG youth drill video on hand position. Use it for both boys.
> - [How to hold a football the RIGHT way — Kurt Benkert](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/W9558zWeIws). A 30-second Short from a former NFL QB on finger placement on the laces and keeping the palm off the ball.

### Skill work, 5yo: "Basket Catch" with a soft football (alongside the 7yo)
**Goal:** Catch a soft football.
**Steps:**
1. Kneel 5 ft away and toss the foam football underhand, slowly, chest high.
2. Hands make a basket: pinkies together, palms up for low balls.
3. Pull the ball into your belly.
4. Back up one step after 3 catches.

**Cue:** **"Basket."**
**Silent demo:** Make a basket with your hands, pretend to put eggs in it, and pull it gently to your belly.
**Errors & fixes:**
- *5yo:* The ball bounces off his chest. **Fix:** Normal. Go back to a closer, slower toss. Say "Make your basket before I throw."
- *5yo:* Hands stay low, so high balls hit his face. **Fix:** Only throw to his belly this week. Save high catches for Week 3.
- *5yo:* Is scared of the pointy ends. **Fix:** Let him hold the foam ball, squeeze it, and bonk himself on the head with it. Then the fear is gone.

**Why it works:** A soft ball tossed at his belly means he'll catch it most of the time, which builds the confidence he needs before harder throws.

> 📺 **Video (for you):** [3 Beginner Football Catching Drills — CAB Training](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N2Y4_Y0zoRw). Beginner progressions that start with a tennis ball and move up to the football. Useful for both boys.

### Game: "Route Call Touchdown" (5 min)
**Steps:**
1. Use cones for an end zone about 15 yards away.
2. **7yo:** you're QB. He calls his own route ("Out!"), runs it, and you throw. A catch moves the ball forward; 3 catches is a touchdown.
3. **5yo:** he runs a "Go" toward you and you hand him or soft-toss him the ball. Then he runs to the end zone while the 7yo tries to pull his flag (Week 1 flag pulling).
4. **Bonus round:** the 7yo is QB and throws the foam ball to his brother from 3–4 yards.

**Cue:** **"Five"** for the 7yo, **"Basket"** for the 5yo.
**Silent demo:** Huddle up, "call" a play by drawing in your palm, then break the huddle with a clap.
**Errors & fixes:** The *7yo* runs the wrong route on purpose to show off. **Fix:** "Called route catches count double." If the *5yo* gets his flag pulled every time, the 7yo has to pull flags while hopping on one foot.
**Fun:** Do real huddles, a team name, and a touchdown dance at the end of every drive.
**Why it works:** Calling his own route makes the 7yo plan it first. The bonus round is a teach-back where the 7yo has to make his throw catchable, which shows real empathy and control.

### Kid's Choice ×2 (6 min)
7yo picks first.

### Cool-down: "Playbook Draw" (3 min)
**Steps:**
1. Sit down, sip water, and take 3 slow breaths.
2. Each boy draws his favorite route with chalk or a stick in the dirt.
3. Walk it in slow motion together, "walk-through speed."
4. **Teach-back:** the 7yo shows his brother the "Laces" grip on the foam ball.

**Cue:** **"Walk-through."**
**Silent demo:** Draw a squiggly silly route (like a pretzel), then walk it super slowly.
**Errors & fixes:** If the *5yo* scribbles, ask "Where does it start? Where does the ball come?" and accept anything. The *7yo* rushes. **Fix:** "Slow-motion replay, like on TV."
**Why it works:** Drawing and walking the route uses picture, words, and movement together. The slow walk also brings their heart rate down.

## SESSION 5: Teach-Back Day & the Triple Threat Course
**Gear:** Everything: cones, a ball of each kind, a target, a tee, a goal.
**20-min version:** shorten the teach-back to 5 min and the course to 2 rounds.

### Warm-up: "Movement Dice" (4 min)
**Steps:**
1. Use a real die or call a number: **1** = skip, **2** = hop on your left foot, **3** = hop on your right foot, **4** = side shuffle, **5** = Timber Start sprint, **6** = Ninja jumps.
2. Each roll gets 15 seconds of that movement across the yard.
3. The boys take turns rolling.

**Cue:** Use the movement's own cue: **"Step-hop," "Wide," "Timber," "Ninja."**
**Silent demo:** Roll the die and act out the movement while holding up the number of fingers.
**Errors & fixes:** The *5yo* forgets which number is which. **Fix:** Tape picture cards to a fence (stick figures are fine). The *7yo* goes sloppy and fast. **Fix:** "Coach's eye. I'm watching for your best one."
**5 vs 7:** The 5yo gets "hop on any foot." The 7yo has to do the one called.
**Fun:** Rolling a 6 also means Dad has to do it.
**Why it works:** Mixing up the week's movements in random order makes them recall each skill on the spot, which helps them remember it better than repeating one move.

### Skill work: "Coach Big Brother" (the full teach-back, 10 min)
**Goal:** The 7yo teaches his brother one skill from this week. You coach the coach.
**Steps for Dad:**
1. Let the 7yo **choose** what to teach (likely the step throw, passing, or the Basket catch).
2. Prep him privately for 60 seconds: "Coaches use 1 word and a silent demo. What's your word? Show me your demo."
3. He runs it: word → demo → his brother tries → he gives one piece of praise and one tip.
4. You stay quiet and only step in with a question: "What did you see him do well?"
5. Switch: the 5yo "teaches" Dad something, such as how to skip or the Ninja landing. You do it badly on purpose so he can fix you.

**Cue:** **"Coach's word?"** (prompting the 7yo to use his cue).
**Silent demo:** You model the coaching itself: act out "show, don't tell" by miming zipping your lips, then demoing.
**Errors & fixes:**
- *7yo:* Lectures for 2 minutes. **Fix:** Give the "zipper" signal (a zip-lips gesture you taught him in prep). "One word and a demo, coach!"
- *7yo:* Criticizes harshly ("No! You're doing it wrong!"). **Fix:** Pull him aside: "Good coaches find something good first. What's one good thing?" Then let him try again.
- *5yo:* Won't listen to his brother. **Fix:** Make it a game: "Can you stump Coach Big Brother? Get it right 3 times and HE has to do 5 jumping jacks."
- *5yo, teaching Dad:* Gets shy. **Fix:** Ask "Is this right?" and do it obviously wrong. Kids can't resist correcting a grown-up.

**5 vs 7:** The 7yo teaches a real skill. The 5yo "teaches" a movement, mostly for the fun of being in charge.
**Fun:** Give him a whistle or a clipboard, call him "Coach [name]," and have the 5yo call you "Rookie."
**Why it works:** Explaining a skill makes the 7yo understand it better himself. He also feels what coaching is like, so he may be more patient when *you* coach *him*. And the 5yo gets to feel competent by being the one who knows.

### Game: "Triple Threat Course" (5 min)
**Steps:**
1. **Station 1:** throw at a target 3 times (Belly / Step).
2. **Station 2:** dribble through 4 cones and shoot (Lock / Steer + Boom).
3. **Station 3:** run a route and catch a toss (Five / Basket).
4. **Finish:** Timber Start sprint to the finish cone.
5. Time each boy against **his own** last time, not against his brother.

**Cue:** Whichever cue fits the station they're on.
**Silent demo:** Walk the course once in slow motion, pointing at each station.
**Errors & fixes:** The *5yo* gets lost between stations. **Fix:** You run beside him as his "tour guide." The *7yo* rushes and gets sloppy. **Fix:** "Good form adds a bonus 5 seconds off your time" (you decide, generously).
**Fun:** Have a podium (a bucket) and both boys win something different: "Fastest" and "Most Improved." Rotate who wins what.
**Why it works:** Timing against himself keeps the 5yo from comparing himself to his older brother. Covering all four sports in one run shows them how the skills connect.

### Kid's Choice ×2 (6 min)
5yo picks first.

### Cool-down: "Week Wrap Circle" (4 min)
**Steps:**
1. Sit in a circle for a butterfly stretch, then reach for your toes.
2. Each person says **one cue word** from this week and shows it.
3. Each person says one thing they got better at and one thing they want to learn next.
4. Do a team cheer ("1-2-3, ATHLETES!").

**Cue:** **"Best thing."**
**Silent demo:** You go first: show "Timber" with a little lean and say "I got better at being a patient coach."
**Errors & fixes:** The *5yo* says "everything" or "nothing." **Fix:** Offer two choices: "Was it catching or skipping?" The *7yo* only lists flaws. **Fix:** "One thing that got better first."
**Why it works:** People remember the end of an experience most, so a warm finish makes them look forward to next week. Saying out loud what they want to learn next gives them a say in Week 3.

## Group coaching at the baseball fields: new topics for Week 2

> 📺 **Watch first:** [Top 10 Tee Ball (& "Coach Pitch") Coaching Tips — YouGoProBaseball](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H8ccXY0SIIM). Two coaches talk honestly about feeling lost coaching 5–7-year-olds for the first time, plus practical tips like where to stand at the tee.

### 1. First-practice routines
**What to do:**
- **Arrive 15 min early** with cones already set up. The first kid who shows up gets a job ("Can you help me put balls in the bucket?") so early arrivals have something to do besides chaos.
- **Use name tape on helmets or shirts.** Learning names on day one is the biggest step toward building trust.
- **Use a 3-part opener every practice:** (1) circle and names, (2) the same warm-up game every time, (3) "Today we're learning ___." Kids this age feel safe when they know what happens next.
- **Teach the field with a game:** "Run to first base! Now run to the pitcher's mound!" It works as a warm-up and teaches the field at the same time.
- **Keep the first practice under 60 minutes,** and spend no more than 10 minutes on any one drill.

**Script:** "Hi, I'm Coach Greg. My job is to help you have fun and get better. Your job is to try hard and be a good teammate. Deal? Shake on it!"
**Why it works:** A predictable routine means you only need to show kids how practice works once. After that, the structure does the work and you spend less time managing behavior.

### 2. Parent helper management
**What to do:**
- **Give every helper a job card** (an index card with one station, one cue word, and one rule). Example: "Tee Station. Cue: DOT. Rule: Every kid gets 5 swings, then rotates."
- **Recruit with specific, small asks:** "Could you run the throwing station for 10 minutes?" works much better than "Can anyone help?"
- **Have a 2-minute parent huddle** before practice: today's cue words, the freeze signal, and "praise effort, one tip max."
- **Stop sideline coaching** kindly and early: "We're working on just one thing today. Can you cheer for effort? It helps them so much."
- **Thank helpers by name** in front of the kids at the end.

**Why it works:** Parents want to help but don't know how, and a job card answers that. It also keeps the message the same at every station, so kids don't hear five different coaches saying different things.

### 3. Keeping fielders busy in tee ball and coach pitch games
**What to do:**
- **Ready-position game:** Before every pitch, call "Ready!" Fielders slap their gloves and get in the "sumo" stance. Give a point to every kid who's ready.
- **"Where's the play?"** Before each batter, ask the fielders "If the ball comes to you, where do you throw it?" They yell "First!"
- **"Ball buddies" rule:** Pair each fielder with a neighbor. On any hit, both buddies run to the ball, and one fields while the other backs them up. This stops the 9-kid dogpile.
- **Rotate positions every inning.** Nobody plays outfield twice in a row.
- **Have outfielders do mini jobs:** count pitches, shout the outs, or play "statue" between batters.

**Why it works:** Bored kids drift (dirt digging, dandelion picking). Giving them a small job on every pitch keeps their attention on the game without you nagging.

### 4. The kid who won't participate
**What to do:**
- **Don't force it, and don't ignore it.** Make him your "assistant coach": he holds the bucket, says "Go!", or counts swings. Being near the activity comes before doing it.
- **Offer a small first step:** "Can you try just one throw to me? Then you can go back to being my helper."
- **Find the reason quietly:** scared of the ball, tired, overwhelmed by the group, or new and shy. A softer ball, a smaller group, or a buddy fixes most of these.
- **Check in with the parent privately and positively:** "He's warming up to it. What does he love at home?" Use that in your coaching ("Throw like a dinosaur!").
- **Praise any participation, quietly,** not in front of the whole team.

**Why it works:** Taking small steps toward the activity, with nothing bad happening, builds courage. Big public praise can make a shy kid hide.

### 5. The kid who dominates
**What to do:**
- **Give him a leadership job** instead of shutting him down: line leader, equipment captain, or "demo kid" for one drill. Leaders still have to wait their turn.
- **Add a challenge just for him:** "You hit it off the tee every time, so now try to hit it to the left side," or "Catch it backhand."
- **Use rules that share the ball:** "The ball has to be touched by 2 different players before the throw to first."
- **Praise teamwork out loud:** "Did you see how he let his buddy field that? That's a big-league teammate."
- **Talk privately** if he's bossing others: "You're really good. Good players help others get good too. Can you help me with that?"

**Why it works:** Kids who take over usually want attention or a bigger challenge. Giving them a job and something harder to try uses that energy instead of fighting it, and keeps the other kids in the game.

### 6. Ending practice well
**What to do:**
- **End with the most fun game,** not a drill or a lecture. Examples: "Coach Pitch Home Run Derby" (one swing each, run all the bases), or "Pick-Up Race" (who can put the most balls in the bucket).
- **Do a 60-second huddle:** "What was your favorite thing today?" (Take 2–3 answers.) Then "Next time, we'll ___."
- **Do a team cheer** with hands in.
- **Hand each kid off to a parent with one good thing:** "She stepped on every throw today!" Parents remember this, and kids hear it again at home.
- **Have the kids help with cleanup:** "Everyone grab 2 cones!"

**Why it works:** Kids remember how practice ended most. A good ending makes them want to come back, which matters more than any drill.

### 7. Game-day dugout management
**What to do:**
- **Use a lineup card** taped up in the dugout with names *and* pictures or colors for non-readers. Assign one "on deck" parent to help.
- **Set up a helmet station:** helmets lined up in batting order on the bench or a fence. "Batter, on deck, in the hole" is three kids, all with helmets on.
- **Make a bat-safety zone:** bats stay in the bat bag or rack. Nobody touches a bat until they're on deck. Draw a line in the dirt if you need to.
- **Use a dugout parent** whose only job is to keep kids seated, hand out water, and track the lineup.
- **Give dugout jobs:** cheer leader (leads one team cheer per inning), glove buddy (hands gloves out when it's time to field), out counter.
- **Run fast transitions:** "When I yell 'Gloves on!', run to your position. Let's see if we can beat 30 seconds!"
- **Keep snacks and water routines simple:** water bottles labeled and lined up; snacks only after the game.

**Why it works:** Dugouts are where things fall apart (lost helmets, swinging bats, crying over turns). Clear jobs and a set routine keep kids safe and the game moving, and every kid gets a moment to be in charge.
