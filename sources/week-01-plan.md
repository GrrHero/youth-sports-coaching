# Week 1 Coaching Plan — Greg + the Boys (ages 7 & 5)
*Baseball · Soccer · Flag Football · Athleticism*

---

## Week 1 at a Glance

**Theme: "Body control and first touches."**
This week isn't about making them great at anything. It's about three things: (1) they love coming outside with you, (2) they learn how a "Dad practice" works (game → learn one thing → game), and (3) they get a first, simple version of each key skill.

**Progression goals by the end of the week (signs it's working, not a test):**

| Skill | 7-year-old | 5-year-old |
|---|---|---|
| Balance | Stands on one foot 10 sec each side | 5 sec each side |
| Running | "Cheek to cheek" arms (hand swings from cheek of face to cheek of bum) most of the time | Arms swinging opposite to legs (no "T-rex arms") |
| Throwing | Side-on, steps toward the target, throws overhand ~30 ft | Points glove at target, steps, throws overhand ~15 ft |
| Catching | Catches soft tosses with the glove, fingers up above the waist | Catches a big/soft ball with two hands, "eyes on it" |
| Soccer | Dribbles with small touches, stops it with the sole of the foot on command | Keeps the ball close while walking/jogging, stops on "red light" |
| Flag football | Pulls a flag from a jogging target, carries the ball "high and tight" | Pulls a flag from a walking target |

**What you need:** soft balls (tennis balls, a foam ball, a beach ball), gloves, a tee + light bat, a size 3 soccer ball (fine for both ages), a small football (junior/"pee-wee" size), 2 flag belts (or socks/bandanas tucked in a waistband), 6–10 cones (shoes, water bottles and paper plates all work).

## Part 1: How to Teach (Read This First)

You already know how to play. Teaching is a different skill, and it's mostly a few habits. Here's the playbook you'll use in every session.

### The 6 rules that make young kids learn

1. **Play → Practice → Play (whole-part-whole).** Start with a game that uses the skill, pull out one small piece to work on, then go straight back to the game.
   *Why it works:* kids learn movement by doing the whole thing. Pulling out one piece briefly gives focus, and going back to the game is where it sticks. Starting with a game also earns their buy-in before you "teach."

2. **One cue at a time.** A 5-year-old can hold about one instruction at a time and a 7-year-old maybe two. Choose one cue word per activity and repeat it. Leave everything else alone, even if it's wrong.
   *Why it works:* young brains get overloaded fast. One focused fix beats five half-fixes.

3. **Show, don't tell (the silent demo).** Show it 2–3 times *without talking*, then once more with the cue word: "Watch my feet... [demo]... *step*!" Face the way they'll face (stand beside them, not across from them) so they don't have to mirror you.
   *Why it works:* kids copy what they see far better than they follow what they hear.

4. **Ask, don't always tell (guided discovery).** "What happened when you stepped toward me?" "Which way did the ball go when you used your toe?" Let them try two ways and tell you which worked better.
   *Why it works:* when they find the answer themselves, they remember it and they feel smart.

5. **Praise effort and the specific thing.** Say "I loved how you stepped toward me!" instead of "Good job." Say "You kept trying even after that drop. That's what athletes do." Aim for at least 4 positive comments for every correction.
   *Why it works:* praising effort builds kids who keep trying (a "growth mindset"). Specific praise tells them exactly what to repeat.

6. **Short and switching.** Each activity takes 4–7 minutes. Stop *while it's still fun*, before it falls apart. Attention spans run roughly 2–3 minutes per year of age, so about 10–15 min for the 5yo and 15–20 for the 7yo even when they're into it, and much less when they're tired.
   *Why it works:* ending on a high keeps them asking for "Dad practice" tomorrow.

### The Coaching Loop (use it for every skill)
**Game → Notice → Show → Cue → Try → Praise → Game**
1. Let them play the game.
2. Notice the *one* most important thing to fix.
3. Show it silently.
4. Say the cue word.
5. They try it 5–10 times.
6. Praise the specific improvement, or the effort if it's not there yet.
7. Back to the game. Say the cue during play as a reminder: "*Step!*"

### How to correct without deflating them
- **Sandwich it:** "Great hustle! → Let's try pointing your glove this time. → I knew you'd get it!"
- **Fix one thing, then let it go.** If the throw is better but the foot is still wrong, celebrate the throw.
- **Use "try" language:** "Let's try..." and "Can you show me...?" instead of "No, that's wrong."
- **Turn it into a challenge:** "Bet you can't keep it close enough to stop it on 'red light'!"

### Running two ages at once
- **Same game, different rules.** The 5yo stands closer, gets a bigger ball and gets more tries. The 7yo gets a smaller target or a time limit.
- **Compete against yourself, not your brother.** Track personal bests ("Last time you got 6 — can you get 7?") rather than head-to-head races, which the 5yo will usually lose.
- **Give the 7yo a "coach" job sometimes.** "Can you show your brother how you step?" It builds confidence and checks that he understands. Keep it short and supervised so it doesn't turn into bossing.
- **When they do go head-to-head, handicap it** (the 7yo hops, or starts farther back) so it's actually close.

| Block | Time | What |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up game | 4–5 min | Movement game (tag, animal walks), no instruction |
| Skill 1 (Loop) | 6–8 min | Game → one cue → game |
| Water / silly break | 1–2 min | Let them talk; ask what was fun |
| Skill 2 (Loop) | 6–8 min | Game → one cue → game |
| Finale game | 4–5 min | Biggest fun of the day, uses that day's skills |
| Huddle | 1 min | "What was your favorite part? What did you get better at?" High fives. |

## Session 1 — Athlete Day + Throwing Intro (25 min)

### Warm-up: Animal Walks (5 min)
Bear crawl, frog jumps, crab walk and flamingo stand (one foot), 10–15 yards each. Call out the animals in random order.
- **Why:** builds the strength, balance and coordination that every sport needs, and it doesn't feel like practice.
- **5yo:** shorter distances, and it's fine if the crab walk falls apart.
- **7yo:** "Can you bear crawl backward?"
- **Fun:** you do it too. Kids LOVE seeing Dad bear crawl.

### Skill 1: Running Form — "Cheek to Cheek" (8 min)
**Break it down:**
1. **Arms only (sitting):** Sit on the grass, legs out, and pump the arms. The hand swings from the cheek of the face back to the cheek of the bum. Elbows bent like an "L."
2. **Arms + march:** Stand up and march in place. Opposite arm goes with the opposite knee (right arm forward when the left knee is up).
3. **Jog:** Jog 15 yards with the arms working.
4. **Run:** Short races, 15–20 yards.

**Cue words:** "Cheek to cheek." Later (7yo, or next week): "Knees up."

**Demo:** Stand beside them (both facing the same way), pump your arms slowly and exaggerate it. Then do the "wrong" way (T-rex arms, or arms swinging across your body) and ask, "Which one looks faster?"

**Common errors and fixes:**
| Error | Looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| T-rex arms | Arms tight to the chest, barely moving | "Cheek to cheek!" Have them sit and pump (step 1) again |
| Windshield wipers | Arms swinging across the body | "Swing like you're drawing on the wall beside you." Stand them next to a fence or wall and say, "Don't touch it!" |
| Same arm/same leg | Rhythm looks off | Go back to a slow march and count "right arm, left knee" |
| Head bobbing or looking back | Slows down, wobbles | "Eyes on the finish line." Put a cone or Dad at the finish |
| Flat, slappy feet | Loud stomping | "Quiet, sneaky feet." Run like a ninja |

**5yo vs. 7yo:** The 5yo only needs arms moving opposite the legs, and "Cheek to cheek" can wait. The 7yo works on cheek to cheek plus a quick start: "Lean like you're about to fall, then go!"

**Fun:** "Beat the Ball." You roll a ball and they race it to a cone. Or "Dad's Shadow": they copy your running style, then you switch to silly styles (robot, penguin) and back to "fast style."

### Skill 2: Throwing Intro — "Point, Step, Throw" (8 min)
**Break it down (do the part, then the whole):**
1. **Side-on:** Stand like you're "looking over your glove shoulder." Glove-side shoulder points at the target.
2. **Point:** Glove arm points at the target ("aim the laser").
3. **Arm back and up:** Throwing hand goes back and up, ball away from the head ("show the ball to the fence behind you").
4. **Step and throw:** Step toward the target with the glove-side foot and throw overhand.
5. **Follow-through:** Throwing hand finishes by the opposite knee or pocket.

**Cue words:** "Point, step, throw." (The 5yo might only get "Point!")

**Demo:** Stand *beside* them facing the target, the same way they face. Do 3 silent throws slowly, exaggerating the point and the step. Then: "Watch my glove hand... *point*... and my front foot... *step*... *throw*!"

**Common errors and fixes:**
| Error | Looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Facing the target square | Chest faces target, arm-only push throw | Put a line on the ground (rope, chalk, hose). Feet go *on* the line, which points at the target. Say "Look over your shoulder" |
| Wrong foot steps | Steps with the throwing-side foot | Put a sock or cone in front of the correct foot: "Step on the sock." |
| No step at all | Throws standing still | "Step on the sock" again, or "Step like you're stepping over a puddle" |
| Pushing/shot-put throw | Ball starts by the ear and gets pushed | Practice "show the ball to the fence behind you." Kneeling throws (on the throwing-side knee) isolate the arm |
| Sidearm | Arm comes around flat | "Throw over the top, like throwing over a fence" (have a real fence or bench to throw over) |
| Throws soft and careful | Lobs | Encourage hard throws at a big target like a fence or wall. Accuracy comes later |

**5yo vs. 7yo:**
- **5yo:** Tennis or foam ball, 10–15 ft away from a BIG target (garage door, fence). Only cue: "Point!" Celebrate any step. Catching will be hard for him, so throw at targets instead of to a partner.
- **7yo:** Real or soft baseball, 20–30 ft, a smaller target (a bucket, a chalk circle). Cues: "Point, step, throw." Then add a challenge: "Hit me in the chest 3 times in a row."

**Fun:** "Knock Down the Castle." Stack empty plastic bottles or cups on a bench or bucket. Points for each one knocked down. The 5yo stands closer. You keep score and narrate like a ballgame announcer.

### Finale: Freeze Tag with Flamingos (4 min)
When tagged, you freeze on one foot like a flamingo. A teammate unfreezes you with a high five. Balance work disguised as tag.

### Huddle
"What made the throws go farther?" (Guided discovery: let them say "stepping!")

## Session 2 — Soccer Dribbling + Balance (25 min)

### Warm-up: Body Part Simon Says with a Ball (4 min)
Each boy has a ball. "Simon says touch the ball with your knee... elbow... bottom!" Then "Simon says dribble... STOP the ball with your foot!"
- **Why:** gets them touching the ball a lot, and they're listening without noticing.

### Skill 1: Dribbling — "Small Touches" (8 min)
**Break it down:**
1. **Walk it:** Walk while tapping the ball with the inside or outside of the foot. The ball stays "on a leash," no more than a big step away.
2. **Jog it:** Same thing at a jog.
3. **Stop it:** On "red light," put the sole of the foot on top of the ball ("put the ball to sleep").
4. **Turn it:** Pull it back with the sole or tap it around (7yo).

**Cue words:** "Small touches." "Ball on a leash." "Foot on top!" (for the stop).

**Demo:** Walk slowly with tiny taps, then do the "wrong" version: one big kick that sends the ball flying, then chase it. Ask: "Which way can I stop it faster?"

**Common errors and fixes:**
| Error | Looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Big toe-kicks | Ball flies 15 ft, kid chases it | "Small touches, like the ball is a puppy on a leash." Make the area smaller |
| Toe pokes | Ball goes wobbly or sideways | "Use your laces or the side of your foot, not your toe." Guided discovery: "Try toe, then side. Which goes straighter?" |
| Head down the whole time | Runs into things | Hold up fingers: "How many fingers?" They have to look up to answer |
| Can't stop it | Ball rolls away on red light | Practice standing still: "Tap, tap, foot on top." |
| Only uses one foot | Fine for now | Leave it this week. Next week add "can you use both feet?" |

**5yo vs. 7yo:** The 5yo walks or jogs it, and stopping on command is a big win. The 7yo jogs and adds a pull-back turn on "U-turn!", and you can time how many crossings he makes in 30 seconds.

**Fun: Red Light / Green Light / Yellow Light** (see the Cal South video below). Green = fast dribble, yellow = slow, red = foot on top. Add "Purple light = dribble with your other foot" or "Honk your horn if you get close to another car!"

### Water break (1–2 min)

### Skill 2: Balance Challenges (6 min)
**Break it down:** Stand on one foot → one foot with eyes closed (7yo) → one foot while catching a soft toss → hopping.
**Cue words:** "Be a statue." "Tummy tight."
**Demo:** You stand on one foot and wobble on purpose, then "tighten your tummy" and get steady. Kids love seeing Dad wobble.
**Common errors:** Looking down (fix: "Look at my hand" and hold it at eye level). Holding their breath or freezing up (fix: "Breathe like a balloon").
**5yo vs. 7yo:** 5yo: arms out like an airplane, 5 seconds. 7yo: arms crossed, 10 sec, eyes closed for 3.
**Fun:** "Flamingo Contest." Who can balance longest while Dad tries to make them laugh (no touching)? Count out loud.

### Finale: Sharks and Minnows (5 min)
Each boy dribbles a ball (the minnows). Dad is the shark, with no ball, who tries to kick their balls out of the square. If you lose your ball, you get it back and keep going, so no one is eliminated.
- **5yo:** Dad is a slow "baby shark."
- **7yo:** Dad is a "great white" (a bit faster).

### Huddle
"How did you keep the ball away from the shark?" (Guided discovery: they'll say "keep it close!")

## Session 3 — Catching + Tee Hitting (25–30 min)

### Warm-up: Balloon or Beach Ball Keep-Up (4 min)
Keep a balloon or beach ball off the ground using hands. Count the hits.
- **Why:** builds tracking ("eyes on the ball") with a slow object. Zero fear.

### Skill 1: Catching — "Eyes on It, Fingers Up" (8 min)
**Break it down:**
1. **Fear-free first:** Use a newspaper ball, foam ball or tennis ball. Bare hands.
2. **Ready position:** Hands out in front, "show me your pancake" (open hands).
3. **Above the waist:** Fingers point up and thumbs come together ("make a diamond").
4. **Below the waist:** Fingers point down and pinkies come together.
5. **Glove (7yo):** Same thing with the glove. "Watch it go into the glove," then cover it with the throwing hand ("close the alligator's mouth").

**Cue words:** "Eyes on it!" "Fingers up!" (for high balls), "Alligator!" (glove + cover).

**Demo:** You catch a soft toss from the other boy (or toss one up to yourself). Exaggerate watching it all the way in, then say "I watched it all the way into my hands."

**Common errors and fixes:**
| Error | Looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Turning the head or closing the eyes | Flinching | Switch to a softer ball (newspaper or foam). "Tell me what color the ball is when it hits your hands" (or draw a smiley face on it and ask them to see it) |
| Fingers pointing at the ball | Ball jams the fingers or bounces off the heel of the glove | "Fingers to the sky!" Practice bare hand, one hand, fingers up (DNA Sports video below) |
| Trapping against the chest | Hugging the ball | OK for the 5yo for now. For the 7yo: "Catch it out in front, like your hands are a basket" |
| Glove stays closed or flat | Ball bounces out | "Open the alligator's mouth" before the throw |
| Stiff arms | Ball bounces off | "Soft hands, like catching an egg." Toss an actual water balloon outside for fun |

**5yo vs. 7yo:**
- **5yo:** Big foam ball or beach ball, two hands, gentle underhand tosses from about 5 ft. Celebrate every catch like it's the World Series.
- **7yo:** Tennis ball → glove. Underhand, then soft overhand from 10–15 ft. Mix glove side, middle and backhand side.

**Fun:** "Catch Ladder." Every catch = take one step back. A drop = one step forward. "How far can you get?" (Personal best each day.)

### Skill 2: Tee Hitting — "Squish the Bug" (8 min)
*This is a first taste only; the full swing breakdown comes next week.*
**Break it down:**
1. **Stance:** Feet wider than the shoulders, standing beside the tee. The tee is even with the belly button, about a bat length away (check by touching the bat to the far edge of the plate or tee).
2. **Hands:** Knuckles lined up (or just "hands together, glove hand on the bottom").
3. **Load:** Bat back by the back shoulder ("bat on your shoulder, then lift it off").
4. **Swing:** Rotate the back foot and "squish the bug" under the back toe as the hips turn.
5. **Finish:** Bat ends over the front shoulder.

**Cue words:** "Squish the bug." "Eyes on the ball."

**Demo:** Slow-motion swing, with an exaggerated back-foot squish. Then a "statue swing": freeze at the finish and say "Belly button faces the pitcher!"

**Common errors and fixes:**
| Error | Looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Hands crossed/backward | Wrong hand on top | Quick check: "Glove hand on the bottom." Put a sticker on the knob hand |
| Chopping down | Hits the tee, not the ball | Lower the tee slightly. "Swing flat, like cutting a table in half" |
| Standing too close/far | Hits with the handle or the tip | Measure with the bat: "Touch the ball with the end of the bat. That's your spot" |
| Back foot planted | All arms | "Squish the bug!" |
| Eyes up early | Misses the ball | "Watch the bat hit the ball." A sticker on the ball to look at helps |

**5yo vs. 7yo:** The 5yo uses a big ball or whiffle ball on the tee with a light, short bat, and making contact is the win. The 7yo uses a regular ball and aims at targets ("hit it past the cone").

**Fun:** "Home Run Derby." Cones at different distances score 1, 2 or 3 points. The 5yo also hits a newspaper ball or a balloon off the tee for laughs (Schupak video below).

**SAFETY:** Only one person near the batter. The other boy stands behind a "safety line" (cone) well away from the swing. Make it a big rule from day 1: "Bat is a tool, not a toy."

### Finale: Run the Bases (4 min)
Hit off the tee, then run to "first base" (a cone) while Dad fields and tries to tag the base. Running form cue: "Cheek to cheek!"

### Huddle
"What helped you catch more balls today?"

## Session 4 — Flag Football Day (25 min)

### Warm-up: Tail Tag (5 min)
Everyone tucks a sock or flag in a back pocket or waistband. Try to grab the others' tails while protecting your own, in a small area.
- **Why:** builds dodging, change of direction and flag-pulling instincts before you've taught anything.

### Skill 1: Flag Pulling — "Break Down, Grab, Rip" (8 min)
**Break it down:**
1. **Eyes on the hips:** Watch the belt (the hips), not the head or ball, because hips don't fake.
2. **Break down:** As you get close, take short "choppy" steps, bend the knees, and get low like a goalie.
3. **Grab:** Grab the flag near the belt.
4. **Rip:** Pull it straight down and away, then hold it up high and yell "FLAG!"

**Cue words:** "Eyes on the belt." "Chop, chop, GRAB." "Hold it up!"

**Demo:** Have the 7yo walk slowly past you wearing flags. Show the choppy steps and the low position, grab, rip, hold up. Then do the "wrong" version: run full speed, reach and miss. Ask: "Why did I miss?"

**Common errors and fixes:**
| Error | Looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Diving or reaching early | Lunges and misses | "Chop, chop, then grab." Practice slowing down on a cone before the grab |
| Grabbing the shirt or body | Holding, pushing | Clear rule: "Flags only. Grab the flag, not the friend." Restart if it happens |
| Watching the ball or head | Gets faked out | "Eyes on the belt buckle" |
| Running past the runner | Overshoots | Practice the "breakdown" (stop low like a goalie) on your whistle |
| Pulls weakly, flag stays | Grabs but doesn't pull | "Rip it down like starting a lawnmower." |

**5yo vs. 7yo:** The 5yo pulls flags from Dad *walking*, then jogging slowly. The 7yo pulls from a jogging Dad who changes direction once. Try the Zig Zag drill from the NFL FLAG video: stationary "targets" with flags, and he runs to each, breaks down and pulls.

**Fun:** "Flag Collector." Dad wears 4–6 flags (socks in the waistband). The boys try to collect them all in 60 seconds. Count each boy's own total and try to beat it next round.

### Skill 2: Running with the Ball + Simple Catch (6 min)
**Break it down:**
1. **Carry:** "High and tight." Ball tucked against the chest/side, hand covering the point ("hide the ball").
2. **Catch (big soft ball or junior football):** Hands out, "make a diamond" with thumbs and pointers for chest-high balls, and "look it in."
3. **Run with it:** Catch, tuck, run to a cone.

**Cue words:** "Hide it!" "Diamond hands!"

**Common errors:** Carrying the ball like a loaf of bread, swinging it (fix: "Squeeze it so the shark can't take it," then poke at it gently to test). Catching with the body (fix for the 7yo: "hands first, then tuck." For the 5yo, body catches are fine this week).

**5yo vs. 7yo:** The 5yo uses a foam ball, short underhand tosses, and any catch counts. The 7yo uses a junior football and runs a simple "go to the cone and turn around" before the catch.

### Finale: Mini Game (5 min)
Dad is quarterback for both teams. Each boy takes turns catching (or taking a handoff) and running for the "end zone" while the other boy tries to pull his flag. Switch every play.
- Handicap: the 7yo starts farther back when the 5yo is the runner, or the 5yo gets "two-hand touch counts" instead of needing a flag pull.

### Huddle
"How did you get close enough to grab the flag?" (Guided discovery: "slowing down!")

## Session 5 — "Backyard Olympics" (Fun Friday, 25–30 min)
*Purpose: show off the week's skills in games, celebrate, and quietly see where each boy is. No new teaching. Just cue reminders.*

Run 5–6 stations of 3–4 min each, one boy at a time or both at once. Keep score on a whiteboard or piece of paper, but **score each boy against his own Monday baseline**, not against his brother.

1. **Flamingo Stand:** Longest one-foot balance (each foot). *Cue: "Statue!"*
2. **Sprint Race:** 20 yards, "cheek to cheek." Handicap the 7yo (he starts 5 yards back, or starts sitting down).
3. **Castle Knockdown:** Throwing at bottles/cups. *Cue: "Point, step, throw."*
4. **Catch Ladder:** How far back can you get? *Cue: "Eyes on it."*
5. **Dribble Gates:** Set up 4 "gates" (two cones each) and dribble through as many as possible in 45 sec. *Cue: "Small touches."*
6. **Flag Pull Challenge:** Pull Dad's flags in 30 sec.

**Medal ceremony:** Hand out "awards" for effort and character, not just results: "Never Gave Up Award," "Best Listener Award," "Biggest Improvement Award." Make sure each boy gets one, and that they're true.

**Why it works:** kids need to feel progress. A celebration day locks in "I'm getting better, and practice is fun," which is the whole goal of Week 1. It also shows you what to focus on next week.

## Next Week Progression (Week 2 Preview)

Keep what works, add one layer per skill. **Only move a boy forward when he's doing the Week 1 cue about 70% of the time.** Otherwise, repeat with a new game (same skill, new fun).

| Skill | Week 2 add-on (7yo) | Week 2 add-on (5yo) |
|---|---|---|
| Running | Starts: "fall and go" lean start, and races with a turn | Cheek to cheek arms |
| Throwing | Hip turn ("belly button to the target" at the finish); add distance | Step + point consistently; throw to Dad (not just targets) |
| Catching | Glove side vs. backhand; moving to the ball; "alligator" two-hand cover | Switch from the big ball to a tennis ball, two hands |
| Hitting | Full swing breakdown: load, stride, squish; soft front toss | Tee contact; "squish the bug" |
| Soccer | Inside-of-foot passing ("ankle locked, hit the middle"); both feet dribbling | Dribble around cones; first kicks at a goal |
| Flag football | Simple routes (go, stop, in/out); QB grip | Catching a soft football; carrying "high and tight" |
| Athleticism | Skipping, hopping on one foot, side shuffles | Skipping, jumping and landing "quiet like a ninja" |

**Also in Week 2:** Start letting each boy choose one game per session (ownership = buy-in), and try one "teach-back" a session where the 7yo shows the skill to his brother.

## Part 2: Volunteer Coaching at the Baseball Fields

Coaching a group is different from coaching your own two. Here's what works with groups of 8–15 kids ages 4–8.

### 1. Keep attention: the group control basics
- **Have a "freeze" signal and practice it first.** For example, "When I say 'Baseball,' you say 'Freeze!' and put your hands on your head." Practice it like a game in the first 2 minutes. It works far better than yelling.
- **Talk to them for 30 seconds or less.** Demo + one cue + "Go!" If you're talking longer than that, you've lost the 5-year-olds.
- **Kids face away from distractions.** When you talk, put yourself in front of the sun or the parking lot so they face away from it (and away from other teams). Have them sit down or "take a knee" so they're not wandering.
- **Get on their level.** Kneel down when you talk to them. It changes everything.
- **Names, names, names.** Learn every name on day 1 (name tags or tape on helmets help). "Nice step, Sofia!" lands much better than "Nice step, kiddo."
- **No lines longer than 3.** Long lines are where trouble happens: kids pick dandelions, wrestle and tune out. If a line is longer than 3, split it into two stations.

### 2. Stations: the secret weapon
With 2–4 helpers (other parents), break into small groups and rotate every 5–7 minutes.

**Sample 4-station rotation (30 min):**
| Station | Activity | Helper's one cue |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Throwing | Throw at a target (bucket, fence) | "Point, step, throw" |
| 2. Catching | Soft toss, "catch ladder" | "Eyes on it" |
| 3. Hitting | Tee hitting into a net/fence (*safety line!*) | "Squish the bug" |
| 4. Running | Run the bases, races, "Beat the Ball" | "Cheek to cheek" |

**Station tips:**
- **Give each helper a card** with the station's one game, one cue and one safety rule. Most parent helpers have never coached, so a card makes them confident.
- **Rotate on a whistle,** always in the same direction (clockwise).
- **Hitting station = tightest safety rules.** Only the batter inside the "bat circle" (chalk or cones), everyone else behind the line, and the helper watches the batter only.

### 3. Managing mixed skill levels
- **Same activity, different difficulty (the "choose your challenge" approach):** Set targets at 3 distances or sizes: "Easy, medium, or hard?" Kids pick their own challenge. Strong kids get pushed, and new kids aren't embarrassed.
- **Adjust the ball:** a foam ball for kids who are nervous, a real ball for kids who are ready. Nobody needs to know why.
- **Pair kids carefully:** sometimes pair similar levels (so both succeed), and sometimes have a confident kid "help" a newer one (in short doses).
- **Have a challenge for the advanced kids** so they don't get bored: "Can you catch it with your eyes closed until I say 'open'?" "Can you hit it to the cone?"
- **Move the struggling kid closer, quietly.** Say "Let's try from right here," and don't say "since you're having trouble."

### 4. Handling frustration and tears
**For the upset or crying kid:**
1. **Get low and calm.** Kneel down and use a soft voice. "Hey, that was frustrating, huh?"
2. **Name the feeling.** "You're upset because you missed it. That's okay. Big leaguers miss a lot too."
3. **Give a little space or a job.** "Want to take a water break, then come back and help me with the cones?"
4. **Make it easier for the next try.** Move them closer, use a softer ball, give them a "win" fast.
5. **Praise the comeback.** "You came back and kept trying. That's what tough players do."

**Don't** say "Stop crying," "It's no big deal," or "Be a big boy/girl." It dismisses their feelings and usually makes it last longer.

**Common triggers and prevention:**
- **Getting out or losing:** In games, avoid elimination (in tag, kids get "unfrozen," never "out").
- **Getting hit by the ball:** Use soft balls early. If a kid gets hit, stop, check, comfort, and let them choose when to go back.
- **Waiting too long:** Short lines and stations fix most of it.
- **Tired, hungry or hot:** Water breaks every 15 min, and keep practices short.
- **Parents watching:** Some kids freeze when a parent is calling out tips. A friendly pre-season note helps: "Please let the coaches coach. Cheer for effort!"

### 5. Positive reinforcement that works
- **Catch them doing something right,** loudly, and as specifically as you can: "Wow, Jayden, you watched the ball all the way into your glove!"
- **Aim for 4–5 positives for every correction.** If you're correcting a lot, the drill's too hard. Make it easier.
- **Praise effort and choices, not talent.** Say "You hustled to first base!" rather than "You're a natural."
- **Team traditions:** a team cheer at the end, a "Hustle Award" (a sticker, or carrying the team bucket) for effort each practice, rotating so every kid earns it over a few weeks.
- **End with a highlight:** in the closing huddle, name one thing *each kid* did well (or a few kids each practice, so everyone gets it every 2–3 practices).

### 6. Age-appropriate language
**Use pictures, not mechanics talk.**

| Instead of saying... | Say... |
|---|---|
| "Rotate your hips" | "Squish the bug" / "Belly button to the pitcher" |
| "Shoulder to target" | "Look over your glove shoulder" |
| "Track the ball" | "Eyes on the ball" / "What color was it?" |
| "Extension through the throw" | "Throw it like you're throwing it over a fence" |
| "Soft hands" | "Catch it like an egg" |
| "Get in an athletic position" | "Ready like a goalie" / "Hands on your knees, bounce bounce" |
| "Don't swing at that" | "Wait for your pitch" (7–8yo) |
| "Lead foot" | "Step on the sock" (put a sock there!) |
| "Stop chasing the ball" (soccer) | "Small touches, ball on a leash" |

**Language habits:**
- Say what TO do, not what NOT to do. "Eyes on the ball" works better than "Don't look away," because kids hear the last words.
- Ask questions: "What worked?" "Show me how you did that!"
- Keep instructions to one or two at a time, and very short.
- Use their names and celebrate small wins out loud.

### 7. A sample 45-min group practice (tee ball / coach pitch)
1. **Arrival game (5 min):** Tag variations or "Ball Hunt" (scatter balls, kids collect them).
2. **Huddle (1 min):** Practice the freeze signal and name today's focus: "Today we're working on our throwing step!"
3. **Warm-up (5 min):** Animal walks, follow-the-leader running.
4. **Stations (24 min):** 4 stations × 6 min.
5. **Water break (2 min).**
6. **Team game (6 min):** Simple base-running race ("everyone runs to first, then second...") or a scrimmage.
7. **Closing huddle (2 min):** Praise a few kids by name, team cheer, high fives.

## Pocket Cheat Sheet (screenshot this)

**The Loop:** Game → Notice → Show (silent) → Cue → Try → Praise → Game
**Rules:** One cue at a time · Talk 30 sec or less · 4+ positives per correction · Stop while it's fun · Praise effort and the specific thing

**Cue words this week:**
- Running: "Cheek to cheek"
- Balance: "Be a statue" / "Tummy tight"
- Throwing: "Point, step, throw"
- Catching: "Eyes on it" / "Fingers up" / "Alligator"
- Hitting: "Squish the bug"
- Dribbling: "Small touches" / "Foot on top"
- Flag pulling: "Eyes on the belt" / "Chop, chop, GRAB"
- Ball carry: "Hide it!"

**When it's going badly:** Make it easier → make it a game → or end with the finale game. A happy 15 minutes beats a frustrating 30.
