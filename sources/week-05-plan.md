## 1. Week Overview

**Theme: Two players, one play.** Last week every activity had a target or an opponent to read. This week the target is a *teammate*. A first baseman has to finish the play his brother starts. A soccer pass only works if the passer runs to get it back. A QB on the move has to square up to a receiver who is also moving, and a defender has to run to where the ball carrier is *going*. Even the athleticism day adds a partner rope game and a first taste of basketball ball handling. Basketball and soccer may be coming up for the boys, so this week sneaks in some light basketball-friendly skills (dribble, chest and bounce pass, pivot) without turning it into a basketball week.

**What carries over from Week 4:**

| Week 4 skill | Week 5 next step |
|---|---|
| Field-and-fire to first (Tick-tock), Beat the Runner | **Playing first base**: foot on the bag, stretch to the throw, and **glove-down tags** at a base |
| Laser Gate front toss, Pumpkin spray hitting | **Short coach pitch** for the 7yo (front toss for the 5yo), still aiming at gates, now timing a real pitching motion |
| 1v1 moves: the Trick and the Slice | **2v1 and the give-and-go (one-two)**: when to dribble, when to pass |
| Lead passes to a landing spot | **QB on the move**: rolling out and throwing while moving |
| Backpedal and break on the ball | **Pursuit angles**: run to the meeting point and pull the flag from the front, not chase from behind |
| Hopscotch patterns, Pogo bounces | **Chalk ladder footwork** and **first jump rope** practice |
| (new) | **Basketball-friendly ball skills**: fingertip dribble, chest and bounce passes, the pivot |

**Suggested schedule** (move days around as needed; 25 to 30 minutes each):

**Heads-up on the clocks:** Daylight saving time ends Sunday, Nov 1, so it gets dark about an hour earlier this week (sunset around 5:40 pm in Central Texas). Plan weekday sessions right after school, or use the driveway under the porch light or the garage for Session 4 (ladder, rope and dribbling all work in a small space). Section 7D has a rainy-day/indoor plan for the team that works at home too.

**Three rules for the whole week:**
1. **Teach both jobs.** Every partner skill has a giver and a getter (thrower and first baseman, passer and runner, QB and receiver). Teach the *getter's* job first, since that's the one kids forget. Then swap jobs every few reps so each boy feels both sides.
2. **One cue word per activity.** Same as always: only that word while they're moving. Say it *before* the rep. If you catch yourself saying a sentence, stop and say the word.
3. **One level each.** Each session lists one level for the 5yo and one for the 7yo. Start them there. Move up after about 4 successes out of 5; move down after 3 misses in a row. Change the setup quietly (distance, speed, ball size). No discussion.

**How to teach this week (whole-part-whole).** Teaching is hard because you already *see* the whole play in your head, and the boys don't yet. Instead of building up from tiny pieces, try this three-step sandwich:
1. **Whole (2 minutes):** play a simple version of the real thing right away, even if it's messy. Example: a 2v1 against you before any passing drill. Watch for the *one* thing that breaks down most.
2. **Part (4 to 6 minutes):** pull out just that one piece and practice it with a silent demo and one cue word. Example: the give-and-go against a cone.
3. **Whole again (the game):** put it right back into the game. Now they have a reason to use the part, and you'll see it show up.

The skill-then-game order in each session already follows this. The new habit is the quick "whole" at the start of Skill Work A: 1 or 2 minutes of the real thing before you teach. You learn what to fix, and they learn why the drill matters.

**New this week, effort-praise scripts:** with partner skills, praise the *job*, not the kid's talent. Swap "You're a natural first baseman!" for "You kept your foot on the bag and stretched. That's why it was an out." Swap "Good job" for "You ran to get it back after your pass. That's a one-two!" Specific effort praise ("you did X, and that's why Y happened") makes kids keep trying when things get hard; talent praise ("you're so good") makes them avoid hard things so they don't stop looking good.

## 2. Session 1: Baseball: First Base Boss & Glove-Down Tags (28 min)

**Equipment:** 2 gloves, a base (or a towel or flattened cardboard) for first base and a second one for "second base," 10 to 12 soft balls (foam or soft-core "safety" balls for the 5yo, wiffle or tennis balls for the 7yo), 4 cones, a stuffed animal or a pillow (the "runner" for the 5yo's tags).

**Levels this session:**
- **5yo:** Plays first base on a towel. You toss soft underhand throws from 10 to 12 feet, right at his chest at first, then a little to each side. He keeps one foot on the towel and catches with two hands. Tags a stuffed animal you slide along the grass toward the base.
- **7yo:** Plays a real first base. Takes 30 to 40 foot throws from his brother or you, including throws a step off-line, and stretches toward each one with his glove-side foot. Tags you as you walk or "slide" into second (soft balls only).

> **Safety note for you:** use only foam or soft-core balls for the 5yo, and tennis or wiffle balls for any throw where a kid is catching from his brother; a wild throw from a 7yo is hard to read. For tags, **nobody slides on the driveway**: tag plays happen on grass, and *you* do the sliding (or slow-motion "pretend slides") so no kid's face or hands end up near a sliding foot. Teach the tagger to tag with the **back of the glove** with the ball squeezed inside, and to keep his own feet beside the base, not on top of it.

### Warm-up: Find the Bag (4 min) · Cue: **Bag**
- **Setup:** First base (towel or base) and a cone 4 big steps away toward where second base would be: that's where a first baseman stands while the pitch is thrown. You stand about 15 feet away, where a shortstop would be.
- **Steps:**
  1. The boy starts at the cone in a ready position (Popcorn from Week 3).
  2. You call "Grounder!" and point somewhere else on the field (not at him). That means the ball wasn't hit to him.
  3. He jogs (5yo) or sprints (7yo) to the bag, puts his **throwing-hand foot on the front edge of the bag**, turns his chest to face you, and shows a big glove target with his hands up.
  4. You check: foot on the bag, chest at you, glove up. Then back to the cone.
  5. 6 to 8 reps each; brothers can take turns being the "shortstop" who calls it.
  6. *7yo add-on:* call it from different places (third base side, second base side). He turns his chest to face the caller each time.
- **Silent demo:** Stand at the cone. Mime the ball being hit away with a look over your shoulder, then trot to the bag. Tap the front edge of the base with your throwing-side foot (exaggerate it: tap-tap), turn your chest to the "shortstop," and hold up a big glove like a stop sign. Point at your foot, then at your chest, then at the glove. Twice.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Stands on the middle of the towel with both feet. **Fix:** Totally fine at 5. "One foot on the bag, one foot ready to move." Put a sticker or a leaf on the front edge of the towel: "Your foot touches the sticker."
  - *5yo:* Faces you with his back to the bag and loses it. **Fix:** Walk it with him once, holding hands. Then make the bag big (a whole beach towel). Success first, precision later.
  - *7yo:* Stretches his foot out before there's even a throw. **Fix:** "Square first, stretch later." Count only reps where he's balanced and facing you with both feet under him before your throw.
  - *7yo:* Puts his glove-side foot on the bag. **Fix:** Don't explain left and right. Ask: "Which foot would let you reach farther toward the ball?" Let him try both and find it.
- **Make it fun:** You're the radio announcer: "Ground ball to short... and the first baseman is THERE!" Time him from the cone to a correct setup and try to beat his best.
- **Why it works:** Most 8U plays at first fail before the throw even happens: the first baseman isn't at the bag or isn't facing the thrower. Practicing the setup alone, with no ball, is a **part practice** step that makes the catch much easier. The "Bag" cue gives one clear picture: get there and get set.
- 🎥 **Video:** [First base footwork for youth players](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R1sbhgRzneQ) · *Verified title: "Youth Baseball 1st Base Footwork and Drills" (Antonelli Baseball).* A coach and an 8-year-old first baseman show sprinting to the bag, the throwing-hand foot on the front edge, squaring to the thrower and then stretching. Good for you; the 7yo can watch the first two minutes with you.

### Skill Work A: Stretch & Squeeze at First (8 min) · Cue: **Stretch**
- **Setup:** First base as before. The thrower (you at first, then the brother) stands 10 to 12 feet away (5yo catching) or 30 to 40 feet away (7yo catching), roughly where shortstop or third base would be. Two cones on each side of the thrower so you can vary where throws come from.
- **Steps:**
  1. **Whole first (1 to 2 minutes):** roll a grounder to the 7yo, he fields and throws to his brother at first, the way they did in Week 4. Watch the first baseman. Usually he's lunging early, off the bag, or catching one-handed.
  2. Now the part: first baseman sets up at the bag with Bag (foot on, chest square, glove up).
  3. You throw. **Only when the ball is in the air** does he step his glove-side foot toward the ball and reach his glove out to meet it: the **Stretch**.
  4. He catches it out in front with the throwing hand coming over to **squeeze** it. His back foot stays touching the bag.
  5. Throw some straight, some a step to the glove side, some a step to the throwing-hand side (he shuffles his foot on the bag to the other corner if needed).
  6. *5yo:* soft underhand tosses at his chest first. Then a little to each side. His "stretch" can be just a reach with both hands while his foot stays on the towel.
  7. *7yo:* real throws from his brother after a grounder (Field-and-Fire from Week 4), then from you overhand with some off-line.
- **Silent demo:** Have the 7yo throw to you. Set up on the bag, wait with your glove up, and only when the ball leaves his hand, take a big, slow step toward the ball, reach your glove out, catch and squeeze with your other hand over it. Freeze in the stretch with a big proud face, then point at your back foot still touching the bag. Show it once wrong (stepping out early, then the throw goes the other way and you can't reach it, with a dramatic "oops"), then right again.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Turns his head or closes his eyes as the ball comes. **Fix:** Softer ball and shorter toss. "Watch the ball land in your glove like a bird landing in a nest." Celebrate every catch, even trapped ones. If he still flinches, see Section 7C.
  - *5yo:* Steps off the towel to go get the ball. **Fix:** That's a natural instinct. Make it a game: "Hot lava everywhere except the towel. One foot always stays on the island."
  - *7yo:* Stretches before the throw, then can't adjust when the throw goes the other way. **Fix:** Say nothing, just throw to the other side a few times. He'll discover why to wait. Then ask: "When's the best time to stretch?"
  - *7yo:* Leans his head and chest way out over his front leg and loses balance. **Fix:** "Sink your legs, keep your eyes behind the glove." Put a ball cap on his head: if it falls off, he leaned too far.
- **Make it fun:** Every catch with a foot on the bag is an "OUT!" with a big umpire call from the thrower. Count outs in a row. Ten in a row = the first baseman picks the next song.
- **Why it works:** Waiting for the throw before stretching is a **perception-action** skill: you can only learn when to move by seeing real throws, including bad ones. Mixing throw locations is **variable practice**, which builds a more adaptable catcher than 20 perfect throws. Starting with a 1-minute "whole" version shows you (and him) exactly why the stretch matters.

### Skill Work B: Glove-Down Tags (6 min) · Cue: **Doorstep**
- **Setup:** A second base (towel or base) on grass. The tagger stands **beside** the base, not on it, on the side the throw is coming from. A "runner" starts 15 feet away: a stuffed animal or pillow you drag or slide along the grass (5yo), or you walking in or doing a slow-motion pretend slide (7yo).
- **Steps:**
  1. You (or the brother) toss the ball to the tagger at the base.
  2. He catches, squeezes the ball in his glove with his throwing hand covering it.
  3. He drops the glove straight down to the front of the base, the **Doorstep**: the spot where the runner has to arrive. Back of the glove facing the runner.
  4. The runner "tags himself" by sliding into the glove. Then the tagger pulls the glove up and away and shows the ball: "OUT!"
  5. 6 reps each, then swap.
  6. *5yo:* the stuffed-animal runner. No real runner at all for him this week.
  7. *7yo:* you are the runner. Walk at first, then a slow-motion "slide" on the grass where your foot arrives at the doorstep. Mix in a "sneaky" runner who goes for the back corner of the base so he has to move his glove.
- **Silent demo:** Stand beside the base. Catch a toss, squeeze it, then drop your glove straight down to the front edge of the base like closing a door. Freeze. Have the 7yo slide his hand into the glove like a runner. Pull the glove up, hold up the ball, and give a huge silent "out" signal (fist pump). Then show the wrong way once: reaching way out to chase the runner, with a goofy wobble.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Swipes at the runner instead of putting the glove down. **Fix:** "The doormat waits; the runner comes to you." Put a paper plate on the front edge of the base. The glove goes on the plate.
  - *5yo:* Drops the ball when the runner touches the glove. **Fix:** "Two hands squeeze the ball like a juice box." Practice catch-and-squeeze without a runner a few times.
  - *7yo:* Reaches up high to tag the runner's chest. **Fix:** Do your slow-motion slide *under* his high tag and call "Safe!" He'll figure out that the glove has to be at the bag.
  - *7yo:* Stands on top of the base and gets his feet in the way. **Fix:** "Base is for the runner." Put a cone where his feet belong, beside the base.
- **Make it fun:** You're a sneaky runner with silly names ("Here comes Speedy the Sock!"). For the 5yo, the stuffed animal "cries" dramatically when it's tagged out.
- **Why it works:** Kids naturally reach toward a runner, which is slow and misses slides. A fixed target (the doorstep) is an **external focus** that replaces a long explanation about glove angles. Using a stuffed animal for the 5yo takes the fear and the collision out of the skill completely; it's **scaled equipment**, the same idea as a lower hoop or a smaller ball.
- 🎥 **Video:** [How to tag a runner](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=etzt_j0KqEA) · *Verified title: "How to Properly Tag a Baserunner // 3 Simple Steps for Youth Players to Get 100% Outs!" (Building Better Baseball).* A youth coach's three-step tag (secure the ball, glove to the bag, let the runner tag himself). For you.

### Game: Bang-Bang at First (7 min) · Cue: **Bang**
- **Setup:** Home plate (you), a short "shortstop" spot 20 to 30 feet from first, and first base. One boy fields, the other plays first base. You are the hitter and the runner.
- **Steps:**
  1. You roll (or softly hit with a fungo-style tap) a grounder to the fielder, then run to first.
  2. Fielder fields and throws to first (Tick-tock from Week 4).
  3. First baseman uses Bag and Stretch to catch it with his foot on the base.
  4. Ball caught at the bag before you touch it = **OUT**, a point for the brothers' team. You beat it = **SAFE**, a point for you.
  5. Close plays are "bang-bang" plays, and the first baseman makes the call: "Bang-bang... OUT!"
  6. Swap jobs every 4 plays. First team to 7.
  7. *5yo at first:* you run slowly (backward or skipping); the 7yo's throw comes from 20 feet. *5yo fielding:* he throws from 15 feet and can take a step or two closer before throwing.
- **Silent demo:** Walk through one play in slow motion with no ball: point to the fielder, mime his throw, then hop to the bag yourself, stretch, catch and freeze as the slow "runner" (the 7yo) arrives. Then point at both brothers and yourself and hold up two fingers vs. one: "you two against me."
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* At first base, he runs toward the thrower to get the ball sooner. **Fix:** "The bag is your home. Outs only happen at home." Lay a hula hoop around the base: he must stay in the hoop.
  - *5yo:* Gets sad when Dad is safe. **Fix:** Run slower and make your "safe" calls rare. It's a team game, so cheer the brothers' outs like a stadium crowd.
  - *7yo:* Throws hard at his little brother and he can't catch it. **Fix:** "A good throw is a catchable throw. Your job is to make your brother look great." Only catches count as outs, so a hard, wild throw costs *his* team.
  - *7yo:* Argues calls. **Fix:** The first baseman's call stands, no matter who is at first. That's a nice lesson in trusting a teammate.
- **Make it fun:** Big stadium noise and a "bang-bang" chant on every close play. If the brothers win, they get to make you run the bases in slow motion while they "announce" you.
- **Why it works:** It joins three skills (field, throw, receive) into one team play, which is exactly what happens in a real game. The brothers are **teammates against you**, so the 7yo learns that his throw is only good if his brother can catch it. That's the week's theme in one game. Your running speed keeps the outs around 60 to 70 percent, so both boys stay motivated.

### Cool-down: Bear Hug Stretch (3 min) · Cue: **Hug**
- **Setup:** Standing on the grass, gloves off.
- **Steps:**
  1. Give yourself a huge bear hug, hands reaching around to your back. Hold 3 slow breaths. Open the arms wide, then hug again with the other arm on top.
  2. Hug one knee to your chest while standing (hold Dad's shoulder for balance). Then the other.
  3. Sit down and hug both knees, rocking gently.
  4. Question: "When was the right time to stretch for the throw?"
- **Silent demo:** Wrap your arms around yourself with a sleepy, happy bear face and sway a little. Then hug a knee and wobble on purpose before steadying.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Hugs too fast and falls over on the knee hug. **Fix:** Let him hold your hand, or do the knee hug sitting down.
  - *7yo:* Rushes through. **Fix:** "Bears hug for 3 slow breaths." Count out loud together.
- **Make it fun:** Finish with a real family bear hug with a growl.
- **Why it works:** It stretches the shoulders and upper back after throwing and the hips after lots of stretching at first base. The question gets him to put the key idea (wait, then stretch) into his own words, which is what makes it stick.

## 3. Session 2: Soccer: One-Two, Magnet & Two Against the Giant (27 min)

**Equipment:** 2 soccer balls (size 3), 8 to 10 cones, 2 small goals (pop-up goals or two pairs of shoes as posts), a wall, garage door or rebounder if you have one.

**Levels this session:**
- **5yo:** Passes to a **still** partner (you or the garage door) and runs to get the ball back, at walking-to-jogging speed. In the 2v1 game he's on a team with his brother, and you defend in "slow giant" mode.
- **7yo:** Plays the one-two around a cone, then around **you** as a live defender. In 2v1 he decides: dribble if you stay with the pass, pass if you come to him.

### Warm-up: Postman Pass-and-Move (4 min) · Cue: **Move**
- **Setup:** A square of 4 cones about 8 big steps apart: these are the "houses." Each boy stands at a house. You stand in the middle with one ball.
- **Steps:**
  1. You pass the ball (the "letter") to one boy.
  2. He stops it and passes it back to you with the inside of his foot (Lock from Week 2).
  3. As soon as the ball leaves his foot, he **moves** to a new, empty house. The next pass goes to his new address.
  4. Go back and forth between the boys. About 60 seconds a round, 3 rounds.
  5. *7yo add-on:* he has to move *before* you pass to him (he calls "Here!" when he's at his new house).
  6. *5yo:* he can pick up the ball and roll it back with his hands on the first round, then use his feet.
- **Silent demo:** Have the 7yo pass to you. Pass it back, then immediately jog to an empty cone, turn and open your arms like "I'm here." Point at your feet, then at the empty cone you ran to. Do it twice.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Passes, then stands and watches the ball. **Fix:** That's the most common habit in kids' soccer. Make a silly rule: "After your pass, the ground under you turns to lava." Point to an empty house.
  - *5yo:* Passes too hard and you have to chase it. **Fix:** "Mail is gentle so it doesn't break." Move closer.
  - *7yo:* Moves to a house where the pass is blocked by you. **Fix:** Stand in the way and say nothing. Ask: "Could I pass to you there?" He'll pick a spot where he can see you.
  - *7yo:* Kicks with his toe. **Fix:** Point at the side of your foot: "Lock," the Week 2 word. One reminder only.
- **Make it fun:** You're a cranky postman who complains when no one is home ("Nobody home again? I'll eat this letter!"). Count perfect deliveries in a row.
- **Why it works:** The give-and-go starts with one habit: **pass, then move**. Practicing it in a simple, no-pressure pattern turns it into a reflex before you add a defender. It also warms up passing and receiving with lots of touches.

### Skill Work A: Bounce-Back One-Two (7 min) · Cue: **One-two**
- **Setup:** A cone "defender" in the middle, a start line 5 big steps before it, and a pair of cones (a gate or small goal) 6 big steps past it. You stand off to one side of the cone, level with it, as the "wall." For the 5yo, the garage door or a rebounder can be the wall.
- **Steps:**
  1. **Whole first (1 to 2 minutes):** play a quick 2v1: both brothers against you. Watch what happens. Usually both kids dribble and nobody passes, or they pass and then stand still.
  2. Now the part: the boy dribbles toward the cone.
  3. Before he reaches it, he passes to you (pass number **one**).
  4. Then he runs past the other side of the cone.
  5. You pass the ball back into the space in front of him (pass number **two**). He collects it and dribbles or shoots through the gate.
  6. 6 to 8 reps, then switch sides so he passes with the other foot.
  7. *5yo:* he passes against the garage door (or to you standing still), runs to where it bounces back, and dribbles through the gate. Walking pace.
  8. *7yo:* after a few reps, you become a live defender who steps toward him. He passes to his brother (now the "wall") and runs past you for the return.
- **Silent demo:** Dribble slowly at the cone, pass to the 7yo standing beside it, hold up one finger. Run past the cone with a sneaky face, point at the open space in front of you, receive his pass and hold up two fingers. Then dribble through the gate and celebrate.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Passes, then waits for the ball to come back to where he is. **Fix:** "The ball comes back to where you're GOING." Put a "treasure spot" (a shoe) past the cone; he runs there after his pass.
  - *5yo:* Passes too early or too far from the wall. **Fix:** Move him closer. Celebrate any pass that hits the wall or reaches you.
  - *7yo:* Runs into the cone, or the same way as his pass. **Fix:** "Your pass goes one way around the defender, you go the other." Draw a little "V" in the air.
  - *7yo:* Passes from too far away, so the defender has time to recover. **Fix:** Ask: "Where's the best moment to pass, far away or right when the defender comes to you?" Let him test both.
- **Make it fun:** He's a pinball bouncing off the bumper (you or the wall). Make pinball sounds. Count one-twos that end in a goal.
- **Why it works:** The one-two (also called a wall pass) is the simplest way to beat a defender **without** dribbling past him, since the ball goes around one side and the player around the other. Starting with the 2v1 "whole" shows the boys the problem before they see the solution, which is **whole-part-whole** in action.
- 🎥 **Video:** [How to do a wall pass](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L7gfjaX2ve0) · *Verified title: "Coaching Soccer ~ How to do a Wall Pass + Online Soccer Academy" (Online Soccer Academy).* A clear coaching demo of the pass-run-receive pattern with a cone defender. The players are older, so it's for you.

### Skill Work B: 2v1 Magnet (6 min) · Cue: **Magnet**
- **Setup:** A channel 8 steps wide and 15 steps long. You (the defender) stand in the middle. The brothers start at one end with one ball, side by side and 4 to 5 steps apart. A small goal at the far end.
- **Steps:**
  1. The boy with the ball dribbles straight at you, like a **magnet** pulling you toward him.
  2. **If you come to him,** he passes to his brother, who is now free.
  3. **If you stay with his brother,** he keeps dribbling and shoots.
  4. Score = 1 point. You steal it = your point.
  5. Start in "slow giant" mode: you only walk, and you clearly commit to one boy. Then get sneakier.
  6. Swap who starts with the ball each rep.
  7. *5yo:* he can just dribble forward and pass when you say "Pass!" from a distance (a scaffold), then try deciding on his own.
  8. *7yo:* you start faking: lean toward him, then step back to the brother. He has to read your *body*, not your words.
- **Silent demo:** With the 7yo as the defender, dribble straight at him. When he steps toward you, pass to the 5yo and point at the open space. Next rep, when he stays with the 5yo, keep dribbling and shoot. Then point at the defender and hold up both hands like a scale: him coming to you = pass, him staying away = dribble.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Never passes, just dribbles into you. **Fix:** "Pass bonus": goals scored after a pass are worth 3. Then he'll pass every time, which is fine for a while. Decision-making comes later at 5.
  - *5yo:* Passes but his brother isn't ready. **Fix:** Teach the receiver's job: "Show your hands and say 'Here!'" (Rule 1: teach the getter's job first.)
  - *7yo:* Always passes or always dribbles, no matter what you do. **Fix:** Freeze for 10 seconds and ask: "Where am I standing? Who's free?" Then replay that moment.
  - *7yo:* Dribbles wide around you, so you never commit. **Fix:** "Magnets go straight at the defender." Narrow the channel.
- **Make it fun:** You're a slow, dramatic giant who gets dizzy when the boys pass around you, and falls over after a great pass.
- **Why it works:** A 2v1 is a **decision**, not a technique: pass or dribble depends on what the defender does. That kind of decision can only be learned against a live defender (**perception-action coupling**). Dribbling *at* the defender is the hidden key; it forces him to commit, which makes the decision easy to read.
- 🎥 **Video:** [2v1 coaching tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x4PA7k8AnhI) · *Verified title: "CATZCoach: 2v1" (AYSO Soccer).* An AYSO coaching clip on the 2v1: dribble at the defender, pass if he takes away the dribble, dribble if he takes away the pass. It's made for coaches and older players, so watch it yourself.

### Game: Two Against the Giant (7 min) · Cue: **Buddy**
- **Setup:** A field about 15 by 20 big steps. Two small goals at the far end, about 10 steps apart. You are the Giant. The brothers are a team with one ball.
- **Steps:**
  1. The brothers attack together against you, the Giant.
  2. A goal counts 1. A goal after a pass between the buddies counts 2. A goal after a one-two counts 3.
  3. If you win the ball, you dribble it back to the start line and the brothers try to win it back (2v1 defending).
  4. Rounds of 2 minutes, then a water break.
  5. *Handicaps:* the Giant has to keep one hand on his head (round 1), then walk only (round 2), then free (round 3, only if the boys are winning easily).
- **Silent demo:** Have the 7yo be the Giant for 20 seconds. You and the 5yo pass to each other around him with exaggerated "high-five" gestures after each pass. Point at the two goals.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Chases the ball wherever it goes, even when his brother has it. **Fix:** "Find a space where your buddy can see you." Point to the open space and say "Buddy!"
  - *5yo:* Upset when the Giant steals the ball. **Fix:** The Giant drops it after a 3-second roar and the boys get it back. Keep his success high.
  - *7yo:* Hogs the ball and doesn't pass to his little brother. **Fix:** Use the scoring: passes make goals worth more. If that's not enough, add "every goal needs a pass."
  - *7yo:* Gets frustrated with his brother's passes. **Fix:** Make him "the captain who makes his buddy look good." Praise him every time he gives an easy pass.
- **Make it fun:** Giant voice ("Fee-fi-fo-fum!"). When the brothers score, the Giant falls asleep for 5 seconds and snores.
- **Why it works:** This is a **small-sided game** with a scoring **constraint** that rewards passing. The brothers *want* to pass because it scores more, not because you told them to. That's the cleanest way to grow teamwork in young kids, and it's the same trick you can use with your team (see Week 4, Section 7A).

### Cool-down: Seesaw Stretch (3 min) · Cue: **Seesaw**
- **Setup:** The brothers sit facing each other on the grass, legs out in a "V," feet touching. They hold hands (or both hold a soccer ball between them).
- **Steps:**
  1. One leans back slowly while the other leans forward gently: the seesaw goes down on one side. Then switch.
  2. 6 slow seesaws, about 3 seconds each way.
  3. Let go and lie back for 3 slow breaths.
  4. Question: "When the Giant came to you, what did you do?"
- **Silent demo:** Sit with one boy and seesaw slowly with an exaggerated "wheee" whisper. Show it fast once and shake your head, then slow again and nod.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Pulls hard and his brother falls forward. **Fix:** "Seesaws are gentle." You sit behind him and slow the rhythm with your hands on his shoulders.
  - *7yo:* Turns it into a tug-of-war. **Fix:** "Can you make it so smooth that the ball doesn't wobble?" Use the ball between them.
- **Make it fun:** Sing "Row, Row, Row Your Boat" slowly while seesawing.
- **Why it works:** It's a partner stretch for the hamstrings and inner thighs after lots of passing and running, and it ends a teamwork day with teamwork. The question gets them to name the 2v1 decision rule in their own words.

## 4. Session 3: Flag Football: QB on the Move & Cut-Off Angles (27 min)

**Equipment:** Flag belts, 1 junior or foam football (foam for the 5yo), 8 to 10 cones, 2 hula hoops (or towels) as landing spots.

**Levels this session:**
- **5yo:** Walks along the rollout lane, **stops**, turns his chest to the hoop and throws (a stop-and-throw). As a defender, walks, then jogs, to a cone where he'll meet you, and pulls a flag.
- **7yo:** Jogs the rollout lane and throws **while still moving**, before he passes the second cone. As a defender, takes an angle toward the meeting point when you pick one of two finish cones late.

### Warm-up: Sidewinder Shuffle Toss (5 min) · Cue: **Sidewinder**
- **Setup:** Two lines of cones about 4 steps apart (5yo) or 6 steps apart (7yo), running 15 steps down the yard like a road with two lanes.
- **Steps:**
  1. You and one boy face each other, one in each lane.
  2. Both of you side-shuffle down the lanes together, like sidewinder snakes, staying across from each other.
  3. While shuffling, toss the ball back and forth (underhand for the 5yo; overhand "phone" throws for the 7yo).
  4. At the end, switch direction so he shuffles leading with his other side.
  5. 3 to 4 trips each.
- **Silent demo:** Shuffle sideways with wide, smooth steps, your chest facing your partner the whole time, and toss the ball while your feet keep moving. Freeze, point at your chest, then at your partner.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Stops his feet to throw or catch. **Fix:** Totally fine at first. Then "Snakes never stop!" Shorten the distance and slow the shuffle way down.
  - *5yo:* Crosses his feet and trips. **Fix:** "Feet are magnets that never touch." Slow it down to a walk-shuffle.
  - *7yo:* Turns his chest down the road (running forward) instead of facing you. **Fix:** "Sidewinders look at their friend." Throw only when his chest faces you.
  - *7yo:* Throws behind you as you move. **Fix:** "Lead me, like Week 4." Ask: "Where will I be when the ball gets there?"
- **Make it fun:** Hiss like a snake on every catch. Count catches in one trip and try to beat it.
- **Why it works:** Throwing and catching while moving sideways is new and awkward; doing it slowly in a warm-up **pre-teaches** the rollout throw in Skill Work A. It also warms up the hips and shoulders for the session.

### Skill Work A: Rollout Rocket Throws (7 min) · Cue: **Square**
- **Setup:** A QB start cone. A "rollout lane" marked by 2 cones going sideways from the QB: one 2 steps to his throwing-hand side, one 6 steps. A receiver (you or the brother) starts 5 steps in front of the lane. A hoop as a landing spot about 6 steps (5yo) or 10 steps (7yo) downfield.
- **Steps:**
  1. **Whole first (1 minute):** you rush him slowly from the side and say "Throw it!" Watch: most kids run away and throw backward, or throw off-balance with their chest pointing the wrong way.
  2. Now the part: on "Hut!", the QB rolls out along the lane, holding the ball up high by his chest with two hands.
  3. Before he throws, he turns his chest and front shoulder to the receiver: **Square**.
  4. Throw to the hoop ahead of the receiver (Week 4's lead pass).
  5. *5yo:* walks the lane, stops at the second cone, squares up and throws to the hoop with you walking toward it. A stop-and-throw is a real skill at 5.
  6. *7yo:* jogs the lane and throws *before* passing the second cone, without stopping. His feet keep moving; his chest turns.
  7. 6 throws rolling to his throwing-hand side. *7yo only:* then 4 rolling the other way, which is much harder (he has to turn his hips more).
- **Silent demo:** Roll out slowly with the ball high, then do an exaggerated turn of your chest toward the receiver, with your front shoulder pointing at the hoop. Throw and freeze, then point at your chest and the hoop: "both point the same way." Show once without squaring (throwing sideways across your body, ball fluttering), make a confused face, then the correct way again.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Throws while still facing the sideline, so the ball goes sideways. **Fix:** "Belly button looks at the hoop, then throw." Put a sticker on his shirt over his belly: "Show your sticker to the hoop."
  - *5yo:* Drops the ball while moving. **Fix:** Two hands on the ball up high. Walk the lane a couple of times with no throw at all: just carry and square.
  - *7yo:* Throws backward across his body when he rolls left (for a righty). **Fix:** Start with only his strong side until it's smooth. When you add the other side, ask: "How can you turn so your chest still points at him?"
  - *7yo:* Stops completely to throw. **Fix:** Put the second cone closer and add a fun rule: if he's still holding the ball when he reaches it, you "sack" him with a gentle flag pull.
- **Make it fun:** He names his plays ("Rocket Rollout!"). A catch in the hoop = touchdown (6 points); a catch anywhere else = 3.
- **Why it works:** In flag football, most 7- and 8-year-old QBs get "sacked" because they can only throw from a still stance. A rollout keeps the play alive. **Square** is an **external** cue (chest toward a target) rather than an internal one ("rotate your hips"), and external cues help kids learn faster. Starting with his strong side, then adding the other, is a **simple-to-complex** progression.
- 🎥 **Video:** [NFL FLAG QB position](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uBVATin-Blg) · *Verified title: "NFL FLAG Football Quarterback (QB) Position" (NFL FLAG).* Covers the one-step drop, pump fake and the rollout, plus the rule that the QB can't cross the line of scrimmage before throwing. The players are older, so it's for you.

### Skill Work B: Cut-Off Corner (6 min) · Cue: **Meet**
- **Setup:** A "T" of cones: you (the runner) start at the bottom of the T, and run straight up toward a finish cone 10 steps away. The defender starts at a cone 5 steps to the side and 2 steps ahead of you. A bright cone (or a hat) halfway along your path is the **meeting point**.
- **Steps:**
  1. On "Go!", you run toward the finish cone with flags on.
  2. The defender doesn't run *at* you. He runs to the **meeting point**, the place where your path and his path cross, and pulls your flag there.
  3. Start walking, then half speed, then (7yo) full speed. First get the path right, then add speed.
  4. *5yo:* walking, then jogging. You make sure he gets there in time. The meeting point stays marked.
  5. *7yo:* take the meeting-point cone away after a few reps. Then add a second finish cone off to the other side and pick one at the last second; he has to adjust his angle.
- **Silent demo:** Let the 7yo be the runner. Point at him, then at the meeting cone, and nod. Walk to the meeting cone and pull his flag as he arrives. Then show it the wrong way: chase him from behind, and when he reaches the finish cone first, shrug with a sad face. Then the right way again with a thumbs-up.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Runs straight at you and ends up chasing you. **Fix:** Put a toy or treat on the meeting cone: "Run to the treasure, then grab the flag." Walk it together, holding hands, once.
  - *5yo:* Reaches the cone, then freezes. **Fix:** "At the meeting place, grab!" Slow down so you arrive right when he does.
  - *7yo:* Overruns the meeting point and you cut back behind him. **Fix:** "Chop, chop, GRAB" from Week 1: short, quick steps as he arrives. Ask: "What happened to your feet right before I got past?"
  - *7yo:* Waits too long before starting his angle. **Fix:** Let him lose a few. Then ask: "When should you start running to the meeting place, early or late?"
- **Make it fun:** You're a runaway train and he's the bridge operator who has to close the gate before the train passes. Train sounds required.
- **Why it works:** Most missed flags at this age aren't speed problems; they're **geometry** problems: the defender runs at where the runner *is*. The marked meeting point is a **visual scaffold** you remove later, and walking it first gets the path right before speed makes it harder. It's the defensive mirror of last week's lead pass ("throw to where he's going" / "run to where he's going").
- 🎥 **Video:** [Pursuit angle drill](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VEQVQWmrSHs) · *Verified title: "Youth Flag Football Drill | Defense Pursuit Drill | Cut off the runner | Where they are going | flag" (Flag Football with Coach D).* A youth flag coach sets up a T-shaped pursuit drill and walks it, then half speed, then full speed, just like this. Useful for team practice too.

### Game: Scramble Showdown (6 min) · Cue: **Scramble**
- **Setup:** A line of scrimmage (cones) and an end zone 15 steps (5yo) or 20 steps (7yo) away. One boy is QB, the other is the rusher and then the defender. You are the receiver.
- **Steps:**
  1. On "Hut!", the rusher counts out loud "One Mississippi, two Mississippi, three Mississippi" from 5 steps away, then rushes the QB.
  2. The QB **scrambles** out on a rollout, squares up and throws to you somewhere down the field.
  3. If you catch it, the rusher turns into a defender: he takes a cut-off angle to the meeting point and tries to pull your flag before you reach the end zone.
  4. Touchdown = 6 for the QB. Flag pulled = 3 for the defender. A "sack" (QB's flag pulled before he throws) = 2 for the defender.
  5. Swap after every 2 plays.
  6. *Handicaps:* the 7yo rushing his brother counts to 5 Mississippi. The 5yo rushing his brother counts to 2.
- **Silent demo:** Walk one play in slow motion: you as QB rolling out and squaring up, the 7yo slowly rushing, a slow throw to the 5yo, then the 7yo cutting across to the meeting point. Point at each of the three jobs as it happens.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* As QB, panics and throws immediately. **Fix:** Fine. Make it a short, easy catch for you. Or give him a longer count.
  - *5yo:* As defender, chases you from behind. **Fix:** Say "Meet!" and point at a spot in front of you. Run slowly toward it so he can get there.
  - *7yo:* Crosses the line of scrimmage before throwing. **Fix:** "Real-game rule: throw from behind the line." A throw from past it doesn't count. That's an actual flag football rule, so it's good to learn now.
  - *7yo:* Rushes his little brother too hard and too soon. **Fix:** The count is the rule. A rusher who goes early gives the QB 6 points.
- **Make it fun:** Play-by-play commentary ("He's scrambling... he's looking... he FIRES!"). Celebrate every great angle with a "Cut off!" chant.
- **Why it works:** The rusher creates real **time pressure**, which is the reason a QB needs to roll out at all. Switching from rusher to pursuit defender in the same play teaches the boys to **transition**, a big idea in every invasion sport (soccer and basketball too).

### Cool-down: Kneel-Down Stretch (3 min) · Cue: **Kneel**
- **Setup:** On soft grass.
- **Steps:**
  1. "Victory formation": kneel on one knee like a QB taking a knee to end the game. Slowly shift your hips forward until you feel a stretch in the front of the down leg. Hold 3 breaths. Switch knees.
  2. On both knees, sit back onto the heels, arms stretched forward on the grass. 3 slow breaths.
  3. Question: "When you were the defender, where did you run, to where I was or where I was going?"
- **Silent demo:** Take a knee with the football held like you just won the Super Bowl, then slowly lean your hips forward with a calm face.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Wobbles on one knee. **Fix:** Let him hold the football on the grass for balance, or hold your hand.
  - *7yo:* Leans way forward with his back bent. **Fix:** "Tall like a QB, hips move, not your head."
- **Make it fun:** "The game's over, we won!" Do a quiet "crowd cheer" in whispers.
- **Why it works:** It stretches the hip flexors after lots of sprinting and shuffling, and the slow breaths bring the heart rate down. The question has him repeat the week's key defensive idea in his own words.

## 5. Session 4: Athleticism: Hot Feet, Rope Rookies & Dribble Traffic (28 min)

**Equipment:** A playground ball or junior basketball for each boy (a size 5 / 27.5" youth basketball for the 7yo; a bouncy playground ball for the 5yo), sidewalk chalk (or a rope ladder or sticks on grass), 2 jump ropes (sized so the handles reach the armpits when he stands on the middle; a longer rope for the "snake"), 3 pieces of colored paper (green, yellow, red), 4 cones. Sneakers on, flat driveway or patio.

**Levels this session:**
- **5yo:** Dribbles with **two hands** or does bounce-and-catch while walking. Chalk ladder with one foot in each box, then two feet in each box, at his own speed. Jump rope: hops over a rope lying on the ground, then "swing, stop, step over" with the rope.
- **7yo:** Dribbles with **one hand**, then the other, walking and changing speed. Chalk ladder with lateral and in-in-out-out patterns. Jump rope: single jumps with a slow turn, counting his best streak.

> **Safety note for you:** give each jumper a "rope bubble" (at least two big steps from anyone else), and the waiting brother stands well outside it. Ropes never go around necks or get used as a leash, and they're put away (not left on the ground) after the session. Do the ladder and rope on a flat, dry surface; stop the rope work when landings get loud or he starts missing a lot. Tired legs trip. If you do this in the garage after dark, clear the floor first.

### Warm-up: Dribble Traffic Cop (5 min) · Cue: **Push**
- **Setup:** An open driveway "road" with cones at each end. You're the traffic cop with three colored papers.
- **Steps:**
  1. Each boy dribbles his ball in place first: **push** the ball down with the soft pads of his fingers (not slapping with a flat palm), at about belly height.
  2. Then you hold up a color:
     - **Green:** dribble while walking down the road.
     - **Yellow:** dribble in place.
     - **Red:** catch the ball and hold it tight against the chest ("park the car").
  3. 60 to 90 seconds per round, 3 rounds.
  4. *5yo:* two-hand dribbles (push down, catch, push down), or bounce-and-catch. Walking only.
  5. *7yo:* one hand on green, switch hands on yellow. Last round: "speed bump" (a cone) he has to dribble around.
- **Silent demo:** Dribble slowly in place, exaggerating your fingers pushing down like pressing a big soft button. Show a flat-hand slap once with a loud *smack* and a frown, then go back to the soft push with a smile. Then walk-dribble and catch-and-hold on an imaginary red light.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Slaps the ball and it bounces away. **Fix:** Bigger, bouncier ball, and two hands. "Push it like you're pushing a doorbell." Celebrate 3 in a row.
  - *5yo:* Stares at the ball the whole time. **Fix:** Totally normal at 5. Don't fix it this week. The color game will make him peek up on his own.
  - *7yo:* Dribbles too high (above his chest) and loses it when walking. **Fix:** "Keep it at your belly button." Hold your hand at belly height as a "ceiling."
  - *7yo:* Only uses his strong hand. **Fix:** Weak-hand green lights get a "bonus round." Don't force it, reward it.
- **Make it fun:** Car horn noises, a siren for "red," and sometimes a "flashing yellow" (you wiggle the paper) where they dribble as low as they can.
- **Why it works:** Dribbling a ball while moving and watching a signal trains **hand-eye coordination** and **attention split** (ball and signal at the same time), skills that show up in every sport with a ball. It's a light, basketball-friendly warm-up that also warms up wrists and fingers for the jump rope later.
- 🎥 **Video:** [Kids' dribbling basics](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DN0K_zTxUxc) · *Verified title: "Basketball Dribbling Drills For 6-9 Year Olds" (Basketball Coach Allen).* Stationary dribbling, crossovers and high/low dribbles at the right level for the 7yo. You can watch it with him.

### Skill Work A: Chalk Ladder Lightning (7 min) · Cue: **Hot feet**
- **Setup:** Draw a ladder on the driveway with chalk: 8 to 10 boxes in a row, each about the size of one of his shoes heel to toe, plus a little extra (about 15 to 18 inches). On grass, use a rope ladder or sticks.
- **Steps:**
  1. **Run-through:** one foot in each box, light and quick, like the ground is hot.
  2. **Two feet in:** both feet touch in every box (right-left in, right-left in).
  3. *7yo:* **Sideways two-in:** standing beside the ladder, step sideways into each box with both feet.
  4. *7yo:* **In-in-out-out:** facing the ladder from the side, step in-in (both feet in the box), out-out (both feet back out), then the next box.
  5. 2 to 3 trips per pattern, walking back as rest. Quality first: no lines touched.
  6. *5yo:* only patterns 1 and 2, at his speed. Hold his hand on the first trip of each.
- **Silent demo:** Walk the pattern in slow motion first, pointing down at each box as your foot lands. Then do it quick and light, tiptoes barely touching like the ground is a hot pan, with an "ow-ow-ow" face. Show one heavy, stompy trip and shake your head.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Skips boxes or jumps two at a time. **Fix:** Make the boxes smaller and put a chalk dot in each one: "Squish every dot."
  - *5yo:* Gets frustrated with two-feet-in. **Fix:** Go back to the run-through and make it a race against the clock. Success first, then try two-in again next time.
  - *7yo:* Speeds up and gets sloppy (lines touched). **Fix:** "Clean, then quick." Only clean trips count toward his timed best.
  - *7yo:* Stands up tall and stiff. **Fix:** "Athlete stance: a little bend in the knees, arms ready." Pump your arms with him.
- **Make it fun:** The ladder is a bridge over a lava river: lines are "cracks." For the 7yo, time clean trips and let him try to beat his record. Let each boy invent one pattern for Dad to try.
- **Why it works:** Quick, light foot contacts in different patterns build **footwork and rhythm**, and the sideways patterns train the **lateral movement** used in every sport: shuffling to a grounder, defending a dribbler, pivoting in basketball. The patterns also make him plan his next step while moving (thinking + moving), and the hopscotch from last week was the stepping stone.

### Skill Work B: Rope Rookie Jumps (6 min) · Cue: **Swing-hop**
- **Setup:** Each boy has a rope (sized to his armpits) and his own space. A flat driveway or patio.
- **Steps:**
  1. **Jump without the rope turning:** lay the rope flat on the ground and do 10 little two-foot hops over it, forward and back, on the balls of the feet (Pogo from Week 4).
  2. **Swing, stop:** hold both handles with the rope behind his heels. Swing it over his head so it lands on the ground in front of his toes. Stop.
  3. **Swing, stop, hop:** after it lands in front, he hops over it. That's one **swing-hop**.
  4. Repeat swing-stop-hop slowly 5 to 10 times. When it's smooth, take away the stop: swing-hop, swing-hop.
  5. *5yo:* steps 1 to 3 only. Stepping over the rope instead of hopping is fine.
  6. *7yo:* steps 1 to 4, then count his best streak of continuous jumps. Try 3, then 5, then 10.
- **Silent demo:** Show the rope behind your heels, swing it over slowly and freeze it on the ground in front of your toes. Point at it, hop over, then grin. Do it 3 times slowly, then once smoothly with no stop. Show your hands low by your hips: small wrist circles, not big arm windmills.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Jumps before the rope arrives, or swings it wildly. **Fix:** Totally normal. Stay on "swing, stop" and just cheer the rope landing in front. A **pool noodle** or a rope cut in half and held in each hand (no rope to jump at all, just the swing motion) works too.
  - *5yo:* Gets frustrated quickly. **Fix:** Very short turns (30 to 60 seconds), and let him go back to hopping over the rope on the ground. Effort praise: "You kept trying after it caught your feet. That's how jumpers learn!"
  - *7yo:* Big arm circles that make the rope hit the ground hard and early. **Fix:** "Elbows stay close, wrists do the work." Have him hold a sock under each arm.
  - *7yo:* Jumps really high and tires out fast. **Fix:** "Little bunny hops just high enough." Pogo bounces from Week 4: small and springy.
- **Make it fun:** Count out loud together, and keep a "streak board" in chalk. For the 7yo, try a jump rope rhyme ("Teddy bear, teddy bear, turn around...").
- **Why it works:** Jumping rope combines **timing, rhythm and coordination** (hands and feet in sync), and kids who learn it early tend to have quicker feet in other sports. Breaking it into jump, then swing, then put them together is **part-whole learning**, the progression most PE teachers use, because trying the whole thing first is usually too hard for beginners.
- 🎥 **Video:** [Teaching a kid to jump rope](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2gt5mjU7FlI) · *Verified title: "How to Teach a Kid to Jump Rope for the First Time | Kindergarten PE Lesson |" (The PE Specialist).* A PE teacher's kindergarten progression (jump first, then the swing, then put them together) with tips on short bursts and encouragement. Great for the 5yo's level; watch it first.

### Game: Snake & Helicopter (6 min) · Cue: **Snake**
- **Setup:** Grass or a soft surface. A long jump rope (or a regular rope) held by you at one end, lying on the ground.
- **Steps:**
  1. **Snake:** you wiggle the rope on the ground side to side like a snake. The boys take turns jumping over it without getting "bitten" (touched).
  2. Make the snake wiggle faster, then add "jumping snake" waves (small up-and-down ripples).
  3. **Helicopter** (7yo, and the 5yo if he's ready): you crouch in the middle and swing the rope slowly in a low circle along the ground. The boys stand in the circle and jump as the rope passes.
  4. Bitten = 1 point to the snake. Everyone tries to keep the snake to 3 points or fewer per round.
  5. *5yo:* slow, small snake wiggles, and he can jump with you holding his hand.
  6. *7yo:* faster waves, and in Helicopter he tries to jump 10 in a row.
- **Silent demo:** Lay the rope on the ground and have the 7yo wiggle it slowly. Jump over it with your knees up high and a scared face, then land and celebrate. Point at the rope and hiss.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Steps on the rope or freezes. **Fix:** Slower snake. Let him be the snake handler for a round (it's great for seeing the timing).
  - *5yo:* Upset when he gets bitten. **Fix:** "Snake bites are just tickles." Make the snake "sleepy" (very slow) for him.
  - *7yo:* Jumps way too early in Helicopter. **Fix:** "Watch the rope, jump when it's one step away." Slow your swing.
  - *7yo:* Gets competitive with his brother's points. **Fix:** It's the boys against the snake. Their bites add up together, so they cheer each other.
- **Make it fun:** Big hissing sounds. The snake has a name (let the 5yo name it). When the boys beat the round, the snake "falls asleep" and they have to tiptoe past it.
- **Why it works:** It's jump-rope timing without the hard part (turning the rope), so both boys get lots of successful jumps. Jumping a moving object trains **reaction and timing**, and the team scoring against the snake keeps a 5 vs. 7 contest out of it.

### Cool-down: Sunset Stretch (4 min) · Cue: **Sunset**
- **Setup:** Standing, then sitting on the grass.
- **Steps:**
  1. Reach both arms high like the sun at noon. Breathe in.
  2. Slowly lower the arms out to the sides, like the sun setting, while breathing out. 4 slow sunsets.
  3. Sit and stretch the legs out, reaching for the toes ("the sun goes down behind the hills"). 3 slow breaths.
  4. Lie back and look at the sky for 3 breaths.
  5. Question: "Which was harder for your feet, the ladder or the rope? What helped?"
- **Silent demo:** Slowly raise your arms high, take a big breath, then lower them in a slow arc with a sleepy, peaceful face.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Flaps the arms quickly. **Fix:** "The sun sets slowly." Do it right beside him at his speed.
  - *7yo:* Wants to show off one more jump rope streak. **Fix:** "Save it for Saturday's treasure trail." That gives him something to look forward to.
- **Make it fun:** With the early sunsets this week, do it outside right as the real sun goes down, and see if they can match it.
- **Why it works:** Slow arm movements with long breaths out bring the heart rate down after a jumpy session. The question invites him to notice what helped, and his answer tells you which skill to put into Saturday's game.

## 6. Session 5: Mixed Backyard Day: Goonies Treasure Trail (29 min)

**Equipment:** A bat, wiffle balls (only), a tee, 4 to 6 cones for gates, a pop-up net if you have one, gloves, a base, a soccer ball and small goal, a foam football and a hula hoop, the chalk ladder, a playground ball or junior basketball, a laundry basket (the "hoop"), and "gold coins" (poker chips, pennies or bottle caps) plus a bucket (the treasure chest).

**Levels this session:**
- **5yo:** Hits off the tee, then short front toss from 8 to 10 feet, at a big middle gate. Pass-and-pivot from 2 to 3 steps away with two-hand passes, a jump stop and a simple turn. Trail stations at short distances.
- **7yo:** Short coach pitch (soft overhand) from 15 to 20 feet at the middle gate, then opposite field. Chest and bounce passes from 4 to 5 steps, a jump stop and a pivot on a "glued" foot. Trail stations at full distance.

> **Safety note for you:** short coach pitch puts you close to the hitter. **Wiffle balls only.** Pitch from one knee (or a bucket) or from behind a pop-up net if you have one, and turn your shoulder away after each pitch. Wear a cap and sunglasses. The waiting brother stays in the "Bat Bubble" (behind a cone 3 big steps back and off to the side). For the passing, do it on grass or a driveway with no cars coming. Fingers stay spread, not pointed at the ball, so a pass doesn't jam a finger.

### Warm-up: Four-Corner Sport Switch (4 min) · Cue: **Switch**
- **Setup:** 4 cones in a square, about 10 steps apart. Each corner is a sport: Baseball, Soccer, Football, Basketball.
- **Steps:**
  1. You call a corner and a move, and they travel there doing it:
     - **Baseball:** shuffle to the corner like going to first base, then stretch at the cone.
     - **Soccer:** run with high knees, then pretend to make a one-two pass.
     - **Football:** sidewinder shuffle, then a pretend rollout throw.
     - **Basketball:** skip there, jump stop with both feet at the cone and pivot.
  2. When you call "**Switch!**", they change direction to a new corner.
  3. 2 rounds through all corners, then a sprint to whatever corner you call last.
  4. *7yo add-on:* he calls the corners for one round.
- **Silent demo:** Do each corner's move for 3 seconds with an exaggerated face and point to the next corner. On "switch," do a dramatic stop-and-turn.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Forgets which corner is which. **Fix:** Put an object at each cone (glove, soccer ball, football, basketball). He runs to the object.
  - *5yo:* Gets silly and stops moving. **Fix:** Call the next corner fast; silly is fine as long as he's moving.
  - *7yo:* Rushes and skips the move at the corner. **Fix:** "No move, no corner." He goes back to the last corner if he skips it.
  - *7yo:* Thinks it's too easy. **Fix:** Let him invent a fifth move for one of the corners.
- **Make it fun:** Call silly switches ("Switch... like a penguin!").
- **Why it works:** A **dynamic warm-up** that reviews this week's movements (stretch, one-two, rollout, jump stop) in a few seconds each, so the body and brain both warm up. Quick direction changes on "switch" add a little **reaction** training.

### Skill Work A: Dance-with-the-Pitcher Gate Hitting (8 min) · Cue: **Dance**
- **Setup:** Home plate. A "middle gate" of two cones 30 to 40 feet out, straight up the middle (Laser Gate from Week 4), and an opposite-field gate for the 7yo. You pitch from 15 to 20 feet (7yo) on a knee or behind a net, or front toss from 8 to 10 feet (5yo).
- **Steps:**
  1. Hitter in his stance.
  2. As your pitching arm goes back, he starts his small load (weight shifts a little onto his back leg). As your arm comes forward, he steps toward you. That's the **dance**: your arm and his body move together, so he's ready when the ball arrives.
  3. He swings at good pitches, aiming through the middle gate.
  4. Through the gate = 2 points. Hit anywhere fair = 1. No comment on misses.
  5. Rounds of 6 pitches, then swap.
  6. *5yo:* starts off the tee for 3 swings ("dance" with an imaginary pitcher: you swing your arm, then he swings), then front toss.
  7. *7yo:* soft overhand from 15 to 20 feet with a real pitching motion. After 2 rounds, pitch some to the outside half and he aims at the opposite-field gate.
- **Silent demo:** Have the 7yo mime a pitching motion while you're the hitter. As his arm goes back, shift a little onto your back leg; as his arm comes forward, step and swing. Do it in slow motion twice, moving together like dance partners. Then point at his arm and at your feet: "we move together."
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Starts the swing after the ball is already past. **Fix:** Go back to the tee, then a slower, higher toss. Say "Dance" as your arm starts back, so he starts early.
  - *5yo:* Steps away from the plate (toward his dugout side). **Fix:** Lay a jump rope on the ground from his front foot toward you: "Step on the rope." (Afraid of the ball? See Section 7C.)
  - *7yo:* Swings at every pitch, including bad ones. **Fix:** "Good pitches only." A swing at a pitch above his shoulders or in the dirt = minus 1. Ask: "Was that one in your zone?"
  - *7yo:* Lunges forward and swings with just his arms. **Fix:** Don't explain weight shift. Move the pitch a little slower and ask: "Where was your weight when you hit your best one, on your back leg or way out front?"
- **Make it fun:** Play music and literally dance in between rounds. Keep a "gate score" per round; each boy chases his own best.
- **Why it works:** Coach pitch is new timing: a moving arm and a ball coming from farther away. Linking the hitter's movement to the pitcher's arm is a **rhythm cue**, and rhythm helps kids start their swing on time instead of reacting late. The gate keeps the focus **external** (where the ball goes), the same as Week 4.

### Skill Work B: Pass-and-Pivot Partners (6 min) · Cue: **Glue**
- **Setup:** Two lines of cones (or chalk lines) 2 to 3 steps apart (5yo) or 4 to 5 steps apart (7yo). One playground ball or junior basketball. You and one boy face each other.
- **Steps:**
  1. **Chest pass:** hold the ball at the chest with fingers spread on the sides, thumbs behind. Step toward the partner and push the ball straight to his chest, finishing with **thumbs pointing down**.
  2. **Bounce pass:** same push, but aim at a spot on the ground about two-thirds of the way to the partner so it bounces up to his waist.
  3. **Catch + jump stop:** the receiver catches with both hands, hops and lands on both feet at the same time ("jump stop").
  4. **Pivot:** he picks one foot to **glue** to the ground (the pivot foot) and turns on the ball of that foot, stepping around with the other foot, like a door swinging on a hinge. Then he passes back.
  5. 8 to 10 passes each type, swapping partners between the brothers and you.
  6. *5yo:* two-hand chest pass from 2 to 3 steps, bounce pass into a hula hoop on the ground, catch, and a simple quarter turn on a glued foot.
  7. *7yo:* chest and bounce passes from 4 to 5 steps, and a half-turn pivot (front pivot) before passing back. Add a "defender" (you) with arms up; he pivots away from you to make a bounce pass to his brother.
- **Silent demo:** Catch a pass, jump stop with a loud double-foot "thump," then put a finger on one shoe and pretend to pour glue on it. Swivel on that foot slowly in both directions, the other foot stepping around. Lift the glued foot once, gasp, and shake your head ("traveling!"). Then a chest pass with thumbs down, held in the finish.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Throws the ball underhand from his belly. **Fix:** Fine for a start. "Push it from your chest like you're pushing a door open." Stand closer.
  - *5yo:* Picks up his glued foot when he turns. **Fix:** Put a paper plate under his pivot foot: "The plate never leaves." Celebrate any turn where it stays.
  - *7yo:* Bounce pass bounces too close to himself and comes up at his partner's knees. **Fix:** Put a chalk "X" two-thirds of the way across. "Hit the X."
  - *7yo:* Pivots on his heel and gets stuck. **Fix:** "Pivot on your tiptoes, like squishing a bug." (That's the Week 1 hitting picture, now for basketball.)
- **Make it fun:** Count passes in a row without a drop. Then play "Monkey in the Middle" gently: you're the monkey, and the boys use pivots and bounce passes to keep the ball away from you.
- **Why it works:** Pivoting and passing is a **basketball-friendly** skill set (catch, stop, protect the ball, pass), but it also teaches body control and balance for every sport. The plate under the pivot foot is a **constraint** that teaches the rule without a lecture, and "thumbs down" gives a clear picture of a good push.
- 🎥 **Video:** [Chest and bounce passes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sKd6aAN8CeY) · *Verified title: "The Two-Handed Pass: Chest and Bounce | Basketball" (SIKANA English).* A slow, clear demo of hand position, stepping into the pass and where to bounce the ball. Adult demonstrators, so it's for you.

### Game: One-Eyed Willy's Treasure Trail (8 min) · Cue: **Gold**
- **Setup:** A trail of 5 stations, numbered with cones, with the treasure chest (bucket) at the end:
  1. **Ladder Lagoon:** a clean trip through the chalk ladder (5yo one foot per box; 7yo in-in-out-out).
  2. **First Base Fort:** you throw from 15 feet; he catches with a foot on the base (Bag and Stretch).
  3. **One-Two Cove:** dribble, pass to you, run past a cone, get it back and score in the small goal.
  4. **Rollout Rock:** roll out and throw to the hoop ahead of the walking brother (or you).
  5. **Pivot Pirate Ship:** catch your pass, jump stop, pivot and bounce-pass into the laundry basket "hoop" lying on its side (5yo: two-hand chest pass into the basket from 2 steps).
  - Each clean station = 1 **gold** coin into the chest.
- **Steps:**
  1. Brothers take turns going through the whole trail (about 45 seconds each).
  2. They're a **team** trying to reach One-Eyed Willy's number (for example, 20 gold coins in 6 trips combined).
  3. "Clean" means done right, not fast.
  4. *5yo:* short distances and big balls. *7yo:* full distances.
- **Silent demo:** Walk the trail once in slow motion, miming each station and dropping a pretend coin in the chest after each. Shake the chest and do a pirate "Arrr!"
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Gets lost between stations. **Fix:** Draw chalk arrows on the ground between stations, like a treasure map. Walk with him the first time.
  - *5yo:* Upset when he misses a coin. **Fix:** Team treasure: his brother's coins count for him too. Give a "bravery coin" for a great try.
  - *7yo:* Rushes and gets sloppy. **Fix:** "Only clean ones earn gold." Speed doesn't score.
  - *7yo:* Criticizes his brother. **Fix:** Make him the "treasure keeper" on his brother's trip: he hands out the coins and has to say one specific nice thing at each station ("You squared up!").
- **Make it fun:** Pirate voices, a hand-drawn treasure map, and a real small prize if they hit the number (they pick the movie or dessert). "Goonies never say die!" when someone misses and tries again.
- **Why it works:** Switching between five different skills is **interleaved practice**. It feels harder than repeating one skill, but it leads to better long-term learning and helps them use the right skill at the right time. Team scoring keeps it cooperative, and the 7yo's "treasure keeper" job practices praising a teammate, a skill your team needs as much as any throw.

### Cool-down: Drop Anchor Breathing (3 min) · Cue: **Anchor**
- **Setup:** Sit cross-legged facing each other.
- **Steps:**
  1. Put both hands on the belly: that's the anchor.
  2. Breathe in slowly through the nose and feel the anchor rise (the ship lifts on a wave). Breathe out slowly through the mouth and feel it drop. 5 slow breaths.
  3. Each boy names his favorite play of the week and the cue word that goes with it.
  4. Finish with one piece of specific effort praise from you to each boy, using the job he did ("You kept your foot on the bag all week and made your brother's throws into outs").
- **Silent demo:** Hands on your belly, breathe in slowly with your eyes closed and your belly rising, then let it sink with a long, slow "ahhh." Make a little wave motion with one hand.
- **Errors & fixes:**
  - *5yo:* Breathes fast and giggles. **Fix:** Laugh with him, then "Can the anchor go down slowly?" Put your hand on his to feel it together.
  - *7yo:* Can't pick a favorite play. **Fix:** Offer two: "The bang-bang out at first, or the one-two goal?"
- **Make it fun:** End with "Goonies never say die!" all together, hands in.
- **Why it works:** Belly breathing slows the heart rate and calms the body. Naming the play and its cue word is **retrieval practice**, which is what makes the week's learning stick.

## 7. Coaching Groups at the Fields: New Tools for This Week

Fall ball wraps up around Nov 14, so this week is about finishing well: fair playing time, smarter defense, the kid who's scared of the ball, a backup plan for rain or early darkness, and ending the season so every kid wants to come back. These build on what you already have (stations, kill the line, ask before tell, let the game teach, cue words in games).

### A. Fair playing time and rotating positions (a simple rotation chart)
In 8U, the fastest way to lose a kid (and a parent) is a season of right field. A written rotation solves most of it before it starts.
- **Write it before game day.** A simple grid: kids down the side, innings across the top, a position in each box. Bring it on an index card and give a copy to your assistant. Deciding in the dugout leads to favorites without meaning to.
- **Rules that keep it fair:**
  - Nobody sits two innings in a row, and nobody sits twice before everyone has sat once.
  - Every kid plays at least one **infield** inning per game (or per two games if you have a big roster).
  - Nobody plays the same position two innings in a row (except maybe a catcher, if your league uses one).
  - Track it across games: over the season, every kid should see pitcher's helper, first base, the left side of the infield and the outfield.
- **First base is the one exception to think about.** It's the busiest spot in coach pitch and a kid who's scared of the ball can get hurt there. Rotate kids into first base only after they've caught throws in practice (Session 1's Stretch & Squeeze is perfect for this). Be open about why: "You'll play first once we've practiced it together."
- **Batting order rotates too.** Move the top of the order down 3 spots each game, so no kid always bats last.
- **Say it out loud early:** "Everyone plays everywhere on this team. That's how you get better at this age." Kids and parents accept rotations when they know the rule and see it applied to everyone, including the coach's kids.
- **Why it works:** Kids at this age haven't settled into positions, and the ones who look weakest now are often the late bloomers. Playing everywhere builds all-round skills (and game sense), and a visible, written plan takes the "favorites" worry off the table.

### B. Teaching game situations with a 2-minute walk-through ("Where's the play?")
Most 8U defensive mistakes aren't catching or throwing; they're not knowing where to throw. Teach it walking, not talking.
- **Freeze-frame walk-through:** put the kids at their positions, a parent or coach as a runner on a base, and say: "Runner on first. Ball hit to you, [position]. Where's the play?" Then walk it in slow motion: the fielder carries the ball (no throwing) to where it should go, and everyone else walks to where *they* go (covering a base, backing up).
- **Keep it to 3 situations per practice:** for 8U, the big three are:
  1. **Nobody on:** throw to first. Always.
  2. **Ball in the outfield:** throw it in to the cutoff/the base in front of the lead runner. Run it in if in doubt ("Get it to an infielder fast").
  3. **Runner on first, ground ball to an infielder:** the closest out. Often that's stepping on second or tagging the runner if he's near; otherwise throw to first.
- **Use a pre-pitch question.** Before each pitch in practice games, call out: "Where's the play?" and have the kids point to the base. It takes 2 seconds and builds the habit of thinking *before* the ball is hit.
- **Make the walk-through a game:** "Situation Freeze": you call a situation and count down from 5; everyone has to be in the right spot when you hit zero.
- **One cue word for the whole team:** "**Lead runner**." (Only add it after the "throw to first" habit is solid.)
- **Why it works:** Walking it slowly removes the time pressure so kids can see the whole picture, the same whole-part-whole idea as at home. Pointing before each pitch is **anticipation practice**, which is what experienced players do automatically.

### C. The kid who's afraid of the ball
Every 8U team has a few. They step away from the plate, turn their head on throws or hide in the outfield. Fear is normal and smart (a baseball *can* hurt). Your job is to make the ball less scary, not to talk them out of it.
- **Never say "Don't be scared" or "It won't hurt."** Both make them think about getting hurt. Say instead: "Let's start with a ball that can't hurt at all."
- **Go back to safe balls.** Foam, tennis or "safety" balls for catching and hitting. Let the kid pick when to move to the next ball. Progress: foam → tennis → soft-core → real ball, closer distance first, then back up.
- **Teach how to get hit safely.** For hitters: practice "turn and tuck" with a foam ball, turning the front shoulder and chin away from an inside pitch toward the catcher. Once they *know* how to protect themselves, they relax. For fielders: "glove in front of your face" catches with a soft ball.
- **Shrink the distance and slow the speed.** Make catches easy enough that they succeed 8 out of 10 times. Confidence comes from success, not courage.
- **Fix the feet, not the fear, at the plate.** A kid stepping "in the bucket" (away from the plate) often just needs a target: a line or a bat on the ground pointing to the pitcher to step on.
- **Pair them with patient partners** in throwing pairs, not with the hardest thrower.
- **Praise brave efforts, not results.** "You kept your eyes on it all the way into the glove!" even when the ball pops out.
- **Talk to the parent privately.** Ask what the kid has said at home. Sometimes it's one bad memory (a ball off the face last spring). Share your plan so they don't push at home.
- **Why it works:** Gradual exposure with lots of success is how fear goes down. Teaching a protective skill gives the kid a sense of **control**, and kids who feel in control stop flinching.
- 🎥 **Video:** [Helping players afraid of the ball](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a2FpwuFORZs) · *Verified title: "Players Afraid of the Baseball? // Coaching Tips on How to Help Players Overcome Their Biggest Fear!" (Building Better Baseball).* A youth coach's tips for gradually building confidence. For you.

### D. Rainy-day and after-dark practice plan (indoor or covered)
With daylight saving time over and a few November storms, you'll lose a practice or two. Have a plan ready so you can say "practice is on, but here's where" instead of cancelling.
- **Where:** a school gym, church hall, covered pavilion, a big garage, or even a parent's driveway with lights. Ask your league; many have a gym slot or a covered batting cage for rain-outs.
- **Equipment swap:** foam or wiffle balls only indoors, tees instead of live pitching, and no full swings near walls or ceilings (use "Bat Bubble" spacing). Tape lines and bases on the floor.
- **A 45-minute indoor plan (4 stations, 8 minutes each, plus warm-up and finish):**
  1. **Warm-up (5 min):** Four-Corner Sport Switch from Session 5 (baseball moves only) or a "situations freeze" walk-through.
  2. **Station 1, Tee hitting into a net or a gym mat:** wiffle balls, aim at a target taped on the wall.
  3. **Station 2, Short-hop grounders and Field-and-Fire:** parent rolls; kids field and throw to a parent at a taped first base with tennis balls.
  4. **Station 3, First base Stretch & Squeeze:** soft tosses to a taped base.
  5. **Station 4, Baserunning with tape:** run through first (Tape from Week 3), make a turn, and practice the Glove-Down Tag with a parent "runner" walking in.
  6. **Finish (5 min):** a quick team game like "Situation Freeze" or a relay, plus the huddle.
- **After-dark option:** if you only have 30 minutes of light, cut to two big stations and the game, and end by sunset. Kids can't track fly balls in dusk; skip fly balls entirely late in the day.
- **Have a short "at home" message ready** for a full cancellation: "Practice is off. 10-minute homework: 25 throws into a laundry basket and 20 tee swings. Tell me tomorrow how many went in!" Kids love homework that's a challenge.
- **Why it works:** Kids at this age need consistency more than perfect conditions. A short indoor practice keeps the routine and the team energy going, and stations keep everyone busy in a small space.

### E. Planning the last practice and the end-of-season celebration
The last practice is the one they'll remember. Make it fun first, skills second, and make sure every kid leaves feeling seen.
- **Last practice = "Fun Day":** let the kids vote on their two favorite drills from the season, then play a kids vs. coaches & parents game (parents bat opposite-handed, run backward to first, etc.). End with a long huddle.
- **Kids coach for 5 minutes:** let a couple of volunteers teach a drill to the team using a cue word they learned ("Coach's word?" from Week 2). They'll remember teaching more than anything else.
- **Awards that every kid earns, for character, not stats.** Give each player a specific award: "Hustle Hero" (ran out every hit), "Best Teammate" (cheered loudest), "Iron Glove" (stopped everything at third), "Most Improved Hitter," "Brave Heart" (kept trying when it was hard). Write one sentence for each kid about a real moment from the season. The specific sentence matters more than the certificate.
- **Avoid MVP or ranking awards** at this age. One "MVP" makes 11 kids feel they weren't.
- **Keep the celebration simple:** a snack at the field after the last game, a team photo, and the award moments. Ask one parent to organize food so you can focus on the kids.
- **Team culture to finish with:** a team cheer the kids made up, everyone signs a ball for the coach or each kid gets a ball signed by the team, and a goodbye handshake line where each kid says one thing they liked.
- **Why it works:** Ending on a strong positive memory is what makes kids want to play again next season (the "peak-end" effect: people judge an experience mostly by its best moment and its ending). Character awards reinforce the behaviors you've praised all season, effort and teamwork, rather than talent.

### F. End-of-season conversations (with kids, parents and helpers)
A few short conversations at the end turn a good season into a lasting one, and set you up for spring.
- **With each kid (1 minute, during the last practice or game):** name one specific thing they got better at ("Remember when you couldn't catch a grounder? Today you made three outs at short."), and one thing to try this winter ("Play catch with your mom or dad 10 times a week"). Kneel to their eye level. That 60 seconds is often what the kid remembers most.
- **Wrap-up message to parents (short email or team-app post):**
  1. Thank them, especially the helpers by job (not by name if posting publicly).
  2. Two or three team highlights ("We made our first double play!").
  3. What the kids learned: rotations, cue words, effort over results.
  4. A simple off-season idea: play catch, hit off a tee, try another sport.
  5. Spring registration info, if you have it.
- **Thank your helpers privately**, maybe with a small card signed by the kids. Ask them if they'd help again.
- **Ask for feedback** with one or two questions ("What's one thing your kid loved? What's one thing I could do better next season?"). Read it later with a cup of coffee, not right after the last game.
- **Handle the "will my kid be on your team next season?" question gently:** "I'd love to coach them again. Team assignments are up to the league, but I'll mention it."
- **Write yourself a "next season" note** while it's fresh: what worked (stations, cue words), what didn't, which parents helped. Future-you will thank you.
- **Why it works:** Specific, personal feedback at the end tells each kid their effort was noticed, which is the strongest motivator for keeping kids in sports. And a clear, positive wrap-up keeps parents on your side for next season.

## 8. Week 6 Preview

Week 6 (Nov 9–15) is the last week of fall ball, so it's a "season finale" week: review, celebrate and show off, plus a peek ahead to winter sports.
- **Baseball: baserunning reads and calling the ball:** running on a ground ball versus holding on a fly ball, rounding first on a hit to the outfield, and calling "Mine!" on pop-ups so two fielders don't collide.
- **Soccer: shooting under pressure and a first goalie try:** shooting quickly after a one-two, plus basic goalkeeping (ready stance, catching a rolled ball, diving onto a soft ball on grass) for both boys.
- **Flag football: fakes and reverses:** a fake handoff and a simple reverse play, plus defenders reading the fake.
- **Athleticism: jump rope streaks and a basketball footwork intro:** rope streak challenges building on this week, and layup footwork ("right, left, up") at a lowered hoop or a laundry basket on a chair.
- **Session 5: "Goonies Showcase Day":** each boy picks his favorite drills from the six weeks and teaches one to Dad (a full teach-back).
- **Group coaching focus:** running the final game day and the awards moment smoothly, keeping kids engaged in a long season-ending game, and an off-season home plan to send parents.
